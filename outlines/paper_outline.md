# Sampling bias of linear estimators under flow-generated sampling

**Working title (placeholder):** Bias of linear estimators under flow-generated
(Lagrangian) sampling: a covariance decomposition and its application to
Gaussian-process ocean-heat-content mapping.

**One-line thesis.** A strongly non-uniform, endogenous, flow-generated sampling
design can be near-orthogonal to a linear functional of the field, so preferential
sampling need not imply preferential bias; we make this precise, decompose the bias
of the estimators actually used for Argo OHC, and compute the decomposition exactly
against a reanalysis treated as truth.

> **Scope box (state early, repeat in conclusions).**
> Results are for a *simulated* Lagrangian array advected in a state-of-the-art
> reanalysis (GLORYS), with the reanalysis field treated as truth. The bias reported
> is therefore *exact* (nothing is estimated) but pertains to the simulated array,
> not the deployed observing system. Two things are deferred to sequels: (i) the
> fidelity gap between simulated and real float trajectories (real floats diverge
> from park-depth reanalysis advection, cf. the trajectory-divergence analysis);
> (ii) bias *bounds* for the deployed array when the field is only partially known.

---

## 1. Introduction

- The estimand: a spatiotemporal average (domain-mean anomaly) of a scalar ocean
  field; concretely, ocean heat content (OHC) over an analysis region and year.
- The observing system: Argo floats sample *Lagrangianly* — trajectories are
  generated by an advecting flow, not chosen by a designer. This is a distinct,
  under-studied source of sampling preferentiality.
- The question: when does the simple sample-mean (or a mapper's) estimate of the
  domain mean become biased, and by how much, purely because of *where the flow
  puts the samples*?
- Contributions:
  1. An exact identity: sampling bias of any linear estimator is an inner product
     between the field and the estimator's discrepancy from the target measure.
  2. A characterization of when a preferential, endogenous, flow-generated design is
     nonetheless (near-)orthogonal to a given linear functional — the paper's thesis.
  3. Placement of the estimators actually used (simple mean, Hájek/inverse-intensity,
     Kuusela–Stein locally stationary GP) in one estimator class.
  4. An exact three-part bias decomposition (geometry / coverage / preferential
     parameter estimation) computed on GLORYS-as-truth.
- Relation to prior work (short, precise; full version in §6):
  - Preferential sampling: Diggle, Menezes, Su (2010); their *functional-vs-stochastic*
    dependence distinction; Anderson's $\beta\sigma^2$ selection shift; Rathbun's
    inverse-intensity estimator; Stein's "some designs don't perturb some estimands."
  - Informative sampling / survey-sampling lineage; Horvitz–Thompson, Hájek.
  - Argo OHC mapping: Roemmich–Gilson; Kuusela & Stein (2018); locally stationary GP.

## 2. The bias of a linear estimator is an inner product

### 2.1 Setup

- Domain $\mathcal{X}=\mathcal{A}\times\mathcal{T}$ (space $\times$ time); probability
  measure $\mathbb{P}$ (normalized area $\times$ time) on the depth-masked domain.
- Target (residual/anomaly) field $\tilde Z:\mathcal{X}\to\mathbb{R}$, mean removed.
- Estimand (a bounded linear functional):
$$
\bar Z \;=\; \langle \tilde Z, \mathbb{P}\rangle \;=\; \int_{\mathcal{X}} \tilde Z \, d\mathbb{P}.
$$

### 2.2 Linear estimators and their measure

- **Definition (linear estimator).** $\hat Z=\sum_i a_i\,\tilde Z(x_i)$ with weights
  $a_i$ that may depend on the design (and on a fitted covariance) but *not* on the
  responses $\tilde Z(x_i)$. Attach the signed **estimator measure**
$$
\nu \;=\; \sum_{i=1}^n a_i\,\delta_{x_i},
\qquad
\hat Z=\langle \tilde Z,\nu\rangle.
$$

- **Proposition 2.1 (bias identity).** For any linear estimator,
$$
B \;=\; \hat Z-\bar Z \;=\; \langle \tilde Z,\ \nu-\mathbb{P}\rangle.
$$
  If additionally $\nu(\mathcal{X})=\sum_i a_i=1$ (the estimator reproduces
  constants), then centering is free:
$$
B \;=\; \langle \tilde Z-\bar Z,\ \nu-\mathbb{P}\rangle,
$$
  because $\langle \bar Z,\ \nu-\mathbb{P}\rangle=0$.

- **Density / covariance form.** With $q=d\,\mathbb{E}[\nu]/d\mathbb{P}$ (where it
  exists),
$$
\mathbb{E}[B] \;=\; \langle \tilde Z-\bar Z,\ q-1\rangle_{\mathbb{P}}
           \;=\; \operatorname{Cov}_{\mathbb{P}}(q,\tilde Z).
$$

### 2.3 Remarks

- The simple mean is the special case $a_i=1/n$, $\nu=w$ (empirical occupancy).
- Noise $Y_i=\tilde Z(x_i)+\varepsilon_i$ affects variance, not bias:
  $\mathrm{Var}$ picks up $\sigma_\varepsilon^2\sum_i a_i^2$; the identity is unchanged.
- Adaptive covariance/hyperparameter estimation makes the estimator only
  *conditionally* linear; identity holds given fitted weights, with a second-order
  correction (revisited in §4 as the preferential-estimation term).

## 3. When is a flow-generated design orthogonal to the functional? *(thesis)*

> This is the conceptual core. Everything downstream demonstrates it.

### 3.1 The bound and the alignment parameter

- **Proposition 3.1 (Cauchy–Schwarz ceiling).**
$$
|B| \;\le\; \lVert \tilde Z-\bar Z\rVert_{\mathbb{P}}\;\lVert q-1\rVert_{\mathbb{P}}
        \;=\; \sigma\,\operatorname{sd}_{\mathbb{P}}(q),
$$
  with $\sigma=\operatorname{sd}_{\mathbb{P}}(\tilde Z)$, attained only when $q-1
  \propto \tilde Z-\bar Z$. Writing the realized bias as
$$
B \;=\; \rho\,\sigma\,\operatorname{sd}_{\mathbb{P}}(q),
\qquad \rho=\operatorname{Corr}_{\mathbb{P}}(q,\tilde Z),
$$
  makes the **alignment** $\rho$ the free quantity: strong non-uniformity
  ($\operatorname{sd}(q)\gg 0$) with $\rho\approx 0$ gives negligible bias.

- **Zero bias does not require uniform sampling** — only $q-1 \perp \tilde Z-\bar Z$
  in $L^2(\mathbb{P})$.

### 3.2 The correct norm for clustered / coherent samples

- The counting norm double-counts along-track correlated profiles. Replace by the
  field-RKHS (covariance) discrepancy:
$$
|B| \;\le\; \lVert \tilde Z-\bar Z\rVert_{\mathcal{H}}\;\lVert \nu-\mathbb{P}\rVert_{\mathcal{H}^*},
$$
  where $\lVert \nu-\mathbb{P}\rVert_{\mathcal{H}^*}$ is a kernel/MMD discrepancy —
  a finite quadratic form over the observed points with the field covariance $C$:
$$
\lVert \nu-\mathbb{P}\rVert_{\mathcal{H}^*}^2
= \sum_{i,i'}(a_i-p_i)\,C(x_i,x_{i'})\,(a_{i'}-p_{i'})
\;-\;(\text{cross \& target terms}).
$$
  *(Note: this is "use the right inner product," not "integrate along curves"; the
  continuous-occupation framing is only a footnote — the estimators sub-sample the
  field before evaluating it.)*

### 3.3 Sufficient conditions for small $\rho$ (why the ocean is near-orthogonal)

- **Endogeneity is necessary but not sufficient.** The velocity field driving the
  occupancy and the anomaly field share a common physical driver (ocean state), so
  $q$ and $\tilde Z$ are dependent — but the bias is a single projection, which can
  vanish while the dependence is strong. Map to Diggle's *functional-vs-stochastic*
  distinction: shared-driver (common cause) is **not** preferential in his sense.
- **(i) Quadrature / geostrophic structure.** Geostrophic $u\sim\nabla^\perp\psi$;
  any occupancy component linear in the skew-gradient is orthogonal on a closed
  domain, $\langle \nabla^\perp\psi,\ f\rangle=0$. Leading-order geostrophic
  contribution to bias is exactly zero; residual is ageostrophic/nonlinear.
- **(ii) Even-vs-odd (magnitude vs signed).** Occupancy responds to dynamical
  *intensity* (dwell $\sim 1/\text{speed}$, EKE), roughly *even* in the signed
  anomaly; $\tilde Z-\bar Z$ is mean-zero/symmetric, so $\operatorname{Cov}(\text{even},
  \text{symmetric})\approx 0$. Warm/cold cancel in the signed projection.
- **Contrast (animals, deferred).** Foraging is a *signed, monotone* response to the
  field → odd → projects directly onto $\tilde Z$ → $\rho$ large. This is the regime
  where preferential sampling *does* imply preferential bias.

### 3.4 Relation to preferential sampling (specialization)

- **Proposition 3.2 (recovery of Diggle/Anderson).** For the log-linear intensity
  $q\propto e^{\beta \tilde Z}$ with $\tilde Z\sim N(0,\sigma^2)$ under $\mathbb{P}$,
$$
B \;=\; \operatorname{Cov}_{\mathbb{P}}(q,\tilde Z) \;=\; \beta\sigma^2,
$$
  exactly Anderson's selection shift. The covariance functional generalizes this to
  an empirically/mechanistically determined $q$ and to a functional estimand,
  without positing $\beta$.

### 3.5 The estimator menu, one axis

- Each estimator = a route to push $\nu\to\mathbb{P}$:
  - **Simple mean:** $\nu=w$ (no correction).
  - **Hájek / inverse-intensity:** $a_i\propto 1/q(x_i)$ (uses known intensity).
  - **Kriging / BLUP:** $a=K^{-1}\bar{\mathbf c}$ (declusters via $K^{-1}$; uses the
    covariance model). Optimal-recovery view: BLUP minimizes worst-case bias over an
    RKHS ball, i.e. minimizes the dual norm of $\nu-\mathbb{P}$.

## 4. Kuusela–Stein is in the class; exact bias decomposition

### 4.1 The KS estimator as a measure

- Pipeline: subtract plug-in mean field $\hat\mu$; per grid node $g$, krige the
  residual from profiles in a moving window $W(g)$; area-integrate the map.
- Linear in residuals given fitted $(\hat\mu,\hat\xi)$; estimator measure
$$
\nu_{KS}=\sum_i A_i\,\delta_{x_i},
\qquad
A_i=\sum_{g:\,x_i\in W(g)} A_g\,\big[\mathbf c_g^\top K_g^{-1}\big]_i,
$$
  with $A_g$ = cell area, $(\mathbf c_g,K_g)$ = fitted local covariance vectors/matrix.
- **Support:** $\nu_{KS}$ lives on observed profiles; it has **no mass** on the
  unmapped (empty-window) region $\mathcal{G}$ (there $\hat r=0$, i.e. fall back to
  $\hat\mu$). Partition $\mathcal{X}=\mathcal{M}\sqcup\mathcal{G}$.

### 4.2 The oracle estimator (Option 1)

- $\nu_{KS}^\star$: KS's *own* local estimators evaluated on the **complete GLORYS
  field** (full spacetime coverage) within each window, with **window footprint,
  covariance family, optimizer, and masking rules held fixed** — only the input data
  differ (sparse-preferential $\to$ complete-uniform).
- Interpretation: $B_{\text{param}}$ below is then the bias from *fitting on a
  sparse, preferentially-located subset vs complete local coverage* — the exact,
  measured Diggle preferential-estimation effect, with model form held fixed.
- **Not** "true GP parameters" (GLORYS is not a draw from the GP). Report the
  Option-2 population-moment gap (KS-on-everything vs the field's actual local
  moments) separately as a **model-misspecification diagnostic**, not part of the
  sampling-bias decomposition.

### 4.3 The decomposition (exact, telescoping)

$$
\hat B_{KS}
=\underbrace{\langle \tilde Z-\bar Z,\ \nu_{KS}^\star-\mathbb{P}\rangle_{\mathcal{M}}}_{B_{\text{geom}}}
+\underbrace{\langle \tilde Z-\bar Z,\ -\mathbb{P}\rangle_{\mathcal{G}}}_{B_{\text{cov}}}
+\underbrace{\langle \tilde Z-\bar Z,\ \nu_{KS}-\nu_{KS}^\star\rangle}_{B_{\text{param}}}.
$$

- **Closure (state and check).** Using $\nu_{KS}^\star(\mathcal{G})=0$ and identical
  support for fitted and oracle runs,
$$
B_{\text{geom}}+B_{\text{cov}}
=\langle \tilde Z-\bar Z,\ \nu_{KS}^\star-\mathbb{P}\rangle,
\quad\Rightarrow\quad
B_{\text{geom}}+B_{\text{cov}}+B_{\text{param}}=\hat B_{KS}.
$$
  Support must be fixed by realization geometry (windows don't depend on $\xi$);
  otherwise a spurious fourth term appears.

- **Term readings.**
  - $B_{\text{geom}}$ — design geometry with oracle parameters; §3 predicts it small
    (near-orthogonal, KS decorrelation).
  - $B_{\text{cov}}$ — coverage; $=-\int_{\mathcal{G}}(\tilde Z-\bar Z)\,d\mathbb{P}$;
    flow-generated gaps; expected to dominate.
  - $B_{\text{param}}$ — preferential parameter estimation; the exact Diggle effect.

### 4.4 Sub-split of $B_{\text{param}}$ (mean vs covariance)

- Nested substitution (fixed, reported order), since $\hat\xi$ is fit on residuals
  from $\hat\mu$:
$$
\nu(\hat\mu,\hat\xi)\ \xrightarrow{\ \mu\text{-swap}\ }\ \nu(\mu^\star,\hat\xi)
\ \xrightarrow{\ \xi\text{-swap}\ }\ \nu(\mu^\star,\xi^\star),
$$
$$
B_{\text{param}}^{\mu}=\langle \tilde Z-\bar Z,\ \nu(\hat\mu,\hat\xi)-\nu(\mu^\star,\hat\xi)\rangle,
\qquad
B_{\text{param}}^{\xi}=\langle \tilde Z-\bar Z,\ \nu(\mu^\star,\hat\xi)-\nu(\mu^\star,\xi^\star)\rangle.
$$
- $B_{\text{param}}^{\xi}$ is the pure covariance-fitting term (the Diggle-faithful
  quantity); $B_{\text{param}}^{\mu}$ is a mean-estimation term (closer to the
  $\beta\sigma^2$ shift). Report the reverse-order interaction gap for honesty.

### 4.5 Conditional-linearity caveat

- KS estimates $(\hat\mu,\hat\xi)$ from data → conditionally linear; identity exact
  given fits; the second-order error *is* $B_{\text{param}}$, now a measured term
  rather than a caveat. This is where Diggle's second-moment problem sits inside the
  first-moment functional bias.

## 5. Demonstration on GLORYS (truth)

### 5.1 Data and simulated array

- GLORYS12V1, $1/12^\circ$, daily $(u,v)$; OHC 0–2000 m; analysis boxes (energetic vs
  quiescent) for the flow-variability contrast.
- Mean-field decomposition $\mathrm{OHC}=\hat m+\tilde Z$ (per-cell trend + harmonics).
- Advection: OceanParcels, park depth 1000 m, 10-day profiles, lifespans from the
  real Argo record; ensemble of realizations (re-seeding gives genuine
  Monte-Carlo replicates).

### 5.2 The estimator ladder (mean $\to$ Hájek $\to$ KS)

- Present the characterization first on the **simple mean** (exact, no fitting):
  strong non-uniformity, $\rho\approx 0$, $B$ far below the ceiling. Cleanest
  orthogonality evidence.
- **Hájek**: what inverse-intensity reweighting buys (bias removed vs variance added).
- **KS**: the realistic mapper; the three-term decomposition; new terms
  (coverage, preferential estimation) appear *because* it is a real method.

### 5.3 Numbers to compute (all exact on GLORYS)

- Per box-year, per estimator: realized $B$, the ceiling
  $\sigma\cdot\operatorname{sd}(q)$ (and the RKHS/MMD version), and $\rho$.
- For KS: $\{B_{\text{geom}},\ B_{\text{cov}},\ B_{\text{param}}^{\mu},\
  B_{\text{param}}^{\xi}\}$ with Monte-Carlo error bars; closure check
  (four terms sum to $\hat B_{KS}$).
- Diagnostics: (a) $B_{\text{param}}^{\xi}\to 0$ as window data density rises;
  (b) $B_{\text{geom}}$ near-zero and mass-normalization $\nu^\star(\mathcal{M})=1$;
  (c) $\mathcal{M}/\mathcal{G}$ per-realization vs ensemble (report the Jensen gap).
- The spectral view (optional figure): $B=\sum_m a_m b_m$ over EOFs, showing bias
  lives only where field and occupancy-excess overlap.

### 5.4 Headline (results-contingent — decide after the numbers)

- If $B_{\text{geom}}\approx 0$, $B_{\text{cov}}$ dominates, $B_{\text{param}}\neq 0$:
  *"modern Argo OHC bias is coverage + parameter-estimation, not sampling geometry."*
- If KS's $B_{\text{geom}}$ is resolved though the mean's was ~0:
  *"the mapper reintroduces geometric sensitivity the simple mean did not have."*

  component that UQ omits (complementary, not competing).

  pushforward / invariant-measure object (mechanism for §3, cited not claimed).

## 7. Discussion / limitations / sequels

- **Exact but simulated.** The decomposition is exact for the simulated array in a
  reanalysis; deployed-array fidelity is a separate question (trajectory divergence).
- **Bias vs UQ.** Bound/decompose the bias KS's error bars ignore; identifiability
  asymmetry — *magnitude* of bias is identifiable from design + roughness; *signed*
  bias needs the off-sample field.
- **Sequels.** (i) Partial-field regime: bounds for the deployed array with the field
  as a GP posterior / reanalysis ensemble; observed trajectories give $q$ exactly,
  field supplies the uncertainty. (ii) Other flow-based systems: surface drifters,
  stratospheric superpressure balloons, animal-borne sensors — the last as the
  large-$\rho$ contrast where preferential sampling *does* bias.
