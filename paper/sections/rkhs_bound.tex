\section{A kernel-discrepancy bound}
\label{sec:rkhs}

The scale-decomposed bound of Proposition~\ref{prop:scale-decomp} pairs the
estimable between-cell factors with a residual
$\sigma_{w^{\perp}}\sigma_{Y^{\perp}}$ that resists estimation from two
directions at once. The weight factor $\sigma_{w^{\perp}}$ is the sub-cell
sampling variability, which cannot be recovered from a sparse point pattern
without a within-cell intensity model (\S\ref{sec:sigmawK}); the field factor
$\sigma_{Y^{\perp}}$ is the sub-cell OHC variability, which is large wherever a
front crosses a cell and cannot be driven to zero by refinement without
emptying the cells of profiles. In the case-study region the front moreover
\emph{migrates} over the analysis window, so no \emph{fixed} partition---equal
measure or field-adaptive---keeps $\sigma_{Y^{\perp}}$ uniformly small while
leaving the cells coarse enough to estimate $\sigma_{w^{\star}}$. The residual
is thus an unestimable factor times an irreducible one.

The obstruction is a property of the geometry, not of the bias. Both factors
arise from bounding the covariance identity
$B_{\mathrm{samp}}(y)=\Cov_\nu(w,y)$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:cov-identity}) by
Cauchy--Schwarz in $L^2(\nu)$, and from the partition used to make that bound
computable. This section bounds the same covariance by Cauchy--Schwarz in a
\emph{reproducing-kernel Hilbert space}. No partition is introduced, so no
$\sigma_{w^{\perp}}$ appears; the sampling factor becomes a single kernel
discrepancy of the observed profile locations against the reference measure,
estimated directly and stably from those locations at every scale at once. The
price is a smoothness assumption on the field, carried by the choice of kernel,
in place of the distribution-free finite-variance assumption of
Proposition~\ref{prop:l2-bound}.

\subsection{A smoothness class for the field}
\label{sec:rkhs-class}

\begin{assumption}[Field smoothness]
\label{ass:rkhs}
Let $k:\X\times\X\to\R$ be a symmetric, positive-definite kernel that is
$\F$-measurable and bounded, $\sup_{x\in\X}k(x,x)=:\kappa_k<\infty$, and let
$\Hk$ be its reproducing-kernel Hilbert space, with inner product
$\inner{\cdot}{\cdot}$ and norm $\normH{\cdot}$
\citep{berlinet2004}. The realized field lies in $\Hk$ with bounded norm,
\[
  y\in\Hk,\qquad \normH{y}\le R ,
\]
for a radius $R>0$.
\end{assumption}

Boundedness gives the continuous embedding $\Hk\hookrightarrow L^2(\nu)$
(indeed $\|f\|_{L^2(\nu)}\le\sqrt{\kappa_k}\,\normH{f}$, as $\nu$ is a
probability measure), so a field in $\Hk$ is in particular an admissible field
in the sense of Definition~\ref{def:Y}, and the estimand $\mu$ and the design
mean $\mu_w$ are unchanged. Assumption~\ref{ass:rkhs} restricts the field to be
smooth at the scale encoded by $k$; unlike the finite-variance hypothesis of
Proposition~\ref{prop:l2-bound} this is a genuine modelling assumption, and its
conservative, product-free calibration is the subject of
\S\ref{sec:rkhs-radius}.

\subsection{The sampling-bias representer}
\label{sec:rkhs-representer}

\begin{definition}[Kernel mean embeddings]
\label{def:embedding}
For a probability measure $\rho$ on $(\X,\F)$ the \emph{kernel mean embedding}
is the Bochner integral
\[
  \xi_\rho \;:=\; \int_\X k(\cdot,x)\dd\rho(x)\ \in\ \Hk ,
\]
well defined under Assumption~\ref{ass:rkhs} because
$\int_\X\normH{k(\cdot,x)}\dd\rho(x)=\int_\X\sqrt{k(x,x)}\dd\rho\le\sqrt{\kappa_k}<\infty$
\citep{smola2007,muandet2017}. Write $\xipi$ and $\xinu$ for the embeddings of
the sampling and reference measures, and
\[
  \psi \;:=\; \xipi-\xinu
\]
for the \emph{sampling-bias representer}.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[RKHS representation of the sampling bias]
\label{prop:representer}
Under Assumption~\ref{ass:rkhs} and conditional on $\{Y=y\}$ and the design,
\[
  B_{\mathrm{samp}}(y)\;=\;\inner{y}{\psi},
  \qquad
  \normH{\psi}\;=\;\MMD_k(\pi,\nu),
\]
where $\MMD_k(\pi,\nu):=\normH{\xipi-\xinu}$ is the maximum mean discrepancy
between $\pi$ and $\nu$.
\end{proposition}
\deferproof{representer}{%
\begin{proof}[Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:representer}]
By Definitions~\ref{def:mu} and~\ref{def:mu-w},
$B_{\mathrm{samp}}(y)=\mu_w-\mu=\int_\X y\dd\pi-\int_\X y\dd\nu$. The
reproducing property $y(x)=\inner{y}{k(\cdot,x)}$ holds for every $x$, and the
map $x\mapsto\inner{y}{k(\cdot,x)}$ is $\rho$-integrable for $\rho\in\{\pi,\nu\}$
since $|\inner{y}{k(\cdot,x)}|\le\normH{y}\sqrt{k(x,x)}\le R\sqrt{\kappa_k}$.
Interchanging the inner product with the Bochner integral
(justified by the same bound),
\[
  \int_\X y\dd\rho=\int_\X\inner{y}{k(\cdot,x)}\dd\rho(x)
  =\Big\langle y,\ \int_\X k(\cdot,x)\dd\rho(x)\Big\rangle_{\Hk}
  =\inner{y}{\xi_\rho}.
\]
Subtracting the two cases gives
$B_{\mathrm{samp}}(y)=\inner{y}{\xipi-\xinu}=\inner{y}{\psi}$. The identity
$\normH{\psi}=\MMD_k(\pi,\nu)$ is Definition~\ref{def:embedding}.
\end{proof}%
}

\subsection{The worst-case design-bias bound}
\label{sec:rkhs-bound}

\begin{theorem}[Kernel-discrepancy bound]
\label{thm:rkhs}
Under Assumption~\ref{ass:rkhs} and conditional on the design, the
preferential-sampling bias satisfies
\[
  \big|B_{\mathrm{samp}}(y)\big|\;\le\; R\,\normH{\psi}\;=\;R\,\MMD_k(\pi,\nu),
\]
and the bound is sharp over the smoothness ball:
\[
  \sup_{\normH{y}\le R}\big|B_{\mathrm{samp}}(y)\big|\;=\;R\,\normH{\psi},
\]
attained, when $\psi\ne0$, at the worst-case field
$y^{\star}=R\,\psi/\normH{\psi}$.
\end{theorem}
\deferproof{rkhs}{%
\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:rkhs}]
By Proposition~\ref{prop:representer} and Cauchy--Schwarz in $\Hk$,
$|B_{\mathrm{samp}}(y)|=|\inner{y}{\psi}|\le\normH{y}\normH{\psi}\le R\normH{\psi}$.
Equality in Cauchy--Schwarz holds iff $y\parallel\psi$; the constraint
$\normH{y}\le R$ is then binding at $y^{\star}=R\psi/\normH{\psi}$, for which
$\inner{y^{\star}}{\psi}=R\normH{\psi}$, giving the supremum.
\end{proof}%
}

\begin{remark}[Non-preferential sampling]
\label{rem:rkhs-zero}
The bound vanishes exactly when $\xipi=\xinu$, i.e. $\normH{\psi}=0$. If $k$ is
characteristic \citep{muandet2017} this occurs iff $\pi=\nu$, recovering the
no-bias case of Remark~\ref{rem:zero-bias}: the kernel discrepancy is zero
precisely for non-preferential sampling.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Relation to the distribution-free bound]
\label{rem:rkhs-vs-l2}
Theorem~\ref{thm:rkhs} and Proposition~\ref{prop:l2-bound} are Cauchy--Schwarz
applied to the \emph{same} covariance $B_{\mathrm{samp}}=\Cov_\nu(w,y)$ in two
inner-product spaces. In $L^2(\nu)$ the worst-case field is $y-\mu\propto w-1$
and the cap is $\sigma_w\sigma_Y$, distribution-free but ranging over all of
$L^2(\nu)$; in $\Hk$ the worst-case field is $y^{\star}\propto\psi$ and the cap
is $R\normH{\psi}$, ranging only over the smoothness ball. When
Assumption~\ref{ass:rkhs} holds both are valid, so
\[
  \big|B_{\mathrm{samp}}\big|\ \le\ \min\big\{\sigma_w\sigma_Y,\ R\normH{\psi}\big\},
\]
and the gap between them is what the smoothness assumption buys. Decisively, the
right-hand cap carries \emph{no partition}: the residual
$\sigma_{w^{\perp}}\sigma_{Y^{\perp}}$ of Proposition~\ref{prop:scale-decomp}
has no analogue here, so the obstruction of \S\ref{sec:sigmawK} does not arise.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Estimating the discrepancy from profile locations}
\label{sec:rkhs-estimation}

The cap of Theorem~\ref{thm:rkhs} factors into a field radius $R$
(\S\ref{sec:rkhs-radius}) and a design factor $\normH{\psi}$ that depends only
on the sampling measure and the kernel. Unlike $\sigma_{w^{\perp}}$, this factor
is estimable directly from the observed profile locations, with no intensity
model and no bandwidth.

\begin{proposition}[Closed form of the discrepancy]
\label{prop:mmd-closed}
Under Assumption~\ref{ass:rkhs},
\begin{align}
  \normH{\psi}^2
  &=\E_{\pi\otimes\pi}\big[k(Z,Z')\big]
    -2\,\E_{\pi\otimes\nu}\big[k(Z,x)\big]
    +\E_{\nu\otimes\nu}\big[k(x,x')\big]
  \label{eq:mmd-uv}\\
  &=\iint_{\X\times\X} k(x,x')\,\big(w(x)-1\big)\big(w(x')-1\big)\dd\nu(x)\dd\nu(x'),
  \label{eq:mmd-w}
\end{align}
where in \eqref{eq:mmd-uv} $Z,Z'\sim\pi$ and $x,x'\sim\nu$ are independent.
\end{proposition}
\deferproof{mmd-closed}{%
\begin{proof}[Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:mmd-closed}]
Expanding the norm and using
$\inner{\xi_\rho}{\xi_{\rho'}}=\iint k(x,x')\dd\rho(x)\dd\rho'(x')$ (two
applications of the reproducing property, as in
Proposition~\ref{prop:representer}),
\[
  \normH{\psi}^2=\inner{\xipi-\xinu}{\xipi-\xinu}
  =\inner{\xipi}{\xipi}-2\inner{\xipi}{\xinu}+\inner{\xinu}{\xinu},
\]
which is \eqref{eq:mmd-uv}. For \eqref{eq:mmd-w}, the change of measure
$\dd\pi=w\dd\nu$ (Definition~\ref{def:w}) turns each expectation into an
integral against $\nu$: $\E_{\pi\otimes\pi}[k]=\iint k\,w(x)w(x')\dd\nu\dd\nu$,
$\E_{\pi\otimes\nu}[k]=\iint k\,w(x)\dd\nu\dd\nu$, and
$\E_{\nu\otimes\nu}[k]=\iint k\dd\nu\dd\nu$; combining and factoring gives the
integrand $k(x,x')(w(x)-1)(w(x')-1)$.
\end{proof}%
}

The reference terms are known functions of $\nu$ and $k$ alone. Writing
\[
  \bar k(z):=\int_\X k(z,x)\dd\nu(x)=\inner{k(\cdot,z)}{\xinu},
  \qquad
  \bar{\bar k}:=\iint_{\X\times\X} k\dd\nu\dd\nu=\normH{\xinu}^2 ,
\]
both are available in closed form, or by Monte Carlo over $\nu$, without any
field product: in the equal-measure coordinates
$(\lambda,u,t)=(\text{lon},\sin\phi,\text{time})$, in which $\nu$ is uniform
because the surface measure is uniform in $(\lambda,u)$ (Remark~\ref{rem:buildK}),
a stationary or separable $k$ makes $\bar k$ a
product of one-dimensional integrals, and $\bar{\bar k}$ a constant. Only the
first term of \eqref{eq:mmd-uv} involves the unknown $\pi$, and it is estimated
from the profiles.

\begin{definition}[Between-float discrepancy estimator]
\label{def:mmd-hat}
With the profiles indexed by float as in Lemma~\ref{lem:bf-unbiased}, let
$\mathcal B$ be the ordered pairs of profiles from \emph{distinct} floats and
$P_{\mathrm{bf}}=|\mathcal B|=N(N-1)-\sum_\ell d_\ell(d_\ell-1)$. The
\emph{between-float} estimator of \eqref{eq:mmd-uv} is
\begin{equation}
  \normpsihatsq
  \;=\;\frac{1}{P_{\mathrm{bf}}}\sum_{(i,i')\in\mathcal B}k(Z_i,Z_{i'})
      \;-\;\frac{2}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\bar k(Z_i)
      \;+\;\bar{\bar k}.
  \label{eq:mmd-hat}
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Unbiasedness]
\label{lem:mmd-unbiased}
If profiles from distinct floats are independent draws with marginal $\pi$
(hypotheses \textup{(A1)}, \textup{(A2)} of Lemma~\ref{lem:bf-unbiased}), then
$\E\big[\normpsihatsq\big]=\normH{\psi}^2$, with no assumption on the joint law
of a single float's profiles.
\end{lemma}
\deferproof{mmd-unbiased}{%
\begin{proof}[Proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:mmd-unbiased}]
Each ordered pair in $\mathcal B$ consists of two profiles from distinct floats,
independent with marginal $\pi$ by (A2), (A1), so
$\E[k(Z_i,Z_{i'})]=\E_{\pi\otimes\pi}[k]$ for every $(i,i')\in\mathcal B$;
averaging over the $P_{\mathrm{bf}}$ pairs gives an unbiased estimate of the
first term of \eqref{eq:mmd-uv}, exactly as in Lemma~\ref{lem:bf-unbiased} with
$k$ in place of the same-cell indicator. Each $Z_i$ has marginal $\pi$, so
$\E[\bar k(Z_i)]=\int_\X\bar k\dd\pi=\iint k\dd\pi\dd\nu=\E_{\pi\otimes\nu}[k]$,
and the second term is unbiased for it. The third term is constant. Summing the
three matches \eqref{eq:mmd-uv}.
\end{proof}%
}

\begin{remark}[The count estimator is the same statistic]
\label{rem:mmd-reduction}
Estimator~\eqref{eq:mmd-hat} is the smooth generalization of the count estimator
\eqref{eq:sigmawK-hat}: take the piecewise-constant kernel
$k_K(x,x')=K\,\1\{x,x'\text{ lie in a common cell of }\mathcal P_K\}$. Then
$\bar k_K(z)=K\,\nu(C_{j(z)})=1$ and $\bar{\bar k}_K=K\sum_j\nu(C_j)^2=1$, so
\eqref{eq:mmd-uv} gives $\normH{\psi}^2=K\sum_j\pi_j^2-2+1=\sigma_{w^{\star}}^2$
(Lemma~\ref{lem:sigmawcell}), and the all-pairs form of \eqref{eq:mmd-hat}
reduces to the count statistic $\sigwhatsq$ of \eqref{eq:sigmawK-hat}. The count estimator is
thus the special case in which the kernel resolves the design only at the cell
scale; a smooth $k$ resolves it at all scales simultaneously, which is what
removes the between/within partition split---and with it the unestimable
$\sigma_{w^{\perp}}$.
\end{remark}

As with $\sigwhat$, within-float profiles are positively associated (a single
float's successive cycles are near one another), so the diagonal removed in
\eqref{eq:mmd-hat} is the trajectory-autocorrelation term of
Remark~\ref{rem:trajectory}; the between-float construction leaves the estimator
unbiased under trajectory dependence. Uncertainty is propagated by the same
delete-one-float jackknife used for $\sigwhat$, and the reported bound uses the
upper limit of the resulting interval for $\normpsihat$.

\subsection{Any profile-based estimator}
\label{sec:rkhs-estimators}

Scientists rarely use the naive profile mean; they interpolate the profiles---by
kriging or objective analysis---and integrate the interpolant. The bound extends
to any such estimator without modification, because every linear functional of a
field in $\Hk$ has a representer.

\begin{proposition}[Worst-case error of a linear estimator]
\label{prop:rkhs-estimator}
Let $\hat\mu_\omega=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\omega_i\,y(Z_i)$ be any profile-based linear
estimator with $\sum_i\omega_i=1$. Under Assumption~\ref{ass:rkhs}, conditional
on the design and the weights,
\[
  \hat\mu_\omega-\mu=\inner{y}{\eta_\omega},
  \qquad
  \eta_\omega:=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\omega_i\,k(\cdot,Z_i)-\xinu,
\]
and $\sup_{\normH{y}\le R}|\hat\mu_\omega-\mu|=R\,\normH{\eta_\omega}$. The
weights minimizing $\normH{\eta_\omega}$ are the kernel (Bayes--Hermite)
quadrature weights---equivalently the kriging predictor of $\mu$---and the
minimum $\normH{\eta_\omega}$ is the associated worst-case quadrature error, or
power function \citep{ohagan1991,micchelli1977,donoho1994}.
\end{proposition}
\deferproof{rkhs-estimator}{%
\begin{proof}[Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:rkhs-estimator}]
By the reproducing property $y(Z_i)=\inner{y}{k(\cdot,Z_i)}$ and
$\mu=\inner{y}{\xinu}$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:representer}), so
$\hat\mu_\omega-\mu=\inner{y}{\sum_i\omega_i k(\cdot,Z_i)-\xinu}=\inner{y}{\eta_\omega}$;
$\sum_i\omega_i=1$ ensures exactness on constants. Cauchy--Schwarz over the ball
gives the supremum $R\normH{\eta_\omega}$ as in Theorem~\ref{thm:rkhs}.
Minimizing the quadratic $\normH{\eta_\omega}^2$ in $\omega$ subject to
$\sum_i\omega_i=1$ is the standard kernel-quadrature/kriging problem, whose
solution is the stated weights.
\end{proof}%
}

Equal weights $\omega_i=1/N$ make $\eta_\omega$ the empirical sampling-bias
representer $N^{-1}\sum_i k(\cdot,Z_i)-\xinu$, whose squared norm is the
plug-in of \eqref{eq:mmd-uv}; the design bias of the naive mean is then bounded
by $R\normH{\psi}$ with $\normH{\psi}$ estimated by \eqref{eq:mmd-hat}
(Lemma~\ref{lem:mmd-unbiased}). For a reweighting estimator the same $R$
multiplies the smaller factor $\normH{\eta_\omega}\le\normH{\eta_{1/N}}$: the
kriging weights minimize the discrepancy, so the estimator practitioners
actually use makes the worst-case bias no larger than the naive one, and the
guarantee covers it under the same field assumption.

\subsection{The field radius}
\label{sec:rkhs-radius}

Only $R=\normH{y}$ is not determined by the profile locations. It is the field's
complexity relative to the kernel, and it cannot be identified from finitely many
observations: the minimum-norm interpolant $\hat y$ through the observed OHC has
$\normH{\hat y}=(\mathbf y^\top K^{-1}\mathbf y)^{1/2}\le\normH{y}$, where
$K=[k(Z_i,Z_{i'})]$ and $\mathbf y=(y(Z_i))_i$, and being a minimum-norm
interpolant this is a \emph{lower} bound---the unsafe direction for a cap. We
therefore treat $R$ as a declared budget and report the guarantee as a curve,
\[
  \big|B_{\mathrm{samp}}\big|\;\le\;R\,\normpsihat,
\]
together with the \emph{breakdown budget}
$R^{\star}(\mathcal T):=\mathcal T/\normpsihat$, the largest field complexity for
which the bias stays below a benchmark $\mathcal T$ (Definition~\ref{def:breakdown}).
This is the field-side analogue of the residual-budget reporting of
\S\ref{sec:breakdown}: an interpretable, criticizable statement about how rough
the OHC field could be, rather than an imported amplitude.

Two calibration choices are made conservatively and without a field product.
The kernel encodes the smoothness class; its length scale is read from the OHC
variogram of the \emph{observed} profiles, taken shorter (rougher) than the
empirical decorrelation length, because a rougher $\Hk$ both weakens the
validity requirement $y\in\Hk$ and enlarges $\normH{\psi}$---the conservative
direction for the design factor. A reanalysis or a fitted Gaussian process may
anchor $R$ in the sensitivity analysis, but as in Remark~\ref{rem:conservative-Y}
assimilation attenuates amplitude, so such a proxy is a lower anchor and a margin
is added; it never enters the guarantee.

\begin{remark}[What enters the guarantee]
\label{rem:rkhs-ledger}
The bound $R\normpsihat$ uses only the profile locations, through
$\normpsihat$ of \eqref{eq:mmd-hat}, and the declared field budget $R$. No
external field amplitude enters; a reanalysis is consulted for the kernel scale
and as a sensitivity anchor for $R$ only. The standing assumptions are
$y\in\Hk$ with $\normH{y}\le R$ (Assumption~\ref{ass:rkhs}) and the
between-float sampling model of Assumption~\ref{ass:sample-pi} for the
unbiasedness of $\normpsihat$. Where Proposition~\ref{prop:scale-decomp} needed
a coarse partition to estimate $\sigma_{w^{\star}}$ and a fine one to control
$\sigma_{Y^{\perp}}$, this bound needs neither: the discrepancy is a single
functional estimated at all scales at once, so the resolution dilemma---and the
migrating front that made it acute---does not arise.
\end{remark}
