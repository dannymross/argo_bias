\section{Background}
\label{sec:background}

Argo floats park and drift at 1000m depth between profiling cycles.
Their locations are determined by currents at that depth, not by experimental design.
If 1000m currents are correlated with upper-ocean temperature or salinity, then Argo-based estimates of quantities like space-time averaged ocean heat content (OHC) could be biased.
This is widely suspected to be negligible, but the magnitude of this bias has not yet been bounded statistically.

This section sets up the estimand, the sampling mechanism, and the resulting sampling-induced bias.

\subsection{Notation and Estimand}
\label{sec:def}

\begin{definition}[Space--time domain]
\label{def:domain}
Fix a spatial region of interest $A \subset S^{2}$, taken to be a Borel subset of the sphere with finite, positive surface area, and a study period $\T \subset \R$, a bounded interval.
The \emph{space--time domain} is the product set
$\X := A \times \T$, equipped with the product $\sigma$-algebra
$\F := \B(A)\otimes\B(\T)$, where $\B(A)$ and $\B(\T)$ are the Borel
$\sigma$-algebras on $A$ and $\T$. A generic element of $\X$ is written
$x = (s,t)$, with spatial location $s \in A$ and time $t \in \T$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Reference space--time measure]
\label{def:nu}
Let $\alpha$ be the normalized area measure on $A$, $\alpha(E)=|E|/|A|$,
and let $\tau$ be a probability measure on $\T$ specifying how the study
period is weighted. The \emph{reference} or \emph{target} probability
measure on $(\X,\F)$ is the product
\[
  \nu \;:=\; \alpha\otimes\tau .
\]
Unless stated otherwise $\tau$ is the seasonally balanced measure of
Definition~\ref{def:tau-balanced}. When the window spans a whole number
of seasonal periods this is the uniform measure $\dd\tau=\dd t/|\T|$, and
$\nu$ reduces to normalized space--time volume, $\nu(E)=|E|/|\X|$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Sampling probability measure]
\label{def:pi}
Let $\pi$ be the \emph{sampling} probability measure on $(\X,\F)$.
\end{definition}


\begin{definition}[Target-population random profile]
\label{def:X}
Let $X = (S,T)$ be the \emph{coordinate map} on $(\X,\F,\nu)$, i.e., the identity function $X : \X \to \X$, $X(x) = x$.
Its distribution is the reference measure by construction, $X \sim \nu$.
Thus, $\Prob(X \in E) = \nu(E)$ for every $E \in \F$. 
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Sampled-population random profile]
\label{def:Z}
Let $Z = (S,T)$ be a random variable on $(\X,\F,\pi)$, with distribution the sampling measure, $Z \sim \pi$.
Thus, $\Prob(Z \in E) = \pi(E)$ for every $E \in \F$.
\end{definition}

$\nu(E)$ is the probability that a profile drawn \emph{uniformly} from the space-time domain lies in $E$.
Whereas, $\pi(E)$ is the probability that a profile drawn at random from the \emph{sampling} or realized advection process lies in $E$.

\begin{definition}[Observed profiles]
\label{def:Zi}
The $N$ observed profile locations are realizations of $N$ random elements $Z_1,\dots,Z_N$, each marginally distributed as $Z_i \sim \pi$ (Definition~\ref{def:Z}).
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Sampling weight]
\label{def:w}
Assume $\pi \ll \nu$. The \emph{sampling weight} is the Radon--Nikodym
derivative $w := \dd\pi/\dd\nu$: the $\nu$-almost-everywhere unique,
non-negative, $\F$-measurable function on $\X$ with
$\pi(E)=\int_E \dd\pi = \int_E w\dd\nu$ for all $E\in\F$. We assume further that
$w \in L^2(\nu)$.
\end{definition}

Composing with the target draw $X\sim\nu$ (Definition~\ref{def:X}) gives
the non-negative random variable $w(X)$; note that $w:\X\to[0,\infty)$ is
a deterministic function on the domain, while $w(X)$ is its composition
with the random location. Lebesgue differentiation gives
\[
  w(x) \;=\; \lim_{E \downarrow x} \frac{\pi(E)}{\nu(E)}
        \;=\; \lim_{E \downarrow x}
              \frac{\text{sampling mass of } E}{\text{reference mass of } E},
\]
so $w$ is the local ratio of sampling density to reference density. Where
$w \equiv 1$ the region is sampled exactly in proportion to its
space--time extent, i.e., as if uniformly; $w > 1$ marks over-sampling
(preferential), $w < 1$ under-sampling; and if $w = 0$ $\nu$-a.e. on a set
$E$ with $\nu(E)>0$, then $\pi(E)=0$ and $E$ is never sampled.

Intuitively, $w(X)$ reweights a target draw $X\sim\nu$ into a sampled draw $Z\sim\pi$.
Non-preferential sampling is $w\equiv 1$ ($\pi=\nu$).
\[
  \Prob(w(X) \in E) = \int_E w\dd\nu = \int_E \dd\pi = \pi(E).
\]


Because $\pi$ is a probability measure, $w(X)$ is mean-one,
\[
  \E_{\nu}\big[w(X)\big] \;=\; \int_{\X} w \dd\nu \;=\; \pi(\X) \;=\; 1,
\]
so $w-1$ is centered, and its dispersion under the reference measure,
\begin{equation}
  \sigma_w^2 \;:=\; \Var_{\nu}\big(w(X)\big)
  \;=\; \int_{\X} (w-1)^2 \dd\nu
  \;=\; \|w-1\|_{L^2(\nu)}^2
  \;=\; \int_{\X} w^2 \dd\nu - 1
  \;=\; \chi^2(\pi \,\|\, \nu),
  \label{eq:sigma-w}
\end{equation}
summarizes how inhomogeneous the sampling is: $\sigma_w = 0$ exactly when
$w = 1$ $\nu$-a.e., i.e. $\pi = \nu$ (non-preferential sampling), and
$\sigma_w$ grows as $\pi$ departs from $\nu$. Since $\E_\nu[w]=1$, the
scalar $\sigma_w$ is equivalently the coefficient of variation of $w(X)$,
a scale-free measure of sampling unevenness. Note that $w\in L^1(\nu)$ is
automatic, whereas the assumption $w\in L^2(\nu)$---equivalently
$\sigma_w<\infty$---is a genuine restriction, excluding sampling measures
so concentrated relative to $\nu$ that their $\chi^2$ divergence diverges.

Two features of \eqref{eq:sigma-w} shape what follows. It is defined
\emph{against the reference measure} $\nu$: it asks how far the sampling
law departs from the target-averaging weights. 

\begin{definition}[OHC random field]
\label{def:Y}
Let $Y: \Omega_Y \to L^2(\nu)$ be a random variable from ocean states to functional space.
\end{definition}

The bias we study is a property of the \emph{realized} ocean, so we condition on a realization of the field throughout.
Fix $\omega_Y\in\Omega_Y$ and write $y := Y(\omega_Y,\cdot)\in L^2(\nu)$ for the corresponding realization.
Because the study concerns a single physical ocean over the period $\T$, we identify $y$ with the true realized OHC field.
Conditional on the event $\{Y = y\}$; $y$ is a fixed element of $L^2(\nu)$.
Composing $y$ with the coordinate map yields the real-valued random variable $y(X): \X \to \R$ on the population space.

\begin{definition}[Estimand]
\label{def:mu}
The estimand is the \emph{realized mean OHC}, 
\[
  \mu = \E_\nu\big[y(X)\big] = \int_\X y \dd\nu.
\]
As a functional of the random field, $\mu(Y)$ is itself a random conditional on $\{Y=y\}$ it is a fixed scalar. 
Where
\[
  \sigma^2_Y(y) = \Var_{\nu}(y(X)) = \int_{\X}(y - \mu)^2\dd\nu = \|\,y - \mu\,\|^2_{L^2(\nu)}
\]
is the space--time variance of the realized field.
\end{definition}

The variability of $y$ over $\X$ is dominated by the seasonal cycle,
which inflates $\sigma_Y$---and hence every field factor in the bounds
below---while contributing nothing to $\mu$ itself over a window spanning
whole years. We therefore carry the field in decomposed form from the
outset.

\begin{definition}[Admissible offset and residual field]
\label{def:offset}
A function $m\in L^2(\nu)$ is an \emph{admissible offset} if it is fixed
(non-random), evaluable at every observed profile location, and its
reference integral $\E_\nu[m]$ is available in closed form. Given such an
$m$ we write
\[
  y' := y-m,\qquad
  \sigma_{Y'}^2 := \Var_\nu\big(y'(X)\big),\qquad
  D := \E_\pi[m]-\E_\nu[m]
\]
for the \emph{residual}, or \emph{deseasonalized}, field, its space--time
variance, and the \emph{offset aliasing}. The offset used throughout is
the harmonic model of Section~\ref{sec:deseason}, whose seasonal
coefficients are fixed externally and whose reference integral is exact.
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[The offset does not change the estimand]
\label{rem:estimand-invariance}
Definition~\ref{def:mu} is stated for $y$ and is not altered by the
choice of $m$: since $\mu=\E_\nu[y']+\E_\nu[m]$ with the second term
known exactly, the estimand remains the realized mean OHC. We do
\emph{not} take $\E_\nu[y']$ as the target, which would make the
scientific quantity depend on an arbitrary modeling choice. What
deseasonalization changes is the \emph{estimator}
\eqref{eq:deseason-est} and, decisively, the field whose dispersion
enters every bound below: the operative factor is $\sigma_{Y'}$ rather
than $\sigma_Y$, with $\sigma_{Y'}=\sigma_Y\sqrt{1-\rho^2}$ for the
fraction $\rho^2$ of field variance the offset explains
(Lemma~\ref{lem:cv-variance}). Over the analysis window, which spans an
integer number of years, the seasonal harmonics contribute exactly zero
to $\E_\nu[m]$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:exact-integral}), so nothing is
approximated here.
\end{remark}

\begin{definition}[Design mean]
\label{def:mu-w}
The \emph{design mean} is the mean of the realized field under the sampling measure,
\[
  \mu_w = \E_\pi\big[y(Z)\big] = \int_\X y \dd\pi.
\]
Equivalenty,
\[
  \mu_w = \int_\X w\,y \dd\nu = \E_\nu\big[w(X)\,y(X)\big].
\]

\end{definition}

Let $\hat\mu$ denote any estimator of $\mu$ built from the observed profiles (for example a kriging/Gaussian-process interpolant).
Its bias decomposes into a sampling term and a reconstruction term.

\subsection{Bias Decomposition}
\label{sec:defbias}

\begin{definition}[Bias decomposition]
\label{def:bias}
The bias of $\hat\mu$ decomposes as
\[
  B(y) := \hat\mu - \mu
  = \underbrace{\big(\mu_w - \mu\big)}_{\textstyle B_{\mathrm{samp}}(y)}
  + \underbrace{\big(\hat\mu - \mu_w\big)}_{\textstyle B_{\mathrm{pred}}(y)},
\]
where $B_{\mathrm{samp}}(y)$ is the \emph{preferential-sampling bias}
and $B_{\mathrm{pred}}(y)$ is the \emph{prediction bias}. \end{definition}

The sampling term admits a covariance representation on the population
space, the starting point for the bound.

\begin{proposition}[Sampling-bias covariance identity]
\label{prop:cov-identity}
Conditional on $\{Y=y\}$ and the design, 
\[
  B_{\mathrm{samp}}(y) = \mu_w - \mu
  = \E_\nu\big[(w(X)-1)\,y(X)\big]
  = \Cov_\nu\big(w(X),\,y(X)\big).
\]
\end{proposition}
\deferproof{cov-identity}{%
\begin{proof}[Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:cov-identity}]
By Definitions~\ref{def:mu} and~\ref{def:mu-w},
$\mu_w-\mu=\E_\nu[w(X)y(X)]-\E_\nu[y(X)]=\E_\nu[(w(X)-1)y(X)]$. Expanding the
covariance about $\mu$ and using~$\E_{\nu}[w(X)]=1$,
\begin{align*}
  \Cov_\nu(w(X),y(X)) &= \E_\nu\big[(w(X)-1)(y(X)-\mu)\big] \\
  &= \E_\nu\big[(w(X)-1)y(X)\big] - \mu\,\E_\nu[w(X)-1] \\
  &= \E_\nu\big[(w(X)-1)y(X)\big],
\end{align*}
since $\E_\nu[w(X)-1]=0$.
\end{proof}%
}

\begin{remark}[No sampling bias under uncorrelated design]
\label{rem:zero-bias}
If, for the realized field, the weight and the field are uncorrelated
under $\nu$---in particular whenever $w\equiv1$---then
$\Cov_\nu(w,y)=0$ and $B_{\mathrm{samp}}(y)=0$. Preferential-sampling
bias arises precisely from co-variation between where the floats
concentrate ($w$) and the value of the field ($y$).
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[Distribution-free $L^2$ bound]
\label{prop:l2-bound}
Under the conditions of Proposition~\ref{prop:cov-identity},
\[
  \big|B_{\mathrm{samp}}(y)\big|
  = \big|\Cov_\nu(w(X),y(X))\big|
  = |\rho_{w,y}|\,\sigma_w\,\sigma_y
  \;\le\; \sigma_w\,\sigma_y,
\]
where $\rho_{w,y}\in[-1,1]$ is the $\nu$-correlation between $w(X)$ and
$y(X)$. The bound requires only finite second moments and imposes no
smoothness assumption on the field.
\end{proposition}

\section{Estimation}
\label{sec:estimation}

\begin{definition}[Equal-measure partition and the projection operators]
\label{def:partition}
A \emph{level-$K$ partition} is a collection
$\mathcal{P}_K=\{C_1,\dots,C_K\}$ of disjoint measurable cells with
$\bigcup_j C_j=\X$ and equal reference measure $\nu(C_j)=1/K$ for all
$j$. Write $\PK:L^2(\nu)\to L^2(\nu)$ for the \emph{cell-averaging
operator}, i.e., the conditional expectation
$\PK f=\E_\nu[f\mid\mathcal{P}_K]$, which replaces a function by its cell
averages,
\[
  (\PK f)(x)=\bar f_j:=\frac{1}{\nu(C_j)}\int_{C_j} f\dd\nu
  \qquad\text{for }x\in C_j ,
\]
and write $\PKp:=\mathsf{I}-\PK$ for the complementary
\emph{within-cell} (or \emph{fluctuation}) operator,
\[
  (\PKp f)(x) := f(x)-(\PK f)(x) = f(x)-\bar f_j
  \qquad\text{for }x\in C_j .
\]
We abbreviate $f^{\perp}:=\PKp f$, so that every $f\in L^2(\nu)$ splits
as
\begin{equation}
  f=\PK f+\PKp f,\qquad\text{i.e.}\qquad \mathsf{I}=\PK+\PKp ,
  \label{eq:split}
\end{equation}
into its between-cell and within-cell parts.
\end{definition}

The two operators project onto complementary subspaces of $L^2(\nu)$.
Let
\[
  V_K=\Big\{\textstyle\sum_{j=1}^{K}a_j\mathbf{1}_{C_j}\;:\;a_j\in\R\Big\}
\]
be the $K$-dimensional subspace of functions constant on each cell (the
\emph{resolution-$K$} functions), whose indicator basis
$\{\mathbf{1}_{C_1},\dots,\mathbf{1}_{C_K}\}$ is orthogonal because the
cells are disjoint. Its orthogonal complement is
\[
  V_K^{\perp}=\Big\{g\in L^2(\nu)\;:\;\int_{C_j}g\dd\nu=0
    \ \text{for every }j\Big\},
\]
the functions with zero average on every cell, which carry only sub-cell
detail.

\begin{remark}
\label{rem:buildK}
Assuming $\nu$ is a measure over normalized area and time, equal-measure
cells are constructed by partitioning  
longitude into equal intervals, $\sin(\mathrm{lat})$ into equal
intervals (equal area on the sphere), and time into equal intervals,
so that $C_j$ is a longitude~$\times\sin(\mathrm{lat})\times$ time box
and $K=K_{\mathrm{lon}}K_{\mathrm{lat}}K_{\mathrm{t}}$. Each cell then
has equal meausre $\nu(C_j)=1/K$.
\end{remark}

\begin{lemma}[$\PK$ and $\PKp$ are complementary orthogonal projections]
\label{lem:projection}
For every $f\in L^2(\nu)$ we have $\PK f\in V_K$ and $\PKp f\in
V_K^{\perp}$; the operator $\PK$ is the orthogonal projection of
$L^2(\nu)$ onto $V_K$, and $\PKp$ the orthogonal projection onto
$V_K^{\perp}$. Consequently both are idempotent and self-adjoint,
\[
  \PK^2=\PK,\qquad (\PKp)^2=\PKp,\qquad \PK\PKp=\PKp\PK=0,
\]
their ranges are orthogonal, $\langle \PK f,\ \PKp g\rangle_\nu=0$ for all
$f,g\in L^2(\nu)$, and the Pythagorean identity
\begin{equation}
  \|f\|_{L^2(\nu)}^2=\|\PK f\|_{L^2(\nu)}^2+\|\PKp f\|_{L^2(\nu)}^2
  \label{eq:pythagoras}
\end{equation}
holds.
\end{lemma}
\deferproof{projection}{%
\begin{proof}[Proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:projection}]
$\PK f$ is constant on each cell, so $\PK f\in V_K$. For the residual,
use $\bar f_j\,\nu(C_j)=\int_{C_j}f\dd\nu$ from the definition of the
cell mean:
\[
  \int_{C_j}\PKp f\dd\nu
  =\int_{C_j}\big(f-\bar f_j\big)\dd\nu
  =\int_{C_j}f\dd\nu-\bar f_j\,\nu(C_j)=0
  \qquad\text{for every }j,
\]
so $\PKp f\in V_K^{\perp}$ by the characterization above. Orthogonality
of the ranges follows cell by cell: $\PK f$ takes the constant value
$\bar f_j$ on $C_j$, so
\[
  \big\langle \PK f,\ \PKp g\big\rangle_\nu
  =\sum_{j=1}^{K}\int_{C_j}\bar f_j\,(\PKp g)\dd\nu
  =\sum_{j=1}^{K}\bar f_j\underbrace{\int_{C_j}\PKp g\dd\nu}_{=\,0}
  =0 .
\]
In particular $f-\PK f$ is orthogonal to all of $V_K$, which is the
defining property of the orthogonal projection onto $V_K$; since
$\PKp=\mathsf{I}-\PK$, it is the orthogonal projection onto
$V_K^{\perp}$. Idempotence, self-adjointness and $\PK\PKp=0$ are the
standard consequences, and applying the orthogonality to
$f=\PK f+\PKp f$ gives \eqref{eq:pythagoras}.
\end{proof}%
}

Intuitively, subtracting the cell mean removes everything a
piecewise-constant function could capture: the average has already
extracted all of $f$ that is visible at cell resolution, so the residual
has nothing left to say there. That residual is $\PKp f$, the
\emph{within-cell fluctuation}: on each cell it equals $f$ minus that
cell's mean, and hence integrates to zero within every cell. It lives in
$V_K^{\perp}$, the functions carrying only sub-cell detail, so the split
$f=\PK f+\PKp f$ of~\eqref{eq:split} separates $f$ into its coarse,
between-cell part and its fine, within-cell part.

\subsection{Decomposing the weight and the field}
\label{sec:decomp-wy}

Lemma~\ref{lem:projection} applies to any $f\in L^2(\nu)$; the two
functions the bound requires are the centered weight $w-1$ and the field
anomaly $y-\mu$. Both are centered under $\nu$: $\E_\nu[w(X)]=1$ by
\eqref{eq:sigma-w}, and $\E_\nu[y(X)]=\mu$ by Definition~\ref{def:mu}.
Applying the operator identity~\eqref{eq:split} to each,
\begin{align}
  w-1 &= \underbrace{\PK(w-1)}_{\text{between-cell}}
        \;+\;\underbrace{\PKp(w-1)}_{\text{within-cell}}
        \;=\;w^{\star}+w^{\perp},
  \label{eq:w-split}\\[2pt]
  y-\mu &= \underbrace{\PK(y-\mu)}_{\text{between-cell}}
        \;+\;\underbrace{\PKp(y-\mu)}_{\text{within-cell}}
        \;=\;y^{\star}+y^{\perp},
  \label{eq:y-split}
\end{align}
where we have written
\[
  w^{\star}:=\PK(w-1)=\PK w-1,\qquad w^{\perp}:=\PKp(w-1)=\PKp w,
\]
\[
  y^{\star}:=\PK(y-\mu)=\PK y-\mu,\qquad y^{\perp}:=\PKp(y-\mu)=\PKp y,
\]
using that $\PK$ fixes constants ($\PK 1=1$, since $1\in V_K$) and hence
$\PKp$ annihilates them ($\PKp 1=0$). Concretely, the resolved parts
$w^{\star},y^{\star}$ are piecewise constant, equal to $\bar w_j-1$ and
$\bar y_j-\mu$ on $C_j$, while the unresolved parts $w^{\perp},y^{\perp}$
are $w-\bar w_j$ and $y-\bar y_j$ on $C_j$, each with zero average on
every cell.

\subsubsection*{Decomposition of the variances}

Because the two components in \eqref{eq:w-split} are orthogonal
(Lemma~\ref{lem:projection}), the Pythagorean
identity~\eqref{eq:pythagoras} splits the dispersion of the weight into
between- and within-cell pieces. Define
\begin{equation}
  \sigma_{w^{\star}} := \big\|w^{\star}\big\|_{L^2(\nu)},
  \qquad
  \sigma_{w^{\perp}} := \big\|w^{\perp}\big\|_{L^2(\nu)},
  \label{eq:sigma-w-parts}
\end{equation}
and analogously for the field,
\begin{equation}
  \sigma_{Y^{\star}} := \big\|y^{\star}\big\|_{L^2(\nu)},
  \qquad
  \sigma_{Y^{\perp}} := \big\|y^{\perp}\big\|_{L^2(\nu)} .
  \label{eq:sigma-Y-parts}
\end{equation}
Then
\begin{equation}
  \sigma_w^2=\sigma_{w^{\star}}^2+\sigma_{w^{\perp}}^2,
  \qquad
  \sigma_Y^2=\sigma_{Y^{\star}}^2+\sigma_{Y^{\perp}}^2 .
  \label{eq:var-split}
\end{equation}

These are exactly the law of total variance. Since
$\PK f=\E_\nu[f\mid\mathcal{P}_K]$, writing $X\sim\nu$ and letting
$J=J(X)$ denote the (random) index of the cell containing $X$,
\eqref{eq:var-split} reads
\[
  \underbrace{\Var_\nu\big(w(X)\big)}_{\sigma_w^2}
  =\underbrace{\Var_\nu\Big(\E_\nu\big[w(X)\mid J\big]\Big)}_{
      \sigma_{w^{\star}}^2\ =\ \frac1K \sum_j (\bar w_j-1)^2}
  \;+\;
  \underbrace{\E_\nu\Big[\Var_\nu\big(w(X)\mid J\big)\Big]}_{
      \sigma_{w^{\perp}}^2\ =\ \frac1K \sum_j \Var_\nu(w\mid C_j)} ,
\]
and identically for $y$: the resolved term is the variance of the cell
means, and the unresolved term is the mean of the within-cell variances.
In words, $\sigma_{w^{\star}}$ measures how unevenly sampling effort is
distributed \emph{across} cells---the dispersion of the cell weights
$\bar w_j=K\pi_j$ about one---while $\sigma_{w^{\perp}}$ measures the
sampling structure \emph{inside} cells, which the partition cannot see.
The same split separates field variation across cells from field
variation within them.

\subsubsection*{Why the split is useful}

The decompositions \eqref{eq:w-split}--\eqref{eq:y-split} are what allow
the bias itself to be separated by scale. Substituting them into the
covariance identity of Proposition~\ref{prop:cov-identity} and using the
orthogonality $\langle \PK f,\PKp g\rangle_\nu=0$ of
Lemma~\ref{lem:projection} to kill the two cross terms,
\begin{equation}
  B_{\mathrm{samp}}
  =\big\langle\, w-1,\; y-\mu \,\big\rangle_\nu
  =\underbrace{\big\langle\, w^{\star},\; y^{\star} \,\big\rangle_\nu}_{\text{between-cell}}
  \;+\;\underbrace{\big\langle\, w^{\perp},\; y^{\perp} \,\big\rangle_\nu}_{\text{within-cell}} ,
  \label{eq:bias-split}
\end{equation}
so the resolved and unresolved scales contribute additively and without
interference. Applying Cauchy--Schwarz to each term of
\eqref{eq:bias-split} separately pairs $\sigma_{w^{\star}}$ with
$\sigma_{Y^{\star}}$ and $\sigma_{w^{\perp}}$ with $\sigma_{Y^{\perp}}$,
giving
\[
  \big|B_{\mathrm{samp}}\big|
  \;\le\;\sigma_{w^{\star}}\,\sigma_{Y^{\star}}
  \;+\;\sigma_{w^{\perp}}\,\sigma_{Y^{\perp}} ,
\]
which is the content of Proposition~\ref{prop:scale-decomp}. This is what
makes a coarse partition---one the float counts can actually
support---admissible for bounding the bias, provided the within-cell
field variation $\sigma_{Y^{\perp}}$ is small.


\begin{proposition}[Scale-decomposition bound]
\label{prop:scale-decomp}
Conditional on the realized field $\{Y=y\}$ and the design, and under
the hypotheses of Proposition~\ref{prop:cov-identity}, for any
partition $\mathcal{P}_K$,
\[
  B_{\mathrm{samp}}
  =\underbrace{\big\langle\,w^{\star},\ y^{\star}\,\big\rangle_\nu}_{\text{between-cell}}
  +\underbrace{\big\langle\,w^{\perp},\ y^{\perp}\,\big\rangle_\nu}_{\text{within-cell}},
\]
and
\[
  |B_{\mathrm{samp}}|
  \;\le\; \sigma_{w^{\star}}\,\sigma_{Y^{\star}}
        \;+\;\sigma_{w^{\perp}}\,\sigma_{Y^{\perp}}
  \;\le\; \sigma_w\,\sigma_Y ,
\]
with $w^{\star},w^{\perp},y^{\star},y^{\perp}$ as in
\eqref{eq:w-split}--\eqref{eq:y-split} and the four dispersions as in
\eqref{eq:sigma-w-parts}--\eqref{eq:sigma-Y-parts}. In particular, the
scale-decomposed bound is never looser than the raw Cauchy--Schwarz
bound $\sigma_w\sigma_Y$.
\end{proposition}
\deferproof{scale-decomp}{%
\begin{proof}[Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:scale-decomp}]
Substitute the decompositions $w-1=w^{\star}+w^{\perp}$ and
$y-\mu=y^{\star}+y^{\perp}$ of \eqref{eq:w-split}--\eqref{eq:y-split}
into the covariance identity
$B_{\mathrm{samp}}=\langle w-1,\,y-\mu\rangle_\nu$
(Proposition~\ref{prop:cov-identity}). Bilinearity of the inner product
gives four terms,
\begin{align*}
  B_{\mathrm{samp}}
  &=\big\langle\, w^{\star}+w^{\perp},\ y^{\star}+y^{\perp} \,\big\rangle_\nu\\[2pt]
  &=\underbrace{\big\langle\, w^{\star},\ y^{\star} \,\big\rangle_\nu}_{\text{between-cell}}
   \;+\;\underbrace{\big\langle\, w^{\star},\ y^{\perp} \,\big\rangle_\nu}_{\text{cross term}}
   \;+\;\underbrace{\big\langle\, w^{\perp},\ y^{\star} \,\big\rangle_\nu}_{\text{cross term}}
   \;+\;\underbrace{\big\langle\, w^{\perp},\ y^{\perp} \,\big\rangle_\nu}_{\text{within-cell}} .
\end{align*}
The two cross terms vanish by the orthogonality $V_K\perp V_K^{\perp}$ of
Lemma~\ref{lem:projection}: $w^{\star},y^{\star}\in V_K$ are constant on
each cell, while $w^{\perp},y^{\perp}\in V_K^{\perp}$ have zero average
on each cell. Explicitly, splitting the integral over the cells and
pulling out the constant value $\bar w_j-1$ of $w^{\star}$ on $C_j$,
\[
  \big\langle\, w^{\star},\ y^{\perp} \,\big\rangle_\nu
  =\int_{\X} w^{\star}\,y^{\perp}\dd\nu
  =\sum_{j=1}^{K}\int_{C_j}(\bar w_j-1)\,y^{\perp}\dd\nu
  =\sum_{j=1}^{K}(\bar w_j-1)\underbrace{\int_{C_j}y^{\perp}\dd\nu}_{=\,0}
  =0 ,
\]
and symmetrically $\langle w^{\perp},y^{\star}\rangle_\nu=0$, exchanging
the roles of $w$ and $y$. Only the between-cell and within-cell terms
survive, which is the stated two-term identity; Cauchy--Schwarz applied
to each of them separately yields
\[
  |B_{\mathrm{samp}}|
  \;\le\;\big|\big\langle w^{\star},y^{\star}\big\rangle_\nu\big|
        +\big|\big\langle w^{\perp},y^{\perp}\big\rangle_\nu\big|
  \;\le\;\sigma_{w^{\star}}\sigma_{Y^{\star}}
        +\sigma_{w^{\perp}}\sigma_{Y^{\perp}} .
\]

For the final inequality, apply the Cauchy--Schwarz inequality in $\R^2$
to the vectors
$\mathbf a=(\sigma_{w^{\star}},\sigma_{w^{\perp}})$ and
$\mathbf b=(\sigma_{Y^{\star}},\sigma_{Y^{\perp}})$:
\[
  \sigma_{w^{\star}}\sigma_{Y^{\star}}+\sigma_{w^{\perp}}\sigma_{Y^{\perp}}
  =\mathbf a\cdot\mathbf b
  \;\le\;\|\mathbf a\|\,\|\mathbf b\|
  =\sqrt{\sigma_{w^{\star}}^2+\sigma_{w^{\perp}}^2}\,
   \sqrt{\sigma_{Y^{\star}}^2+\sigma_{Y^{\perp}}^2}
  =\sigma_w\,\sigma_Y ,
\]
the last equality being the Pythagorean identities \eqref{eq:var-split}.
Equality holds precisely when $\mathbf a$ and $\mathbf b$ are parallel,
i.e. when $w$ and $y$ distribute their dispersion between the resolved
and unresolved scales in the same proportion,
$\sigma_{w^{\perp}}/\sigma_{w^{\star}}=\sigma_{Y^{\perp}}/\sigma_{Y^{\star}}$;
otherwise the scale-decomposed bound is strictly tighter.
\end{proof}%
}

Finally, we record how both decompositions behave under a change of
partition, since the resolution at which they are evaluated is itself a
choice (Section~\ref{sec:resolution}).

\begin{lemma}[Coarsening is a monotone lower bound]
\label{lem:coarsening}
For any partition $\mathcal{P}_K$,
\[
  \sigma_{w^{\star}}\;\le\;\sigma_w,
  \qquad\text{with}\qquad
  \sigma_w^2-\sigma_{w^{\star}}^2
  =\sigma_{w^{\perp}}^2
  =\E_\nu\big[\Var_\nu(w\mid\mathcal{P}_K)\big]\;\ge\;0 .
\]
Moreover, under refinement the bound tightens monotonically: if
$\mathcal{P}_{K'}$ refines $\mathcal{P}_{K}$, in the sense that every
cell of $\mathcal{P}_{K}$ is a union of cells of $\mathcal{P}_{K'}$, then
$\sigma_{w^{\star}}(\mathcal{P}_{K})\le\sigma_{w^{\star}}(\mathcal{P}_{K'})\le\sigma_w$.
The same statements hold verbatim for the field, with
$(\sigma_{Y^{\star}},\sigma_{Y^{\perp}},\sigma_Y)$ in place of
$(\sigma_{w^{\star}},\sigma_{w^{\perp}},\sigma_w)$.
\end{lemma}

\deferproof{coarsening}{%
\begin{proof}
The identity is the Pythagorean decomposition \eqref{eq:var-split},
$\sigma_w^2=\sigma_{w^{\star}}^2+\sigma_{w^{\perp}}^2$, together with the
identification of the within-cell term as an expected conditional
variance (Section~\ref{sec:decomp-wy}); non-negativity of
$\sigma_{w^{\perp}}^2=\|w^{\perp}\|_{L^2(\nu)}^2$ gives
$\sigma_{w^{\star}}\le\sigma_w$. For monotonicity, refinement enlarges the
subspace of piecewise-constant functions, $V_{K}\subseteq V_{K'}$, and
the norm of an orthogonal projection cannot decrease when the range is
enlarged: $\|\mathsf{P}_{V_{K}}f\|\le\|\mathsf{P}_{V_{K'}}f\|$ for every
$f\in L^2(\nu)$. Applying this to $f=w-1$ gives the claim.
\end{proof}%
}

\section{Implementation}
\label{sec:implementation}

The variance components that define the preferential sampling bound can be estimated without fitting models.
The between-cell weight dispersion $\sigma_{w^{\star}}$ follows from
float counts alone (Section~\ref{sec:sigmawK}). The leading field factor
is bounded above by the observed OHC variance together with a
conservative kurtosis constant (Section~\ref{sec:sigmaY-bound}), applied
to the deseasonalized field $y'$---the seasonal cycle having been
removed as a control variate whose own sampling bias is computed rather
than bounded (Section~\ref{sec:deseason}). The residual factor
$\sigma_{Y'^{\perp}}$ is estimated from within-cell float pairs
\eqref{eq:sigmaYperp-pairs}. No external field product enters the
guarantee as an amplitude; where one is consulted it supplies a scale or
a dimensionless ratio only (Remark~\ref{rem:conservative-Y}).
$\sigma_{w^{\perp}}$, the sampling variability \emph{within} the cell scale cannot be estimated from a sparse point pattern without reintroducing a within-cell intensity model.
However, proposition~\ref{prop:scale-decomp} confines it to a product with $\sigma_{Y^{\perp}}$, so the magnitude of $\sigma_{w^{\star}}$ only matters to the extent the OHC field varies within cells.
Therefore, our goal is to determine the cell scale such that within-cell OHC variability, $\sigma_{Y^{\perp}}$, is small such that the $\sigma_{w^{\star}}$ $\sigma_{Y^{\perp}}$ product is negligible.

We establish the three factors in turn---the weight dispersion from
counts, the field envelope, and the offset that defines the field it is
applied to---and only then fix the partition
(Section~\ref{sec:resolution}), since each of the constraints that
determine it is stated in terms of one of these quantities. Readers
wanting the conclusion first may take the operating cell scale to lie in
the ${\approx}25$--$76$\,km band of \eqref{eq:cell-km}, bracketed below
by the float spacing $s_F$ and above by the OHC decorrelation length
$L_{\mathrm{rel}}$.

\subsection{Estimating $\sigma_{w^{\star}}$ from float counts}
\label{sec:sigmawK}

\begin{definition}[Cell sampling weight]
\label{def:sweight}
Let $\bar w_j$ be the \emph{sampling weight} for cell $j$, which is the ratio of the sampling mass of the cell to its reference mass,
\begin{equation}
  \bar w_j \;=\; \frac{1}{\nu(C_j)}\int_{C_j}w\dd\nu \;=\; \frac{\pi(C_j)}{\nu(C_j)} \;=\; \frac{\pi_j}{1/K}
  \;=\; K\pi_j ,
  \label{eq:cell-weight}
\end{equation}
where $\pi_j:=\pi(C_j)$ for the probability that a randomly sampled profile lies in cell $C_j$.
\end{definition}

This is coarse cell counterpart of the pointwise weight $w$ of
Definition~\ref{def:w}: $\bar w_j=1$ means cell $j$ is sampled exactly as
under uniform coverage, $\bar w_j>1$ over-sampled (preferential),
$\bar w_j<1$ under-sampled, and $\bar w_j=0$ unsampled. Because
$\sum_j\pi_j=1$ and $\nu(C_j)=1/K$, the weights are mean-one under the
reference measure, $\sum_j\nu(C_j)\bar w_j=\sum_j\pi_j=1$.

Let $n_j:=N(C_j)$ be the number of profiles observed in cell $j$ and
$N=\sum_j n_j$ the total. The natural estimator of $\bar w_j$ is
\begin{equation}
  \widehat{\bar w}_j=K\,\frac{n_j}{N}=\frac{n_j}{\,N/K\,}
  =\frac{\text{observed count in }C_j}{\text{count expected under uniform}} ,
  \label{eq:wbar-hat}
\end{equation}
so that a map of $\widehat{\bar w}_j$ over the domain displays directly
where floats over- and under-sample relative to uniform coverage.

\begin{lemma}[Cell sampling weight variance]
\label{lem:sigmawcell}
The variance of the cell sampling weights is
\[
  \sigma^2_{w^{\star}} = K\sum_{j=1}^{K}\pi_j^2-1 .
\]
\end{lemma}
\deferproof{sigmawcell}{%
\begin{proof}[Proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:sigmawcell}]
$\sigma^2_{w^{\star}}$ is the variance of the cells weights about one.
This is because $w^{\star}=\PK w-1$ is a constant function on each cell with cell value on $C_j$ is $\bar w_j-1$.

Accordingly,
\begin{align*}
  \sigma_{w^{\star}}^2
  &=\int_{\X}(w^{\star})^2\dd\nu
   =\sum_{j=1}^{K}\int_{C_j}\big(\bar w_j-1\big)^2\dd\nu
   =\sum_{j=1}^{K}\big(\bar w_j-1\big)^2\,\nu(C_j)
   =\frac{1}{K}\sum_{j=1}^{K}\big(K\pi_j-1\big)^2 \\
  &=\frac{1}{K}\sum_{j=1}^{K}\Big(K^2\pi_j^2-2K\pi_j+1\Big)
   =K\sum_{j=1}^{K}\pi_j^2\;-\;2\underbrace{\sum_{j=1}^{K}\pi_j}_{=\,1}
     \;+\;\frac{1}{K}\underbrace{\sum_{j=1}^{K}1}_{=\,K} \\
  &=K\sum_{j=1}^{K}\pi_j^2-1 .
  \label{eq:sigmawK-def}
\end{align*}
\end{proof}%
}

Note, uniform sampling within the partition ($\pi_j=1/K$ for all $j$, i.e.
$\bar w_j\equiv1$) gives $K\cdot K^{-1}-1=0$.
Whereas when all sampling mass is in a single cell gives $K-1$.
Hence, $0\le\sigma_{w^{\star}}\le\sqrt{K-1}$.
Thus, between-cell sampling variability can increase arbitrarily as the number of cells $K$ grows, degenerating as almost all cells have zero observed counts.

\begin{assumption}[Profiles sample the sampling measure]
\label{ass:sample-pi}
Conditional on the total $N$, the random elements $Z_1,\dots,Z_N$ of
Definition~\ref{def:Zi} are independent draws from the sampling measure
$\pi$ of Definition~\ref{def:pi}; equivalently, the cell counts follow
$(n_1,\dots,n_K)\sim\mathrm{Multinomial}\big(N;\pi_1,\dots,\pi_K\big)$
with $\pi_j=\pi(C_j)$.
\end{assumption}

The plug-in $\hat\pi_j=n_j/N$ gives
\[
  \sigwhatsqplug
  =K\sum_j\hat\pi_j^2-1=\frac{X^2}{N},
  \qquad X^2=\sum_{j=1}^{K}\frac{(n_j-N/K)^2}{N/K},
\]
where $X^2$ is \emph{Pearson's goodness-of-fit statistic for
uniformity}.
This plug-in estimator is upward biased and removing the diagonal (self-pairs) removes the bias (Lemma~\ref{lem:unbiased}):
\begin{equation}
  \sigwhatsq
  \;=\;K\sum_{j=1}^{K}\frac{n_j(n_j-1)}{N(N-1)}\;-\;1
  \label{eq:sigmawK-hat}
\end{equation}

\begin{remark}[Negative estimates]
\label{rem:negative}
Being unbiased for a non-negative quantity,
\eqref{eq:sigmawK-hat} can take negative values in finite samples.
A negative value arises exactly when the observed coincidence rate falls below the uniform rate, $\sum_j n_j(n_j-1)/[N(N-1)]<1/K$, and so indicates sampling at least as
even as the reference measure.
We retain the signed value for interval construction and report
$\sigwhat=\sqrt{\max\{0,\sigwhatsq\}}$
as the point estimate; near-zero results are stated on the $\sigma^2$
scale with their signed interval, as ``consistent with zero'' rather than
truncated.
\end{remark}

\begin{lemma}[Bias of the plug-in; unbiasedness of \eqref{eq:sigmawK-hat}]
\label{lem:unbiased}
Under Assumption~\ref{ass:sample-pi},
\[
  \E\big[\sigwhatsqplug\big]
  =\sigma_{w^{\star}}^2+\frac{K}{N}\Big(1-\textstyle\sum_j\pi_j^2\Big),
  \qquad
  \E\big[\sigwhatsq\big]=\sigma_{w^{\star}}^2 .
\]
The plug-in bias is non-negative, vanishing only in the degenerate case
$\sum_j\pi_j^2=1$ (all sampling mass in one cell), and grows with $K/N$;
the diagonal-removed estimator~\eqref{eq:sigmawK-hat} is exactly unbiased
for every $N\ge 2$ and every $K$.
\end{lemma}
\deferproof{unbiased}{%
\begin{proof}[Proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:unbiased}]
\begin{align}
  \mathrm{Bias}\big(\sigwhatsqplug\big)
  &=\E\Big[K\sum_{j=1}^{K}\hat\pi_j^2-1\Big]
    -\Big(K\sum_{j=1}^{K}\pi_j^2-1\Big)
   =K\sum_{j=1}^{K}\Big(\E\big[\hat\pi_j^2\big]-\pi_j^2\Big), \\
  \mathrm{Bias}\big(\sigwhatsq\big)
  &=\E\Big[K\sum_{j=1}^{K}\frac{n_j(n_j-1)}{N(N-1)}-1\Big]
    -\Big(K\sum_{j=1}^{K}\pi_j^2-1\Big)
   =K\sum_{j=1}^{K}\Big(\E\Big[\frac{n_j(n_j-1)}{N(N-1)}\Big]-\pi_j^2\Big).
  \label{eq:bias-diag}
\end{align}
It therefore suffices to evaluate the two cell-level expectations.

\emph{Step 1: moments of the counts.} Under
Assumption~\ref{ass:sample-pi} the counts are multinomial, so each
marginal is $n_j\sim\mathrm{Binomial}(N,\pi_j)$, giving
\[
  \E[n_j]=N\pi_j,\qquad \Var(n_j)=N\pi_j(1-\pi_j),\qquad
  \E[n_j^2]=\Var(n_j)+\E[n_j]^2=N\pi_j(1-\pi_j)+N^2\pi_j^2 .
\]

\emph{Step 2: the plug-in cell term is biased.} Dividing the second
moment by $N^2$,
\[
  \E\big[\hat\pi_j^2\big]=\frac{\E[n_j^2]}{N^2}
  =\pi_j^2+\frac{1}{N}\,\pi_j(1-\pi_j)
  \;>\;\pi_j^2 \quad\text{whenever }0<\pi_j<1 ,
\]
so each cell term over-estimates $\pi_j^2$ by $\pi_j(1-\pi_j)/N$.
Substituting into \eqref{eq:bias-plug} and using $\sum_j\pi_j=1$,
\[
  \mathrm{Bias}\big(\sigwhatsqplug\big)
  =K\sum_{j=1}^{K}\frac{\pi_j(1-\pi_j)}{N}
  =\frac{K}{N}\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{K}\pi_j-\sum_{j=1}^{K}\pi_j^2\Big)
  =\frac{K}{N}\Big(1-\sum_{j=1}^{K}\pi_j^2\Big),
\]
which is the first claim. It is non-negative because
$\sum_j\pi_j^2\le\big(\sum_j\pi_j\big)^2=1$, vanishing only when all
sampling mass lies in a single cell, and it grows linearly in $K/N$.

\emph{Step 3: removing the self-pairs.} The inflation arises because
$n_j^2$ counts all $N^2$ \emph{ordered} pairs of profiles falling in
$C_j$, including the $N$ pairs of a profile with itself. A pair of
\emph{distinct} profiles lies in $C_j$ with probability $\pi_j^2$, since
the two are independent draws (Assumption~\ref{ass:sample-pi}); a
profile paired with itself lies there with probability $\pi_j$, as it
coincides with itself by definition. The self-pairs are thus spurious
coincidences, and $n_j(n_j-1)=n_j^2-n_j$ removes exactly those $N$
terms, leaving the $N(N-1)$ ordered pairs of distinct profiles. Hence
\[
  \E\big[n_j(n_j-1)\big]=N(N-1)\,\pi_j^2 ,
  \qquad\text{so}\qquad
  \E\Big[\frac{n_j(n_j-1)}{N(N-1)}\Big]=\pi_j^2 ,
\]
the normalization matching the number of pairs retained. Each cell term
is therefore exactly unbiased for $\pi_j^2$, and \eqref{eq:bias-diag}
gives
\[
  \mathrm{Bias}\big(\sigwhatsq\big)
  =K\sum_{j=1}^{K}\big(\pi_j^2-\pi_j^2\big)=0 ,
\]
i.e. $\E\big[\sigwhatsq\big]=\sigma_{w^{\star}}^2$
for every $N\ge2$ and every $K$.
\end{proof}%
}


\begin{remark}[Trajectory dependence and the between-float estimator]
\label{rem:trajectory}
Estimator~\eqref{eq:sigmawK-hat} treats all profiles as independent draws
from $\pi$ (Assumption~\ref{ass:sample-pi}), yet profiles from one float
trace a single advective path and are not mutually independent. The
resulting error is nonetheless one-sided under the physically relevant
form of that dependence. Writing $c_a$ for the cell of profile $a$ and
extending the computation of Lemma~\ref{lem:unbiased} without its
independence step,
\begin{equation}
  \E\big[\sigwhatsq\big]-\sigma_{w^{\star}}^2
  =\frac{K}{N(N-1)}\sum_{a\ne b}
     \Big(\Prob(c_a=c_b)-\textstyle\sum_j\pi_j^2\Big),
  \label{eq:diag-bias-identity}
\end{equation}
an exact identity over the ordered pairs of distinct profiles. Two
independent draws from $\pi$ share a cell with probability exactly
$\sum_j\pi_j^2$, so each summand is the excess coincidence of a pair over
that baseline. Between-float pairs contribute nothing when distinct floats
are independent, and a non-negative amount under the shared advection
discussed below; within-float pairs---two cycles of one float, some ten
days and a short displacement apart relative to the cell scale---fall in a
common cell at least as often as independent draws, so their summands are
non-negative. Hence $\E\big[\sigwhatsq\big]\ge\sigma_{w^{\star}}^2$:
within-float clustering inflates \eqref{eq:sigmawK-hat}, the conservative
direction for an upper bound on $\sigma_{w^{\star}}$.
\end{remark}

Therefore, we also present an alternative \emph{between-float} estimator that counts only coincidence pairs from distinct floats.
Let $m_{\ell j}$ be the number of profiles of float $\ell$ in cell $j$ and $d_\ell=\sum_j m_{\ell j}$ its total, \begin{equation} \sigwhatsqbf = K\,\frac{\sum_j n_j(n_j-1)-\sum_\ell\sum_j m_{\ell j}(m_{\ell j}-1)} {N(N-1)-\sum_\ell d_\ell(d_\ell-1)}\;-\;1 , \label{eq:sigmawK-bf} \end{equation} which removes the within-float diagonal and normalizes by the between-float pair total.
Under the assumption that \emph{distinct} floats are independent draws from $\pi$, it is unbiased for $\sigma_{w^{\star}}^2$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:bf-unbiased}), with no within-float independence required, so it removes the within-float autocorrelation to which \eqref{eq:sigmawK-hat} is exposed.
This between-float independence is, however, only approximate: floats with a shared deployment or advective history---nearby releases entrained in the same current or mesoscale features---have positively associated cell memberships, so two of their profiles fall in a common cell with probability at least $\sum_j\pi_j^2$, and hence $\E\big[\sigwhatsqbf\big]\ge\sigma_{w^{\star}}^2$. The residual bias is thus in the same upward direction as the within-float clustering the estimator removes---conservative for an upper bound on $\sigma_{w^{\star}}$---and the between-float coherence it leaves is the weaker term, the same water parcel no longer being shared and trajectories diverging over time. We therefore use \eqref{eq:sigmawK-bf} in this spirit, as a conservative input to the bound rather than an exactly unbiased estimator.
Comparing \eqref{eq:sigmawK-hat} and \eqref{eq:sigmawK-bf}, which differ only through the within-float diagonal terms removed from the latter, is moreover an assumption-free diagnostic of how much of the sampling variability is trajectory autocorrelation rather than preferential sampling.

\begin{lemma}[Unbiasedness of the between-float estimator]
\label{lem:bf-unbiased}
If profiles from \emph{distinct} floats are independent draws with marginal
distribution $\pi$ then $\E\big[\sigwhatsqbf\big] = \sigma_{w^{\star}}^2$.
\end{lemma}
\deferproof{bf-unbiased}{%
\begin{proof}[Proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:bf-unbiased}]
Index profiles by $(\ell,i)$, with $\ell=1,\dots,L$ the float and
$i=1,\dots,d_\ell$ the profile within float $\ell$; let
$c_{\ell i}\in\{1,\dots,K\}$ be the cell membership of profile $(\ell,i)$, so that
\[
  m_{\ell j}=\sum_{i=1}^{d_\ell}\1\{c_{\ell i}=j\},\qquad
  n_j=\sum_{\ell}m_{\ell j},\qquad
  N=\sum_\ell d_\ell .
\]
We use only the two hypotheses of the lemma:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(A1)] $\Prob(c_{\ell i}=j)=\pi_j$ for every profile $(\ell,i)$ and
  cell $j$;
\item[(A2)] the cell membership vectors of distinct floats are independent,
  $(c_{\ell i})_{i\le d_\ell}\perp\!\!\!\perp(c_{\ell'i'})_{i'\le d_{\ell'}}$
  for $\ell\ne\ell'$.
\end{enumerate}
Nothing is assumed about the joint distribution
$(c_{\ell 1},\dots,c_{\ell d_\ell})$ of the cells within a single float.
Write
$S_{\mathrm{bf}}=\sum_j n_j(n_j-1)-\sum_\ell\sum_j m_{\ell j}(m_{\ell j}-1)$
and $P_{\mathrm{bf}}=N(N-1)-\sum_\ell d_\ell(d_\ell-1)$ for the numerator
and denominator of~\eqref{eq:sigmawK-bf}, so
$\sigwhatsqbf=K\,S_{\mathrm{bf}}/P_{\mathrm{bf}}-1$.

The whole argument rests on one structural observation: $S_{\mathrm{bf}}$
and $P_{\mathrm{bf}}$ are sums over the \emph{same} index set, the ordered
pairs of profiles drawn from two \emph{distinct} floats. The denominator
counts all such pairs; the numerator counts those that land in a common
cell. Each pair coincides with one and the same expected probability
$\sum_j\pi_j^2$, so $\E[S_{\mathrm{bf}}]=\bigl(\sum_j\pi_j^2\bigr)
P_{\mathrm{bf}}$ term by term, and the ratio is exactly $\sum_j\pi_j^2$.
The three steps below make this precise.

\emph{Step 1 (the between-float pair set).}
Let
\[
  \mathcal B=\bigl\{\bigl((\ell,i),(\ell',i')\bigr):\ \ell\ne\ell',\
    i\le d_\ell,\ i'\le d_{\ell'}\bigr\}
\]
be the ordered pairs of profiles belonging to two distinct floats. Its
size depends only on the design $(d_\ell)_\ell$: since $N=\sum_\ell d_\ell$,
\[
  |\mathcal B|=\sum_{\ell\ne\ell'}d_\ell d_{\ell'}
  =\Bigl(\sum_\ell d_\ell\Bigr)^2-\sum_\ell d_\ell^2
  =N(N-1)-\sum_\ell d_\ell(d_\ell-1)=P_{\mathrm{bf}} .
\]
Thus $P_{\mathrm{bf}}$ is a deterministic constant, strictly positive as
soon as two floats each contribute a profile.

\emph{Step 2 ($S_{\mathrm{bf}}$ counts the coinciding pairs in $\mathcal B$).}
Expanding $n_j=\sum_\ell m_{\ell j}$ separates diagonal from off-diagonal
terms,
\[
  n_j(n_j-1)=n_j^2-n_j
  =\sum_\ell m_{\ell j}(m_{\ell j}-1)+\sum_{\ell\ne\ell'}m_{\ell j}m_{\ell'j},
\]
so summing over $j$ cancels the within-float diagonal against the term
subtracted in $S_{\mathrm{bf}}$, leaving
\[
  S_{\mathrm{bf}}=\sum_{\ell\ne\ell'}\sum_{j=1}^{K}m_{\ell j}m_{\ell'j}
  =\sum_{\ell\ne\ell'}\sum_{i=1}^{d_\ell}\sum_{i'=1}^{d_{\ell'}}
    \sum_{j=1}^{K}\1\{c_{\ell i}=j\}\1\{c_{\ell'i'}=j\}
  =\sum_{((\ell,i),(\ell',i'))\in\mathcal B}\1\{c_{\ell i}=c_{\ell'i'}\},
\]
where the second equality inserts the definition of $m_{\ell j}$ and the
third uses
$\sum_j\1\{c_{\ell i}=j\}\1\{c_{\ell'i'}=j\}=\1\{c_{\ell i}=c_{\ell'i'}\}$. So
$S_{\mathrm{bf}}$ is exactly the number of pairs in $\mathcal B$ that fall
in a common cell; every pair with $\ell=\ell'$ has dropped out.

\emph{Step 3 (each pair in $\mathcal B$ coincides with probability
$\sum_j\pi_j^2$).}
Fix $\bigl((\ell,i),(\ell',i')\bigr)\in\mathcal B$, so $\ell\ne\ell'$.
Decomposing over the common cell and applying (A2) then (A1),
\[
  \E\bigl[\1\{c_{\ell i}=c_{\ell'i'}\}\bigr]
  =\sum_{j=1}^{K}\Prob\bigl(c_{\ell i}=j,\;c_{\ell'i'}=j\bigr)
  \overset{\text{(A2)}}{=}\sum_{j=1}^{K}\Prob(c_{\ell i}=j)\Prob(c_{\ell'i'}=j)
  \overset{\text{(A1)}}{=}\sum_{j=1}^{K}\pi_j^{2}.
\]
This is the same constant for every pair in $\mathcal B$, independent of
$i,i'$; (A2) is invoked only across the two distinct floats $\ell\ne\ell'$,
never within a float.

\emph{Step 4 (assembly).}
By Step 2 and linearity, summing the constant of Step 3 over the
$|\mathcal B|=P_{\mathrm{bf}}$ pairs of Step 1,
\[
  \E[S_{\mathrm{bf}}]
  =\sum_{((\ell,i),(\ell',i'))\in\mathcal B}
     \E\bigl[\1\{c_{\ell i}=c_{\ell'i'}\}\bigr]
  =P_{\mathrm{bf}}\sum_{j=1}^{K}\pi_j^{2}.
\]
Since $P_{\mathrm{bf}}$ is deterministic and nonzero,
\[
  \E\bigl[\sigwhatsqbf\bigr]
  =\frac{K\,\E[S_{\mathrm{bf}}]}{P_{\mathrm{bf}}}-1
  =K\sum_{j=1}^{K}\pi_j^{2}-1
  =\sigma_{w^{\star}}^{2}.
\]
The within-float contributions $\sum_\ell\sum_j m_{\ell j}(m_{\ell j}-1)$ and
$\sum_\ell d_\ell(d_\ell-1)$ cancelled in Steps~1--2, before any expectation was
taken, so no assumption on the dependence among a single float's profiles
was needed. (Such dependence still affects $\Var[\sigwhatsqbf]$, but not
its mean.)
\end{proof}%
}

\subsection{A data-only upper bound on the field dispersion}
\label{sec:sigmaY-bound}

The scale-decomposed bound of Proposition~\ref{prop:scale-decomp}
requires a value for the field dispersion, over all of $\X$ and not only
where floats went. A dense external product would supply it directly,
but at a price that Remark~\ref{rem:conservative-Y} makes precise. We
bound it instead from the profiles themselves. The obstruction is that a
float-only dispersion targets the wrong measure
(Proposition~\ref{prop:float-contam}); the resolution is that this
contamination is itself a preferential-sampling bias---of the
squared-anomaly field---so the same calculus that bounds the
first-moment bias bounds it too, converting the observed OHC variance
into a conservative upper bound that requires only a single scalar shape
constant.

Everything in this subsection is stated for a generic field
$y\in L^2(\nu)$ and is applied in practice to the residual field $y'$ of
Definition~\ref{def:offset}; Corollary~\ref{cor:deseason-bound} records
the substitution.

\begin{proposition}[Float-only field dispersion is contaminated]
\label{prop:float-contam}
The empirical dispersion of $Y$ over the float locations estimates
$\Var_\pi(Y)$, not $\Var_\nu(Y)=\sigma_Y^2$, and
\[
  \Var_\pi(Y)-\Var_\nu(Y)=\Cov_\nu\!\big(w,(Y-\mu)^2\big).
\]
\end{proposition}
\deferproof{float-contam}{%
\begin{proof}[Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:float-contam}]
The float locations are draws from $\pi$ (Assumption~\ref{ass:sample-pi}),
so their empirical variance is consistent for $\Var_\pi(Y)$. Using
$\dd\pi=w\dd\nu$ and $\E_\nu[w]=1$,
$\Var_\pi(Y)=\int(y-\mu_\pi)^2 w\dd\nu$; expanding and comparing with
$\int(y-\mu)^2\dd\nu$ gives the stated covariance, the sampling bias of
the squared-anomaly field.
\end{proof}%
}

The discrepancy is non-zero for exactly the reason $B_{\mathrm{samp}}$
is: it is the $w$--$Y$ coupling this paper studies, applied to
$(Y-\mu)^2$. A float-only $\hat\sigma_Y$ is thus inflated when floats
favour the high-variability front and deflated when they collect in
quiescent water. De-biasing by importance weighting
($\widehat{\sigma_Y^2}=\frac1n\sum_i w(x_i)^{-1}(y(x_i)-\hat\mu)^2$)
recovers $\Var_\nu(Y)$ in principle but requires $w$---the object we
avoid modelling---and is dominated by $1/w$ in the undersampled cells
where floats are scarcest, so its variance is largest exactly where the
bound is most sensitive. We therefore do not de-bias, but bound.

\begin{remark}[Why the field dispersion is not imported]
\label{rem:conservative-Y}
The natural alternative is to read $\sigma_Y$ off a dense reanalysis.
Data assimilation, however, tends to \emph{attenuate} anomaly amplitude,
so such a product is liable to understate $\sigma_Y$---the unsafe
direction for an upper bound, and one whose magnitude cannot be
certified from the product itself. The failure is silent: an attenuated
$\hat\sigma_Y$ returns a bound that is too small with no diagnostic
signalling it, and the natural remedy of recomputing across products
bounds only the spread between products, not their common bias. This is
the asymmetry that governs every external input in this paper. A product
may legitimately supply a dimensionless \emph{shape} (the kurtosis of
Remark~\ref{rem:kappa}, which is affine-invariant and so blind to
attenuation), a \emph{scale} (the decorrelation length of
Section~\ref{sec:resolution}, which fixes a partition rather than a
magnitude), or the \emph{location} of a control variate
(Remark~\ref{rem:cv-robust}, where error costs efficiency and not
validity)---but never an \emph{amplitude} entering a bound
multiplicatively.
\end{remark}

\begin{assumption}[Finite fourth moment]
\label{ass:l4}
The realized field satisfies $y\in L^4(\nu)$, equivalently
$\sigma_g:=\big\|\,(y-\mu)^2-\sigma_Y^2\,\big\|_{L^2(\nu)}<\infty$.
\end{assumption}

This is the field-side analogue of the $w\in L^2(\nu)$ restriction of
Definition~\ref{def:w}: the first-moment bound needs two field moments,
the second-moment bound four. Throughout this section write
$g:=(y-\mu)^2$ for the \emph{squared-anomaly field}, so that
$\E_\nu[g]=\sigma_Y^2$ (Definition~\ref{def:mu}) and
$\sigma_g^2=\Var_\nu(g)$.

\begin{proposition}[Exact second-moment contamination]
\label{prop:var-contam}
Let $R:=\Var_\pi(Y)$ be the design variance of the field---the
population analogue of the empirical variance of $y$ over the float
locations. Under Assumption~\ref{ass:l4},
\[
  R=\sigma_Y^2+\Cov_\nu\!\big(w(X),\,g(X)\big)-B_{\mathrm{samp}}^2 .
\]
\end{proposition}
\deferproof{var-contam}{%
\begin{proof}[Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:var-contam}]
Centering the design variance at the target mean $\mu$ rather than the
design mean $\mu_w$ and using $\E_\pi[y-\mu]=\mu_w-\mu=B_{\mathrm{samp}}$
(Definition~\ref{def:mu-w}, Proposition~\ref{prop:cov-identity}),
\[
  R=\E_\pi\big[(y-\mu_w)^2\big]
   =\E_\pi\big[(y-\mu)^2\big]-(\mu_w-\mu)^2
   =\E_\pi[g]-B_{\mathrm{samp}}^2 .
\]
Change of measure $\dd\pi=w\dd\nu$ (Definition~\ref{def:w}) gives
$\E_\pi[g]=\E_\nu[w\,g]$, and since $\E_\nu[w-1]=0$,
\[
  \E_\nu[w\,g]=\E_\nu[g]+\E_\nu[(w-1)g]
             =\sigma_Y^2+\Cov_\nu\!\big(w,g\big),
\]
the last equality because
$\Cov_\nu(w,g)=\E_\nu\big[(w-1)(g-\E_\nu g)\big]=\E_\nu[(w-1)g]$.
Combining the two displays yields the identity.
\end{proof}%
}

\begin{remark}[Relation to Proposition~\ref{prop:float-contam}]
\label{rem:var-contam-order}
Proposition~\ref{prop:float-contam} is
Proposition~\ref{prop:var-contam} with the $-B_{\mathrm{samp}}^2$ term
dropped. We retain it. It is $O(\sigma_w^2\sigma_Y^2)$---second order in
the sampling unevenness---and, entering with a negative sign, it
\emph{widens} the upper envelope derived below, so keeping it is the
conservative choice rather than a refinement to be discarded.
\end{remark}

Because $\Cov_\nu(w,g)$ is the covariance of the weight with the field
$g$, it is a preferential-sampling bias in the exact sense of
Proposition~\ref{prop:cov-identity}, now carried by the squared-anomaly
field. Every step of Sections~\ref{sec:defbias}--\ref{sec:estimation}
therefore transfers verbatim with $g$ in place of $y$. Decomposing
$g-\sigma_Y^2=g^{\star}+g^{\perp}$ through the split~\eqref{eq:split}
and repeating Proposition~\ref{prop:scale-decomp},
\begin{equation}
  \big|\Cov_\nu(w,g)\big|
  \;\le\;\sigma_{w^{\star}}\sigma_{g^{\star}}
        +\sigma_{w^{\perp}}\sigma_{g^{\perp}}
  \;\le\;\sigma_w\sigma_g ,
  \label{eq:var-scale-decomp}
\end{equation}
where
$\sigma_{g^{\star}}:=\|\PK(g-\sigma_Y^2)\|_{L^2(\nu)}$ and
$\sigma_{g^{\perp}}:=\|\PKp(g-\sigma_Y^2)\|_{L^2(\nu)}$.

\begin{lemma}[Kurtosis reduction]
\label{lem:kurt}
Let $\kappa:=\E_\nu[(y-\mu)^4]/\sigma_Y^4$ be the reference-measure
kurtosis of the anomaly field. Then $\kappa\ge1$ and
\[
  \sigma_g=\sigma_Y^2\sqrt{\kappa-1}.
\]
\end{lemma}
\deferproof{kurt}{%
\begin{proof}[Proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:kurt}]
By definition of $g$ and $\E_\nu[g]=\sigma_Y^2$,
\[
  \sigma_g^2=\Var_\nu(g)=\E_\nu[g^2]-\big(\E_\nu g\big)^2
   =\E_\nu\big[(y-\mu)^4\big]-\sigma_Y^4=(\kappa-1)\,\sigma_Y^4 .
\]
Non-negativity of the variance gives $\kappa\ge1$ (equivalently, Jensen's
inequality applied to $G=(y-\mu)^2$).
\end{proof}%
}

The one factor of \eqref{eq:var-scale-decomp} not paired with an
estimable weight dispersion is the within-cell term
$\sigma_{w^{\perp}}\sigma_{g^{\perp}}$, and it is controlled by exactly
the cell-scale choice that already controls the mean-bound residual,
because a field that is nearly constant on a cell has a nearly constant
square.

\begin{lemma}[The within-cell residual is governed by within-cell field
variation]
\label{lem:gperp}
Let $\kappa^{\star}:=\E_\nu[(y^{\star})^4]/\sigma_{Y^{\star}}^4$ and
$\kappa^{\perp}:=\E_\nu[(y^{\perp})^4]/\sigma_{Y^{\perp}}^4$ be the
kurtoses of the between- and within-cell components. Then
\begin{equation}
  \sigma_{g^{\perp}}\;\le\;c_g\,\sigma_{Y^{\perp}},
  \qquad
  c_g:=2\,\sigma_{Y^{\star}}\big(\kappa^{\star}\kappa^{\perp}\big)^{1/4}
       +\sigma_{Y^{\perp}}\sqrt{\kappa^{\perp}} .
  \label{eq:gperp-bound}
\end{equation}
In particular $\sigma_{g^{\perp}}\to0$ as $\sigma_{Y^{\perp}}\to0$ under
refinement, so the within-cell residual of \eqref{eq:var-scale-decomp} is
controlled by the same cell-scale choice that controls the mean-bound
residual $\sigma_{w^{\perp}}\sigma_{Y^{\perp}}$.
\end{lemma}
\deferproof{gperp}{%
\begin{proof}[Proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:gperp}]
On a cell $C_j$ set $a_j:=\bar y_j-\mu$ and $y^{\perp}:=y-\bar y_j$, so
$y-\mu=a_j+y^{\perp}$ and $g=a_j^2+2a_jy^{\perp}+(y^{\perp})^2$. Since
$\int_{C_j}y^{\perp}\dd\nu=0$, the cell mean is
$\bar g_j=a_j^2+\overline{(y^{\perp})^2}_j$, whence
\[
  g^{\perp}=g-\bar g_j
   =2a_jy^{\perp}+\Big((y^{\perp})^2-\overline{(y^{\perp})^2}_j\Big).
\]
Taking $L^2(\nu)$ norms and applying the triangle inequality,
$\sigma_{g^{\perp}}\le 2\|y^{\star}y^{\perp}\|_{L^2(\nu)}
 +\|\PKp((y^{\perp})^2)\|_{L^2(\nu)}$, where $y^{\star}$ denotes the
piecewise-constant function equal to $a_j$ on $C_j$. For the first term,
Cauchy--Schwarz in $L^2(\nu)$ applied to $(y^{\star})^2$ and
$(y^{\perp})^2$ gives
\[
  \|y^{\star}y^{\perp}\|_{L^2(\nu)}^2
  =\E_\nu\big[(y^{\star})^2(y^{\perp})^2\big]
  \le\Big(\E_\nu[(y^{\star})^4]\,\E_\nu[(y^{\perp})^4]\Big)^{1/2}
  =\big(\kappa^{\star}\kappa^{\perp}\big)^{1/2}
   \sigma_{Y^{\star}}^2\sigma_{Y^{\perp}}^2 .
\]
For the second, $\PKp$ is a contraction (Lemma~\ref{lem:projection}), so
$\|\PKp((y^{\perp})^2)\|\le\|(y^{\perp})^2\|_{L^2(\nu)}
 =\sqrt{\kappa^{\perp}}\,\sigma_{Y^{\perp}}^2$. Adding the two gives
\eqref{eq:gperp-bound}.
\end{proof}%
}

\begin{remark}[Why moments rather than a supremum]
\label{rem:gperp-moments}
An earlier form of \eqref{eq:gperp-bound} used $\|y^{\star}\|_\infty$ in
place of $\sigma_{Y^{\star}}(\kappa^{\star})^{1/4}$. The moment form is
preferred because a supremum cannot be estimated safely from a finite
sample: the maximum over $F$ floats systematically \emph{under}-estimates
$\|y^{\star}\|_\infty$, which is the unsafe direction for an upper bound,
whereas fourth moments are estimated without such one-sided error.
\end{remark}

We now carry the within-cell residuals through the argument rather than
discarding them. Both enter only through the single scalar
\begin{equation}
  \varepsilon\;:=\;\sigma_{w^{\perp}}\,\sigma_{Y^{\perp}},
  \label{eq:residual-budget}
\end{equation}
the \emph{residual budget}, which is exactly the quantity already
appearing in the mean bound of Proposition~\ref{prop:scale-decomp}. No
new unknown is introduced.

\begin{proposition}[Data-only upper bound on $\sigma_Y$]
\label{prop:sigmaY-envelope}
Set $\Delta:=\sigma_{w^{\star}}\sqrt{\kappa-1}+\sigma_{w^{\star}}^2$ and
suppose $\Delta<1$. Then for the residual budget $\varepsilon$ of
\eqref{eq:residual-budget},
\begin{equation}
  \sigma_Y\;\le\;\overline{\sigma}_Y(\varepsilon)
  \;:=\;\frac{b\,\varepsilon
    +\sqrt{\,b^2\varepsilon^2
      +(1-\Delta)\big(R+d\,\varepsilon+\varepsilon^2\big)\,}}
    {1-\Delta},
  \label{eq:envelope-eps}
\end{equation}
where $b:=\sigma_{w^{\star}}+(\kappa^{\star}\kappa^{\perp})^{1/4}$ and
$d:=\sigma_{Y^{\perp}}\sqrt{\kappa^{\perp}}$. The map
$\varepsilon\mapsto\overline{\sigma}_Y(\varepsilon)$ is continuous and
strictly increasing, and
\[
  \overline{\sigma}_Y(0)=\sqrt{\frac{R}{1-\Delta}} .
\]
\end{proposition}
\deferproof{sigmaY-envelope}{%
\begin{proof}[Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:sigmaY-envelope}]
Proposition~\ref{prop:var-contam} gives exactly
$\sigma_Y^2=R-\Cov_\nu(w,g)+B_{\mathrm{samp}}^2
 \le R+|\Cov_\nu(w,g)|+B_{\mathrm{samp}}^2$. For the covariance, apply
\eqref{eq:var-scale-decomp}, then $\sigma_{g^{\star}}\le\sigma_g$
(Lemma~\ref{lem:coarsening} applied to $g$) with
Lemma~\ref{lem:kurt}, and Lemma~\ref{lem:gperp} for the within-cell part:
\[
  |\Cov_\nu(w,g)|
  \le\sigma_{w^{\star}}\sqrt{\kappa-1}\,\sigma_Y^2
    +c_g\,\sigma_{w^{\perp}}\sigma_{Y^{\perp}}
  =\sigma_{w^{\star}}\sqrt{\kappa-1}\,\sigma_Y^2+c_g\varepsilon .
\]
Since $\sigma_{Y^{\star}}\le\sigma_Y$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:coarsening}),
$c_g\varepsilon\le2(\kappa^{\star}\kappa^{\perp})^{1/4}\varepsilon\,\sigma_Y
 +d\varepsilon$. For the squared bias,
Proposition~\ref{prop:scale-decomp} and the same inequality give
\[
  B_{\mathrm{samp}}^2
  \le\big(\sigma_{w^{\star}}\sigma_{Y^{\star}}+\varepsilon\big)^2
  \le\sigma_{w^{\star}}^2\sigma_Y^2
     +2\sigma_{w^{\star}}\varepsilon\,\sigma_Y+\varepsilon^2 .
\]
Summing and collecting powers of $\sigma_Y$,
\[
  (1-\Delta)\,\sigma_Y^2-2b\varepsilon\,\sigma_Y
  -\big(R+d\varepsilon+\varepsilon^2\big)\;\le\;0 .
\]
Under $\Delta<1$ the leading coefficient is positive, so the admissible
set is the interval between the roots and $\sigma_Y$ is at most the
larger one, which is \eqref{eq:envelope-eps}. Monotonicity and continuity
in $\varepsilon$ are immediate from the formula, and setting
$\varepsilon=0$ gives $\sqrt{(1-\Delta)R}/(1-\Delta)$.
\end{proof}%
}

Every quantity in \eqref{eq:envelope-eps} except $\varepsilon$ is
available without a field product: $R$ from the observed OHC,
$\sigma_{w^{\star}}$ from the count statistic
$\sigwhat$~\eqref{eq:sigmawK-hat}, $\sigma_{Y^{\perp}}$ from within-cell
float pairs \eqref{eq:sigmaYperp-pairs}, and
$\kappa,\kappa^{\star},\kappa^{\perp}$ chosen constants
(Remark~\ref{rem:kappa}). The residual budget $\varepsilon$ is not
estimable---that is the standing conclusion of
Section~\ref{sec:decomp-wy}---and so is never assumed small. It is
carried as an argument, and the bound is reported as a function of it.

\begin{remark}[When $\Delta\ge1$]
\label{rem:delta-fails}
The hypothesis $\Delta<1$ is not a technical convenience and cannot be
repaired. When $\Delta\ge1$ the inequality
$\sigma_Y^2\le R+\Delta\sigma_Y^2+\cdots$ is satisfied by arbitrarily
large $\sigma_Y$, and the failure is informational rather than
algebraic: at that level of sampling unevenness the second-moment
contamination could account for the entire observed dispersion, so the
spread of the float values is consistent with a quiet field sampled very
unevenly and with a variable field sampled evenly. No estimator can
separate the two from the profiles alone, and the only remedy is to
coarsen the partition until $\sigma_{w^{\star}}$ falls below the ceiling
of Lemma~\ref{lem:refinement-ceiling}. Because $\sigwhat$ is itself
estimated, the check should use the upper limit of its jackknife interval
rather than the point estimate, and the same $\kappa$ must be used in the
ceiling and in \eqref{eq:envelope-eps}: a large $\kappa$ chosen for
conservatism in the field factor cannot be paired with a small one chosen
to license a finer partition.
\end{remark}

\begin{corollary}[Field-product-free bias bound]
\label{cor:field-free}
Under the hypotheses of Proposition~\ref{prop:sigmaY-envelope}, for every
residual budget $\varepsilon$,
\begin{equation}
  |B_{\mathrm{samp}}|
  \;\le\;\mathcal{B}(\varepsilon)
  \;:=\;\sigma_{w^{\star}}\,\overline{\sigma}_Y(\varepsilon)
        +\varepsilon ,
  \label{eq:bias-eps}
\end{equation}
and $\mathcal{B}$ is continuous and strictly increasing in $\varepsilon$.
\end{corollary}
\deferproof{field-free}{%
\begin{proof}[Proof of Corollary~\ref{cor:field-free}]
Proposition~\ref{prop:scale-decomp} gives
$|B_{\mathrm{samp}}|\le\sigma_{w^{\star}}\sigma_{Y^{\star}}
 +\sigma_{w^{\perp}}\sigma_{Y^{\perp}}$. Replace $\sigma_{Y^{\star}}$ by
the larger $\sigma_Y\le\overline{\sigma}_Y(\varepsilon)$
(Lemma~\ref{lem:coarsening} and
Proposition~\ref{prop:sigmaY-envelope}) and the second term by
$\varepsilon$ by definition \eqref{eq:residual-budget}. Monotonicity is
inherited from $\overline{\sigma}_Y$.
\end{proof}%
}

\subsubsection*{Negligibility, made quantitative}
\label{sec:breakdown}

Since $\mathcal{B}$ is increasing and continuous, the claim ``the
residual is negligible'' can be replaced by a number.

\begin{definition}[Breakdown budget]
\label{def:breakdown}
Fix a benchmark $\mathcal{T}>0$ in the units of the estimand. The
\emph{breakdown budget} is
\[
  \varepsilon^{\star}(\mathcal{T})
  :=\sup\{\varepsilon\ge0:\ \mathcal{B}(\varepsilon)\le\mathcal{T}\},
\]
with $\varepsilon^{\star}=0$ if $\mathcal{B}(0)>\mathcal{T}$. The
reported conclusion is then not that $\varepsilon$ is small but that the
bound remains below $\mathcal{T}$ for every residual budget up to
$\varepsilon^{\star}(\mathcal{T})$.
\end{definition}

This converts an adjective into a falsifiable statement: a reader who
believes the sub-cell weight variation could exceed
$\varepsilon^{\star}/\sigma_{Y^{\perp}}$ has a concrete objection, and
one that can be argued from the float geometry. We report
$\mathcal{B}(\varepsilon)$ as a curve, together with
$\varepsilon^{\star}$ at two benchmarks: the OHC trend over the analysis
window, and the posterior standard deviation of the OHC estimate the
bound is meant to qualify. Both are in the same units as
$B_{\mathrm{samp}}$ and are the quantities against which a bias is
decision-relevant.

\begin{remark}[Why a prediction-error benchmark is used here and not earlier]
\label{rem:pred-benchmark}
It is tempting to declare terms negligible relative to a pointwise
prediction error, e.g.\ a kriging standard error for OHC. Two objections
rule this out as a criterion for \emph{dropping} terms. First, the units
differ: $B_{\mathrm{samp}}$ is an error in a regional integral, whereas
prediction error is pointwise and necessarily much larger, so such a
threshold would license discarding terms that are large relative to
$\mu$. Second, a prediction variance requires a fitted covariance model
with an amplitude, and importing an amplitude to certify a bound is
precisely what Remark~\ref{rem:conservative-Y} forbids. The legitimate
role of a prediction error is as an \emph{estimator}, not a threshold:
the pooled within-cell semivariance \eqref{eq:sigmaYperp-pairs} is
exactly the mean squared error of predicting $y$ at a point by its cell
mean, and it supplies $\sigma_{Y^{\perp}}$ in \eqref{eq:envelope-eps}.
The distinction is that discarding terms against a benchmark is invalid,
whereas carrying terms and comparing the resulting bound to a benchmark,
as in Definition~\ref{def:breakdown}, is not.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Closing the budget by declaring a total unevenness]
\label{rem:chi2-ceiling}
If a single number is wanted in place of a curve, $\varepsilon$ can be
eliminated at the cost of one explicit assumption. Since
$\sigma_{w^{\perp}}^2=\sigma_w^2-\sigma_{w^{\star}}^2$
(Lemma~\ref{lem:coarsening}), a declared ceiling
$\sigma_w^2=\chi^2(\pi\|\nu)\le\chi^2_{\max}$ on the \emph{total}
sampling unevenness gives
\[
  \varepsilon\;\le\;\sqrt{\big(\chi^2_{\max}-\sigma_{w^{\star}}^2\big)_+}
  \;\cdot\;\sigYperphat ,
\]
and \eqref{eq:bias-eps} evaluated there is a closed bound. We prefer the
curve as the primary report, since $\chi^2_{\max}$ is an assumption the
framework otherwise avoids; but it is an interpretable and criticizable
one---a statement about how non-uniform Argo sampling could possibly
be---and is stated as a variant for readers who want a single figure.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Interpreting and choosing the shape constants]
\label{rem:kappa}
Three dimensionless shape constants enter the envelope:
$\kappa$ through Lemma~\ref{lem:kurt}, and $\kappa^{\star}$,
$\kappa^{\perp}$ through Lemma~\ref{lem:gperp}. They are not
interchangeable and are discussed in turn.

\paragraph*{The leading constant.}
The constant $\kappa$ is the kurtosis of the \emph{one-point value
distribution} of the anomaly field: pool the values $y(x)-\mu$ over all
$x\in\X$ under the reference measure $\nu$ and take the standardized
fourth moment. It is not a temporal kurtosis of a series, nor a kurtosis
across ocean realizations, and by Lemma~\ref{lem:kurt} it is
forced---no summary of the field other than $\kappa$ enters the variance
term. Physically it measures \emph{intermittency}: how concentrated the
anomaly amplitude is across space--time. Rings and the meandering jet
core are compact, high-amplitude features on a quiescent background,
which fatten the tails and lift $\kappa$ above the Gaussian value $3$.

Two points guide a conservative choice. First, $\kappa$ is \emph{not}
monotone in ``frontal-ness.'' A front expressed as rare sharp excursions
on a background is leptokurtic ($\kappa>3$); a front that partitions the
box into two bulk water masses gives a \emph{bimodal}, platykurtic
distribution ($\kappa<3$, with floor $\kappa=1$). Which regime holds is
set by the de-trending of Section~\ref{sec:resolution}: removing the
standing planar gradient strips the two-mass partition and raises
$\kappa$; leaving it lowers $\kappa$. So $\kappa$ must be read from the
same anomaly field that enters the bound. Reference anchors are
exact---Gaussian $3$, Laplace $6$, exponential $9$---and vertically
integrated OHC tends to the milder leptokurtic end, the depth integral
smoothing the intermittency of instantaneous surface fields.
Conservative here means \emph{large}: a larger $\kappa$ shrinks the
factor $1-\Delta$ and widens the envelope, the only ceiling being
finiteness of \eqref{eq:envelope-eps}, which by
Lemma~\ref{lem:refinement-ceiling} is
\[
  \kappa\;<\;1+\Big(\frac{1-\sigma_{w^{\star}}^2}{\sigma_{w^{\star}}}\Big)^{2},
\]
the same condition as $\sigma_{w^{\star}}<\sigma_{\max}(\kappa)$ read in
the other direction. It lies far above any physical value when
$\sigma_{w^{\star}}$ is small, but it is not vacuous at fine partitions
(Remark~\ref{rem:delta-fails}).

Second, $\kappa$ is a far safer quantity to import than $\sigma_Y$
itself, for a structural reason: it is affine-invariant. Under any
rescaling $y\mapsto ay+b$ both $\E_\nu[(y-\mu)^4]$ and $\sigma_Y^4$ carry
a factor $a^4$ and cancel. The specific defect that made the reanalysis
unsafe as a source of $\sigma_Y$---assimilation \emph{attenuates}
anomaly amplitude (Remark~\ref{rem:conservative-Y}), the unsafe
direction---is a roughly multiplicative rescaling, to which $\kappa$ is
blind. A reanalysis, or a heavier-tailed physical prior, is therefore a
defensible source for the \emph{shape} constant $\kappa$ even though it
is not for the \emph{amplitude} $\sigma_Y$; the one caveat is that
assimilation may damp extremes more than the bulk, understating $\kappa$,
so a margin is added rather than taking a reanalysis value at face value.

\paragraph*{The two residual constants.}
The constants $\kappa^{\star}$ and $\kappa^{\perp}$ are the kurtoses of
the between- and within-cell components $y^{\star}=\PK(y-\mu)$ and
$y^{\perp}=\PKp(y-\mu)$ of the same field, evaluated on the operating
partition. Their role is strictly subordinate. They enter
\eqref{eq:envelope-eps} only through
$b=\sigma_{w^{\star}}+(\kappa^{\star}\kappa^{\perp})^{1/4}$ and
$d=\sigma_{Y^{\perp}}\sqrt{\kappa^{\perp}}$, both of which multiply the
residual budget $\varepsilon$; at $\varepsilon=0$ they leave the bound
entirely. In particular they do \emph{not} enter $\Delta$, so they do not
affect the refinement ceiling of Lemma~\ref{lem:refinement-ceiling}, and
the resolution trade-off of Remark~\ref{rem:kappa-resolution} is governed
by $\kappa$ alone. They also enter weakly: the fourth root means that
doubling either constant inflates $(\kappa^{\star}\kappa^{\perp})^{1/4}$
by only $2^{1/4}\approx1.19$, and less than that in $b$, which also
carries $\sigma_{w^{\star}}$.

No ordering may be assumed. Kurtosis is not monotone under orthogonal
projection, so neither $\kappa^{\star}\le\kappa$ nor
$\kappa^{\perp}\le\kappa$ holds in general---cell means that split into
two water masses are platykurtic while the field they came from need not
be---and the three constants must therefore be chosen jointly rather than
deduced from one another. Physically, $\kappa^{\star}$ is the kurtosis of
a smoothed field, and cell averaging pulls it toward the Gaussian value;
but at the operating resolution each cell holds roughly one decorrelation
volume, so little averaging has occurred and $\kappa^{\star}\approx\kappa$
is the natural default. The constant $\kappa^{\perp}$ is
resolution-dependent at both ends: in the coarse limit
$y^{\perp}\to y-\mu$ and $\kappa^{\perp}\to\kappa$, while below the
decorrelation length $y^{\perp}$ is approximately a local gradient times
a displacement, which within any single cell is \emph{platykurtic}.
Pooling across cells, however, makes $y^{\perp}$ a scale mixture whose
mixing variable is the local gradient magnitude, and scale mixtures
inflate kurtosis; it is thus the intermittency of the \emph{gradient}
field---sharp at the front, weak in the interior---rather than of the
field itself that sets $\kappa^{\perp}$.

Unlike $\kappa$, the constant $\kappa^{\perp}$ can be estimated directly
from the profiles, using the same within-cell float pairs that supply
$\sigYperphat$. Writing $D_{ii'}:=y'(Z_i)-y'(Z_{i'})$ for pairs falling
in a common cell of the operating partition,
\begin{equation}
  \hat\kappa^{\perp}
  \;=\;\frac{2\,\overline{D^4}}{\big(\overline{D^2}\big)^{2}}\;-\;3,
  \qquad
  \overline{D^{2}}\big/2=\sigYperphatsq ,
  \label{eq:kappaperp-pairs}
\end{equation}
where the bars denote averages over within-cell pairs. The identity
underlying \eqref{eq:kappaperp-pairs} is that for two independent draws
from a common law, $\E[D^4]=2\E[y^{\perp4}]+6\sigma^4$ and
$\E[D^2]=2\sigma^2$; the second equality in \eqref{eq:kappaperp-pairs}
shows it reuses exactly the statistic of \eqref{eq:sigmaYperp-pairs}, at
no additional data cost. Like every float-based quantity it targets the
design law rather than $\nu$, but it is a dimensionless shape and so
inherits the affine-invariance argument above; and since only an upper
bound is required, the estimate is padded rather than taken at face
value. Its variance is high---fourth moments of pair differences on a
thin sample---so it is used to check the plausibility of a chosen value
rather than to supply one.

Given the insensitivity noted above, we recommend collapsing the two to a
single conservative constant. Since
$(\kappa^{\star}\kappa^{\perp})^{1/4}\le\max(\kappa^{\star},\kappa^{\perp})^{1/2}$,
setting $\kappa^{\star}=\kappa^{\perp}=\kappa_{\mathrm{res}}$ with
$\kappa_{\mathrm{res}}$ a padded upper value---for instance the same
constant used for $\kappa$---is valid, costs little in the width of
$\mathcal{B}(\varepsilon)$, and keeps the number of declared constants at
two rather than three. The reported sensitivity analysis varies $\kappa$
and $\kappa_{\mathrm{res}}$ separately, since only the former interacts
with the choice of resolution.
\end{remark}

\paragraph{Estimation and uncertainty.} The design variance is estimated
by the ordinary empirical variance of the observed OHC,
\[
  \hat R=\frac1N\sum_{i=1}^{N}\big(y(x_i)-\hat\mu_\pi\big)^2 ,
  \qquad
  \hat\mu_\pi=\frac1N\sum_{i=1}^{N}y(x_i),
\]
which is consistent for $\Var_\pi(Y)$ under
Assumption~\ref{ass:sample-pi} ($\hat\mu_\pi$ estimates the design mean
$\mu_w$). Its float-clustered uncertainty is propagated by the same
delete-one-float jackknife~\eqref{eq:jackvar} used for $\sigwhat$
(Section~\ref{sec:uncertainty}), and the envelope
$\overline{\sigma}_Y$ inherits its interval by monotone propagation of
the jackknife bounds on $\hat R$ and $\sigwhat$ through
Proposition~\ref{prop:sigmaY-envelope} at the chosen $\kappa$. In the
reported bound at each resolution is
$\sigwhat\,\overline{\sigma}_Y$. Where an auxiliary field product is
available it may be used to sanity-check $\overline{\sigma}_Y$ and,
through Remark~\ref{rem:kappa}, the chosen $\kappa$---but as a
cross-check read in the knowledge that it attenuates, never as an input
to the guarantee.


\subsection{Deseasonalization as a control variate}
\label{sec:deseason}

The seasonal cycle does not bias the estimand---over an integer number of
years it integrates to zero against $\nu$---but it enters the bound
twice, and unfavourably. It inflates $\sigma_Y$, and hence every product
in Proposition~\ref{prop:scale-decomp}; and, being a smooth periodic
signal, its one-point distribution is strongly platykurtic, so leaving it
in the field depresses the kurtosis $\kappa$ of
Section~\ref{sec:sigmaY-bound} and makes the envelope
$\overline{\sigma}_Y$ appear tighter than the intermittency of the eddy
field warrants (Remark~\ref{rem:kappa}).

Removing it, however, appears to require a temporal partition---monthly
or seasonal bins---whose edges are arbitrary and whose occupancy is
uneven under preferential sampling. This subsection avoids any partition
of $\T$. We treat the seasonal cycle as a \emph{control variate}: a
known, low-dimensional offset subtracted from the field, whose own
sampling bias is not bounded but \emph{computed in closed form}, and
whose functional form is continuous in $t$.

\subsubsection{The control-variate identity}
\label{sec:cv-identity}

Recall the admissible offset $m$, residual field $y'=y-m$ and offset
aliasing $D=\E_\pi[m]-\E_\nu[m]$ of Definition~\ref{def:offset}. Only the
closed-form integrability of $\E_\nu[m]$ is restrictive; nothing requires
$m$ to be the true seasonal cycle, as Proposition~\ref{prop:cv-decomp}
makes precise.

\begin{proposition}[Control-variate decomposition of the bias]
\label{prop:cv-decomp}
For any admissible offset $m$,
\[
  B_{\mathrm{samp}}
  \;=\;\underbrace{D}_{\text{computable}}
  \;+\;\underbrace{\Cov_\nu\big(w(X),\,y'(X)\big)}_{\text{bounded}} .
\]
\end{proposition}
\deferproof{cv-decomp}{%
\begin{proof}[Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:cv-decomp}]
By Proposition~\ref{prop:cov-identity},
$B_{\mathrm{samp}}=\Cov_\nu(w,y)$, and covariance is linear in its second
argument, so $\Cov_\nu(w,y)=\Cov_\nu(w,m)+\Cov_\nu(w,y')$. For the first
term, $\E_\nu[w-1]=0$ gives
$\Cov_\nu(w,m)=\E_\nu[(w-1)m]=\E_\nu[wm]-\E_\nu[m]$, and the change of
measure $\dd\pi=w\dd\nu$ of Definition~\ref{def:w} gives
$\E_\nu[wm]=\E_\pi[m]$. Hence $\Cov_\nu(w,m)=\E_\pi[m]-\E_\nu[m]=D$.
\end{proof}%
}

The asymmetry that makes this useful is that $\E_\nu[m]$ is a known
number whereas $\E_\nu[y]=\mu$ is the estimand itself
(Definition~\ref{def:mu}); the same manipulation applied to $y$ would
require the answer. The offset is therefore not merely measured but
removed by construction.

\begin{definition}[Deseasonalized estimator]
\label{def:deseason-est}
Given an admissible offset $m$ and observed profiles $Z_1,\dots,Z_N$
(Definition~\ref{def:Zi}) with field values $y(Z_i)$, set
\begin{equation}
  \hat\mu_m
  \;:=\;\frac1N\sum_{i=1}^{N}\big(y(Z_i)-m(Z_i)\big)\;+\;\E_\nu[m].
  \label{eq:deseason-est}
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Exact cancellation of the offset aliasing]
\label{prop:cv-cancel}
Write $\hat\mu_{\mathrm{naive}}:=N^{-1}\sum_i y(Z_i)$ and
$\hat D:=N^{-1}\sum_i m(Z_i)-\E_\nu[m]$. Then, pathwise,
\begin{equation}
  \hat\mu_m=\hat\mu_{\mathrm{naive}}-\hat D ,
  \label{eq:cv-pathwise}
\end{equation}
and under Assumption~\ref{ass:sample-pi}, $\E[\hat D]=D$ and
\[
  \E\big[\hat\mu_m\big]-\mu \;=\; \Cov_\nu\big(w,y'\big).
\]
\end{proposition}
\deferproof{cv-cancel}{%
\begin{proof}[Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:cv-cancel}]
Splitting the sum in \eqref{eq:deseason-est} gives
$\hat\mu_m=N^{-1}\sum_iy(Z_i)-\big[N^{-1}\sum_im(Z_i)-\E_\nu[m]\big]$,
which is \eqref{eq:cv-pathwise}; this is an algebraic identity in the
realized sample, the same numbers $m(Z_i)$ appearing in both
expressions. Under Assumption~\ref{ass:sample-pi} each $Z_i\sim\pi$ and
$m$ is a fixed function, so $\E[N^{-1}\sum_im(Z_i)]=\E_\pi[m]$ and
$\E[\hat D]=\E_\pi[m]-\E_\nu[m]=D$; likewise
$\E[\hat\mu_{\mathrm{naive}}]=\E_\pi[y]=\mu_w$
(Definition~\ref{def:mu-w}). Therefore
\[
  \E[\hat\mu_m]-\mu=(\mu_w-\mu)-D=B_{\mathrm{samp}}-D=\Cov_\nu(w,y')
\]
by Definition~\ref{def:bias} and Proposition~\ref{prop:cv-decomp}.
\end{proof}%
}

\begin{remark}[Why the offset must be added back under $\nu$]
\label{rem:cv-asymmetry}
Estimator \eqref{eq:deseason-est} subtracts the offset \emph{as the
floats sampled it} and restores it \emph{as the domain contains it}. The
two operations are deliberately taken under different measures, and
their mismatch $-\hat D$ is precisely the correction. Restoring
$\E_\pi[m]$ instead would return $\hat\mu_m=\hat\mu_{\mathrm{naive}}$
identically: subtracting a fitted seasonal cycle and averaging the
residuals, without the $\E_\nu[m]$ offset, is that null operation up to
an additive constant.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Validity does not depend on $m$ being correct]
\label{rem:cv-robust}
Proposition~\ref{prop:cv-decomp} used no property of $m$ beyond
Definition~\ref{def:offset}. A misspecified offset leaves a larger
residual field but cannot invalidate the decomposition, since its own
aliasing $D$ is cancelled whatever it is. This is the structural
difference from importing $\sigma_Y$ from an external product
(Remark~\ref{rem:conservative-Y}), where an attenuated value silently
breaks the inequality: here an inaccurate import costs efficiency only.
Moreover $\hat D$'s own error is ordinary Monte Carlo error of a
\emph{known} function, of order
$\mathrm{sd}_\pi(m)/\sqrt{N_{\mathrm{eff}}}$, and so contributes
variance---estimable from the data---rather than bias.
\end{remark}

\begin{corollary}[Bound for the deseasonalized estimand]
\label{cor:deseason-bound}
Let $\sigma_{Y'}^2:=\Var_\nu(y')$ with scale components
$\sigma_{Y'^{\star}},\sigma_{Y'^{\perp}}$ defined through
\eqref{eq:y-split} applied to $y'$. Then
\[
  \big|\E[\hat\mu_m]-\mu\big|
  \;\le\;\sigma_{w^{\star}}\sigma_{Y'^{\star}}
        +\sigma_{w^{\perp}}\sigma_{Y'^{\perp}}
  \;\le\;\sigma_w\,\sigma_{Y'} ,
\]
and, if $y'\in L^4(\nu)$ with residual kurtosis
$\kappa':=\E_\nu[(y'-\E_\nu y')^4]/\sigma_{Y'}^4$ and
$\Delta':=\sigma_{w^{\star}}\sqrt{\kappa'-1}+\sigma_{w^{\star}}^2<1$,
\[
  \big|\E[\hat\mu_m]-\mu\big|
  \;\le\;\sigma_{w^{\star}}\,\overline{\sigma}_{Y'}(\varepsilon')
        +\varepsilon' ,
\]
where $R':=\Var_\pi(Y')$ is the design variance of the residual field,
$\varepsilon':=\sigma_{w^{\perp}}\sigma_{Y'^{\perp}}$ is its residual
budget \eqref{eq:residual-budget}, and
$\overline{\sigma}_{Y'}(\cdot)$ is the envelope
\eqref{eq:envelope-eps} formed from $R'$ and the residual-field
kurtoses. Negligibility of $\varepsilon'$ is not assumed; it is reported
through the breakdown budget of Definition~\ref{def:breakdown}.
\end{corollary}
\deferproof{deseason-bound}{%
\begin{proof}[Proof of Corollary~\ref{cor:deseason-bound}]
By Proposition~\ref{prop:cv-cancel} the bias of $\hat\mu_m$ is
$\Cov_\nu(w,y')$, a preferential-sampling bias in the sense of
Proposition~\ref{prop:cov-identity} carried by the field $y'$. Since
Propositions~\ref{prop:l2-bound} and~\ref{prop:scale-decomp} hold for any
$L^2(\nu)$ field, they apply verbatim with $y'$ in place of $y$. The
second display applies Proposition~\ref{prop:sigmaY-envelope} and
Corollary~\ref{cor:field-free} to $y'$, whose hypotheses hold by
assumption.
\end{proof}%
}

\begin{lemma}[Variance reduction]
\label{lem:cv-variance}
$\sigma_{Y'}^2=\sigma_Y^2-2\Cov_\nu(y,m)+\Var_\nu(m)$. In particular
$\sigma_{Y'}\le\sigma_Y+\sigma_m$, and if $m$ is replaced by its
optimally scaled version $\zeta^{\star}m$ with
$\zeta^{\star}=\Cov_\nu(y,m)/\Var_\nu(m)$, then
\[
  \sigma_{Y'}^2=\sigma_Y^2\big(1-\rho^2\big),
  \qquad \rho:=\mathrm{Corr}_\nu(y,m).
\]
\end{lemma}
\deferproof{cv-variance}{%
\begin{proof}[Proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:cv-variance}]
The first identity is bilinearity of $\Var_\nu$; the bound is the
triangle inequality in $L^2(\nu)$. Substituting $\zeta^{\star}m$ gives
$\sigma_Y^2-2\zeta^{\star}\Cov_\nu(y,m)+(\zeta^{\star})^2\Var_\nu(m)
 =\sigma_Y^2-\Cov_\nu(y,m)^2/\Var_\nu(m)$, which is
$\sigma_Y^2(1-\rho^2)$.
\end{proof}%
}

Thus the tightening of the bound is by the factor $\sqrt{1-\rho^2}$,
governed entirely by the fraction of field variance the offset explains,
while a poor offset costs at most the controlled amount of
Lemma~\ref{lem:cv-variance} and no validity at all.

\subsubsection{A harmonic offset with closed-form reference integral}
\label{sec:harmonic}

It remains to exhibit a class of offsets rich enough to absorb the
seasonal cycle and simple enough that $\E_\nu[m]$ is exact. Two
structural facts do the work: $\nu$ factorizes, and the basis is chosen
orthogonal under the factors.

\begin{lemma}[Product form of the reference measure]
\label{lem:nu-product}
Let $\lambda$ denote longitude and $\phi$ latitude, and set
$u:=\sin\phi$. On $A=[\lambda_0,\lambda_1]\times[\phi_0,\phi_1]$ the
surface element is $\dd\sigma=R_{\oplus}^2\dd\lambda\dd u$ with $R_{\oplus}$ the
Earth radius, so the normalized area measure $\alpha$ is
\emph{uniform} on the rectangle
$[\lambda_0,\lambda_1]\times[u_0,u_1]$, $u_i=\sin\phi_i$. With $\tau$ the
time measure of Definition~\ref{def:nu}---uniform or not---the reference
measure is by construction the product $\nu=\alpha\otimes\tau$, whence
$\E_\nu[f\cdot g]=\E_\alpha[f]\,\E_\tau[g]$ for $f\in L^2(\alpha)$
depending on $s$ and $g\in L^2(\tau)$ depending on $t$.
\end{lemma}
\deferproof{nu-product}{%
\begin{proof}[Proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:nu-product}]
In spherical coordinates $\dd\sigma=R_{\oplus}^2\cos\phi\dd\lambda\dd\phi$;
substituting $u=\sin\phi$, $\dd u=\cos\phi\dd\phi$, gives
$\dd\sigma=R_{\oplus}^2\dd\lambda\dd u$, constant density on the
$(\lambda,u)$-rectangle, so $\alpha(E)=|E|/|A|$ is uniform there and
$|A|=R_{\oplus}^2(\lambda_1-\lambda_0)(u_1-u_0)$. Definition~\ref{def:nu}
sets $\nu=\alpha\otimes\tau$ directly, so the factorization of
expectations is Fubini's theorem; no uniformity of $\tau$ is used.
\end{proof}%
}

The temporal factor of $\nu$ is a modelling choice, and one worth making
deliberately. If $\tau$ is uniform and the window does not span a whole
number of years, some seasonal phases are visited more often than others,
and the estimand $\mu$ then contains a genuine seasonal component: a
window running from February to July weights summers more heavily than
winters. This is not an error, but it makes $\mu$ depend on the accident
of the window's endpoints and is not comparable across regions analysed
over different periods. The following choice removes it exactly.

\begin{definition}[Seasonally balanced time measure]
\label{def:tau-balanced}
Write $\varphi(t):=t\bmod P\in[0,P)$ for the \emph{seasonal phase} of
$t$, and for $\varphi\in[0,P)$ let
\[
  M(\varphi):=\#\{\,j\text{ an integer}:\ \varphi+jP\in\T\,\}
\]
be the number of times the window visits phase $\varphi$. The
\emph{seasonally balanced} time measure is the probability measure $\tau$
on $\T$ with Lebesgue density
\begin{equation}
  f_\tau(t)\;=\;\frac{1}{P\,M(\varphi(t))} .
  \label{eq:tau-balanced}
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Balancing]
\label{lem:tau-balanced}
Let $n:=\lfloor|\T|/P\rfloor$. The measure of
Definition~\ref{def:tau-balanced} satisfies:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $M$ takes only the values $n$ and $n+1$, so $f_\tau$ is
  two-valued; it is constant, $f_\tau\equiv1/|\T|$, exactly when
  $|\T|/P$ is a positive integer.
\item $f_\tau$ is a probability density on $\T$.
\item The push-forward of $\tau$ under the phase map $\varphi$ is
  uniform on $[0,P)$.
\item $\E_\tau\big[e^{i\omega_k t}\big]=0$ for every non-zero integer
  $k$; consequently
  $\E_\tau[\cos\omega_k\tilde t]=\E_\tau[\sin\omega_k\tilde t]=0$ for any
  centring $\tilde t=t-\bar t$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}
\deferproof{tau-balanced}{%
\begin{proof}[Proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:tau-balanced}]
(1) The points $\varphi+jP$ form a lattice of spacing $P$, and the number
of lattice points in an interval of length $|\T|$ is either
$\lfloor|\T|/P\rfloor$ or $\lfloor|\T|/P\rfloor+1$. If $|\T|=nP$ the
count is $n$ for every $\varphi$ up to a Lebesgue-null set of endpoints,
giving $f_\tau=1/(nP)=1/|\T|$.

(2) Decomposing $\T$ by phase and summing over the visits to each phase,
\[
  \int_\T f_\tau\dd t
  =\int_0^{P}\sum_{j:\,\varphi+jP\in\T}\frac{\dd\varphi}{P\,M(\varphi)}
  =\int_0^{P}\frac{M(\varphi)}{P\,M(\varphi)}\dd\varphi=1 .
\]

(3) The same computation restricted to a Borel set $B\subseteq[0,P)$
gives $\tau(\varphi^{-1}(B))=|B|/P$, the uniform law.

(4) Since $\omega_kP=2\pi k$, the map $t\mapsto e^{i\omega_kt}$ factors
through the phase, $e^{i\omega_kt}=e^{i\omega_k\varphi(t)}$. By (3),
$\E_\tau[e^{i\omega_kt}]=P^{-1}\int_0^{P}e^{i\omega_k\varphi}\dd\varphi=0$
for $k\ne0$. Centring multiplies this by the constant
$e^{-i\omega_k\bar t}$, so it too vanishes; taking real and imaginary
parts gives the cosine and sine statements.
\end{proof}%
}

\begin{remark}[What balancing costs and what it buys]
\label{rem:tau-cost}
Operationally \eqref{eq:tau-balanced} down-weights the fractional excess
of the window from $1/n$ to $1/(n+1)$: a two-valued weight, and nothing
more. Three consequences are worth recording. First, the estimand becomes
the \emph{seasonally balanced} mean OHC, which is comparable across
windows and regions and no longer moves when the endpoints are nudged.
Second, equal-measure cells (Definition~\ref{def:partition}) now have
equal $\tau$-measure rather than equal duration, so time-bin edges are
placed at $\tau$-quantiles; the count estimator \eqref{eq:sigmawK-hat} is
otherwise untouched, since it only ever required $\nu(C_j)=1/K$. Third,
$w=\dd\pi/\dd\nu$ now measures departure from seasonally balanced uniform
sampling, which is the more meaningful null: floats are no longer charged
for unevenness that is an artefact of where the calendar was cut. The
cost is a modest loss of effective sample size, in the ratio $(n+1)/n$,
from the reweighting. The alternative---truncating to the largest
integer-period sub-window---is conceptually cheaper but discards up to a
full year of profiles, which is expensive here because
Lemma~\ref{lem:refinement-ceiling} already binds through
$\sigma_{w^{\star}}$ and both $N$ and $F$ enter that constraint.
\end{remark}

Write $|\T|$ for the window length, $P=1\,\mathrm{yr}$ for the seasonal
period, $\omega_k:=2\pi k/P$, and $\tilde t:=t-\bar t$ for time centred
at the reference mean time $\bar t:=\E_\tau[t]$, which is the window
midpoint when $\tau$ is uniform. Let $\psi_1$ denote the
shifted Legendre polynomial of degree one, normalized on the relevant
interval so that $\E_\alpha[\psi_1]=0$ and $\E_\alpha[\psi_1^2]=1$.

\begin{definition}[Harmonic offset class]
\label{def:harmonic}
Let $\mathcal M_K$ be the linear span of the separable functions
$\Phi_j(\lambda,u)\,G_k(\tilde t)$ with spatial factors
$\Phi_0\equiv1$, $\Phi_1=\psi_1(\lambda)$, $\Phi_2=\psi_1(u)$, and
temporal factors
\[
  G_0\equiv1,\quad G_1=\tilde t,\quad
  G_{2k}=\cos\omega_k\tilde t,\quad
  G_{2k+1}=\sin\omega_k\tilde t,\qquad k=1,\dots,K .
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Exact reference integral]
\label{lem:exact-integral}
Let $m=\sum_{j,k}c_{jk}\Phi_jG_k\in\mathcal M_K$. Then
\begin{equation}
  \E_\nu[m]\;=\;c_{00}\;+\;\sum_{k=1}^{K}c_{0,2k}\,
  \mathrm{sinc}\!\Big(\frac{\pi k|\T|}{P}\Big),
  \qquad \mathrm{sinc}(z):=\frac{\sin z}{z}.
  \label{eq:exact-integral}
\end{equation}
In particular, if the window spans an integer number of periods,
$|\T|/P$ is a positive integer, then $\E_\nu[m]=c_{00}$.
\end{lemma}
\deferproof{exact-integral}{%
\begin{proof}[Proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:exact-integral}]
By Lemma~\ref{lem:nu-product},
$\E_\nu[\Phi_jG_k]=\E_\alpha[\Phi_j]\,\E_\tau[G_k]$. By construction
$\E_\alpha[\Phi_j]=0$ for $j\in\{1,2\}$ and $\E_\alpha[\Phi_0]=1$, so
every term with $j\ge1$ vanishes irrespective of its temporal partner.
For the surviving $j=0$ column, $\tau$ is uniform on an interval
symmetric about $\tilde t=0$, so $\E_\tau[\tilde t]=0$ and
$\E_\tau[\sin\omega_k\tilde t]=0$ by oddness, while
\[
  \E_\tau\big[\cos\omega_k\tilde t\big]
  =\frac{1}{|\T|}\int_{-|\T|/2}^{|\T|/2}\cos(\omega_k\tilde t)\dd\tilde t
  =\frac{2\sin(\omega_k|\T|/2)}{\omega_k|\T|}
  =\mathrm{sinc}\!\Big(\frac{\pi k|\T|}{P}\Big),
\]
using $\omega_k|\T|/2=\pi k|\T|/P$. Summing gives
\eqref{eq:exact-integral}; the sinc vanishes exactly when
$k|\T|/P$ is an integer, which holds for all $k$ when $|\T|/P$ is an
integer.
\end{proof}%
}

\begin{remark}[Spectral leakage of the observing window]
\label{rem:leakage-window}
The factor $\mathrm{sinc}(\pi k|\T|/P)$ is the leakage kernel of the
window: it is exactly zero on integer periods and otherwise bounded by
$P/(\pi k|\T|)$, decaying as $|\T|^{-1}$. The familiar prescription that
seasonal signals ``average out over whole years'' is thus exact rather
than approximate, and for a ragged window the residue is a known
constant, not an approximation error. For the analysis window of
Section~\ref{sec:resolution}, $|\T|/P=3$ and $\E_\nu[m]=c_{00}$: the
exact space--time integral of the offset is the fitted intercept, read
directly off the design, with no numerical integration performed. If a
non-uniform $\tau$ is preferred, nothing breaks: $\E_\tau[G_{2k}]$ and
$\E_\tau[G_{2k+1}]$ are the real and imaginary parts of the
characteristic function $\varphi_\tau(\omega_k)$.
\end{remark}

Only $K=2$ (annual and semiannual) is retained. Higher harmonics carry
little power in a vertically integrated quantity, and each costs two
parameters against the $F$ floats that constitute the effective sample
size for uncertainty propagation (Section~\ref{sec:uncertainty}).

\subsubsection{Estimation from the Roemmich--Gilson climatology}
\label{sec:rg}

The offset must be fixed. We therefore take the seasonal coefficients
from the Roemmich--Gilson (RG) Argo-only climatology, which supplies
monthly mean fields on a $1^\circ\times1^\circ$ grid, rather than
estimating them in-window.

\begin{assumption}[Offset independence]
\label{ass:rg-indep}
The climatology used to construct $m$ is built from profiles collected
strictly before the analysis window, namely the RG monthly fields for
$2004$--$2019$. Consequently $m$ is non-random with respect to
$Z_1,\dots,Z_N$ and Definition~\ref{def:offset} is satisfied exactly.
\end{assumption}

Assumption~\ref{ass:rg-indep} is not a technicality. RG is itself an
Argo product, so a climatology overlapping $2020$--$2022$ would share
profiles with the analysis sample, making $m$ data-dependent and
correlating $\hat D$ with $y'$; restricting to $2004$--$2019$ removes
the overlap at no cost, sixteen years being ample to identify two
harmonics.

\paragraph{Field construction.} Extract RG temperature and salinity over
$A$ and integrate to OHC over exactly the depth range, equation of state
and reference convention used to build the observed profile values, so
that $m$ and $y$ are commensurate; any residual offset in convention is
absorbed by $c_{00}$ and is harmless. Averaging by calendar month over
$2004$--$2019$ yields twelve fields $\bar m_r(\lambda_q,\phi_q)$,
$r=1,\dots,12$, on the grid cells $q$ of the box.

\paragraph{Projection.} The RG fields are then projected onto
$\mathcal M_2$ by weighted least squares over the $12\,n_{\mathrm{cell}}$ grid--month
values. Two corrections are required.

\begin{lemma}[Monthly bin-average attenuation]
\label{lem:bin-average}
Let $\Delta=P/12$ and let $\tilde t_r$ be the midpoint of calendar month
$r$. A monthly mean of a continuous-time harmonic satisfies
\[
  \frac1\Delta\int_{\tilde t_r-\Delta/2}^{\tilde t_r+\Delta/2}
    \cos(\omega_k\tilde t)\dd\tilde t
  =\cos(\omega_k\tilde t_r)\,
   \mathrm{sinc}\!\Big(\frac{\pi k}{12}\Big),
\]
and identically for $\sin$. Hence regressing monthly means on
midpoint-evaluated harmonics understates the continuous-time amplitudes
by the factors $\mathrm{sinc}(\pi k/12)$, equal to $0.9886$ for $k=1$ and
$0.9549$ for $k=2$.
\end{lemma}
\deferproof{bin-average}{%
\begin{proof}[Proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:bin-average}]
$\frac1\Delta\int_{\tilde t_r-\Delta/2}^{\tilde t_r+\Delta/2}
 e^{i\omega_k\tilde t}\dd\tilde t
 =e^{i\omega_k\tilde t_r}\cdot\frac1\Delta\int_{-\Delta/2}^{\Delta/2}
 e^{i\omega_k v}\dd v
 =e^{i\omega_k\tilde t_r}\,\mathrm{sinc}(\omega_k\Delta/2)$, and
$\omega_k\Delta/2=\pi k/12$. Taking real and imaginary parts gives the
two statements; the sinc factor is real, so it scales amplitude without
shifting phase.
\end{proof}%
}

Accordingly the harmonic amplitudes are either fitted against
bin-averaged basis functions directly, or fitted against
midpoint-evaluated ones and divided by $\mathrm{sinc}(\pi k/12)$. Either
route recovers \emph{continuous-time} coefficients
$(\alpha^{\mathrm{RG}}_k,\beta^{\mathrm{RG}}_k)$, and this is the step at
which the monthly partition of the climatology is deconvolved away: the
resulting offset is an analytic function of $t$, evaluated at each
profile's exact timestamp, with no bin edges and no interpolation
anywhere in the framework.

Second, the regression must be weighted by $\cos\phi_q$. The grid is
uniform in $\phi$, whereas $\alpha$ is uniform in $u=\sin\phi$
(Lemma~\ref{lem:nu-product}); an unweighted fit would target a measure
other than the one under which \eqref{eq:exact-integral} was derived.
Equivalently, one evaluates the basis at cell centres in $(\lambda,u)$
and weights by cell area.

\begin{remark}[A piecewise-constant alternative]
\label{rem:lookup}
One may instead use RG directly as a lookup table,
$m(x)=\bar m_{r(t)}(q(s))$, whose reference integral is the exact finite
sum $\sum_{q,r}\bar m_{qr}\,\alpha(q)\,\tau(r)$. This is admissible under
Definition~\ref{def:offset} and the entire framework applies. It is
nonetheless inferior: it reinstates the temporal partition this
subsection exists to avoid, carries $12\,n_{\mathrm{cell}}$ free values so that the
climatology's own sampling noise propagates into $y'$, and its
discontinuities complicate the interpretation of $\kappa'$. The
low-dimensional projection is preferred.
\end{remark}

\paragraph{The hybrid offset.} We freeze only the seasonal block and fit
the remaining terms in-window:
\begin{equation}
  m(x)=
  \underbrace{c_{00}+\gamma\tilde t+a_1\psi_1(\lambda)+a_2\psi_1(u)}
    _{\text{fitted on the analysis window}}
  +\underbrace{\sum_{k=1}^{2}
    \big[\alpha^{\mathrm{RG}}_k\cos\omega_k\tilde t
        +\beta^{\mathrm{RG}}_k\sin\omega_k\tilde t\big]}
    _{\text{frozen from RG, Assumption~\ref{ass:rg-indep}}} .
  \label{eq:hybrid-offset}
\end{equation}
The seasonal \emph{shape} is what sixteen years of climatology identify
well and what three years identify poorly; the mean level and trend over
$2020$--$2022$, by contrast, are properties of the analysis window that
a climatology has no standing to assert, and importing them would be a
substantive assumption about the period rather than a benign one.
Freezing the harmonics also removes the collinearity between $\tilde t$
and $\cos\omega_1\tilde t$ that uneven seasonal sampling would otherwise
induce. Note that the standing-gradient terms $a_1\psi_1(\lambda)
+a_2\psi_1(u)$ perform, within this single regression, the planar
de-trending applied per snapshot in Section~\ref{sec:resolution}, making
explicit the choice that Remark~\ref{rem:kappa} identifies as
determining whether $\kappa'$ falls in the leptokurtic or the bimodal
regime.

\begin{remark}[What the climatology may and may not supply]
\label{rem:rg-scope}
RG enters only through $m$. It must \emph{not} be used to supply
$\sigma_{Y}$, $\sigma_{Y^{\perp}}$ or $\kappa'$: as an objectively
analysed product on a $1^\circ$ grid---at or coarser than the relevance
scale of \eqref{eq:verdict}---it cannot represent mesoscale rings and
systematically attenuates anomaly amplitude, which is the unsafe
direction of Remark~\ref{rem:conservative-Y}. In the role of an offset
that same attenuation is harmless: by Remark~\ref{rem:cv-robust} it
reduces $\rho$ in Lemma~\ref{lem:cv-variance} and nothing else. This
asymmetry---an external product may set the \emph{location} of a control
variate but not the \emph{amplitude} of a bound---is the reason the
control-variate framing is preferred to direct field imports. Its
corollary is that $\rho^2$ should be expected to be modest for
full-depth OHC in a ring-dominated box, RG capturing the large-scale
seasonal signal and leaving the entire mesoscale in $y'$ by
construction.
\end{remark}

\paragraph{Estimation and uncertainty.} With the frozen harmonics
inserted, the four remaining coefficients of \eqref{eq:hybrid-offset} are
obtained by ordinary least squares of $y(Z_i)$ on
$(1,\tilde t,\psi_1(\lambda),\psi_1(u))$ at the profile locations,
deliberately unweighted: weighting by $1/\hat w$ would reintroduce the
object the framework avoids modelling, and buys nothing, since by
Remark~\ref{rem:cv-robust} validity does not require the
$\nu$-optimal projection. Because the design contains an intercept, the
residuals satisfy $N^{-1}\sum_i y'(Z_i)=0$, so by
\eqref{eq:deseason-est} and Lemma~\ref{lem:exact-integral} the estimator
reduces on an integer-period window to
\begin{equation}
  \hat\mu_m=\E_\nu[\hat m]=\hat c_{00} ,
  \label{eq:deseason-collapse}
\end{equation}
the fitted intercept. The framework thus contains the ordinary
regression estimator as a special case, and identifies its residual
error as $\Cov_\nu(w,y')$: the sampling bias of everything the
regression failed to capture, which Corollary~\ref{cor:deseason-bound}
bounds.

Uncertainty is propagated by the delete-one-float jackknife
\eqref{eq:jackvar} of Section~\ref{sec:uncertainty}, refitting the four
in-window coefficients within each replicate and re-deriving $\hat R'$,
$\hat\kappa'$ and $\hat c_{00}$; fitting once and resampling only the
residuals would understate uncertainty by treating a fitted object as
known. With four estimated parameters against $F$ floats the refit is
stable. Under Assumption~\ref{ass:rg-indep} the frozen block contributes
no estimation error.

\paragraph{Diagnostics.} We report: $\hat\rho^2=1-\hat R'/\hat R$, the
variance explained, which by Lemma~\ref{lem:cv-variance} is the
tightening factor of the bound; $\hat
D=\hat\mu_{\mathrm{naive}}-\hat\mu_m$ with its standard error
$\widehat{\mathrm{sd}}_\pi(m)/\sqrt{N_{\mathrm{eff}}}$, the quantity of
seasonal aliasing removed; and $\hat\kappa$ against $\hat\kappa'$, where
the raw value is expected to be depressed---a pure sinusoid has
kurtosis $3/2$---and the residual value to exceed the Gaussian $3$, so
that deseasonalization correctly \emph{widens} the envelope
$\overline{\sigma}_{Y'}$ while narrowing $\sigma_{Y'}$. As a check on
Assumption~\ref{ass:rg-indep} we additionally fit the harmonics
in-window and compare amplitude
$\{(\alpha_k^2+\beta_k^2)^{1/2}\}$ and phase
$\{\arctan(\beta_k/\alpha_k)\}$ against the RG values; a large phase
discrepancy indicates either an anomalous season or severe temporal
aliasing of the sampling, which the two corresponding values of $\hat D$
distinguish.


\subsection{Choosing the resolution}
\label{sec:resolution}


The partition $\mathcal{P}_K$ is the only tuning choice in
\eqref{eq:sigmawK-hat}, and it is not free. By
Lemma~\ref{lem:coarsening} the resolved dispersion $\sigma_{w^{\star}}$
is genuinely resolution-dependent, and in a particular direction: it
increases monotonically under refinement, toward $\sigma_w$ and without
plateau. The cell scale cannot therefore be chosen by refining until the
estimate stabilises, and must be fixed externally.

Two consequences shape everything below. First, the coarse estimator is
\emph{conservative in the wrong direction} for a naive bound: since
$\sigma_{w^{\star}}\le\sigma_w$, the product $\sigma_{w^{\star}}\sigma_Y$
is \emph{not} a valid upper bound on $|B_{\mathrm{samp}}|$---it may
understate it. This is precisely why the bound must be taken in its
scale-decomposed form,
$\sigma_{w^{\star}}\sigma_{Y^{\star}}+\sigma_{w^{\perp}}\sigma_{Y^{\perp}}$
(Proposition~\ref{prop:scale-decomp}), which pairs the coarse weight
dispersion only with the coarse field dispersion and accounts separately
for what lies below the cell scale.

Second, $K$ cannot be chosen by refining until the estimate stabilizes.
Lemma~\ref{lem:coarsening} says the refinement curve
$K\mapsto\sigma_{w^{\star}}$ climbs monotonically toward $\sigma_w$; and
because Argo trajectories cluster at short range, the empirical curve
does not plateau within any resolution the data support---it simply
continues to rise, at fine scales driven by trajectory autocorrelation
and count noise rather than by preferential sampling
(Section~\ref{sec:resolution}, ``Why the estimator degrades when
$N/K<1$''). There is no resolution at which the data reveal ``the true
$\sigma_w$''. The cell scale must therefore be fixed \emph{externally},
and the remainder of this section does so by bracketing it between two
limits: a \emph{relevance} scale from above, below which resolving $w$ is
unnecessary, and a \emph{sampling} scale from below, beyond which
resolving $w$ is infeasible. Three scales are in play and must not be
conflated.

The lemma applies to the field as well, but with a decisive difference in
consequence. For the weight, $\sigma_{w^{\star}}\le\sigma_w$ is a genuine
obstruction: the missing piece $\sigma_{w^{\perp}}$ cannot be recovered
from a sparse point pattern, and the refinement curve does not plateau,
so $\sigma_w$ is not identified from the data. For the field, by
contrast, the situation is benign: $\sigma_{Y'}$ is bounded above by the
envelope of Proposition~\ref{prop:sigmaY-envelope}, and
$\sigma_{Y'^{\star}}\le\sigma_{Y'}$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:coarsening}, so the
leading term is controlled without identifying the split
$\sigma_{Y'}^2=\sigma_{Y'^{\star}}^2+\sigma_{Y'^{\perp}}^2$ exactly. Of the four factors in the scale-decomposed bound of
Proposition~\ref{prop:scale-decomp}, exactly one---$\sigma_{w^{\perp}}$---is
unavailable, and it is paired with one---$\sigma_{Y^{\perp}}$---that is
both measurable and, by Lemma~\ref{lem:coarsening}, monotonically
decreasing under refinement. This pairing is what makes the bound usable:
refining the partition drives $\sigma_{Y^{\perp}}\downarrow 0$ and with it
the unestimated product, at the cost of driving $N/K$ toward the
coincidence-starved regime in which $\sigma_{w^{\star}}$ itself becomes
unestimable. The two monotonicities run in opposite directions along the
same refinement axis, and the operating band is where both remain
tolerable.

\subsubsection*{The rendering scale}

A gridded field product, where one is consulted, is delivered at
$1/12^\circ$ (${\approx}7$\,km here). This is the resolution at which such
a product carries information about \emph{scale}, and it is the grid on
which the empirical variogram of the relevance scale below is computed.
It says nothing about the resolution the float pattern can support, and
it is not a candidate cell scale for $\sigma_{w^{\star}}$.

By Remark~\ref{rem:conservative-Y} the product is never read for an
amplitude. Its two admissible uses are the variogram \emph{range}, which
fixes a partition and is invariant to the multiplicative attenuation that
makes the sill untrustworthy, and the dimensionless \emph{ratio}
\eqref{eq:residual-diagnostic}, in which a common attenuation factor
cancels between numerator and denominator. The residual factor itself is
estimated from the profiles, by pooling within-cell float pairs on the
operating partition:
\begin{equation}
  \sigYperphatsq
  \;=\;\frac{\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{K}
      \sum_{\{i<i'\,:\,Z_i,Z_{i'}\in C_j\}}
      \tfrac12\big(y'(Z_i)-y'(Z_{i'})\big)^2}
     {\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{K}\binom{n_j}{2}} ,
  \label{eq:sigmaYperp-pairs}
\end{equation}
the pooled within-cell semivariance. This targets the mean within-cell
dispersion under $\pi$ rather than $\nu$, and so inherits the
contamination of Proposition~\ref{prop:float-contam} at the sub-cell
scale---but only to the extent that $w$ varies within cells, which is
exactly the quantity $\sigma_{w^{\perp}}$ whose smallness the operating
resolution is chosen to enforce. The estimator is therefore
self-consistently valid in precisely the regime in which the term it
measures matters. Its limitation is data volume rather than bias: cells
holding fewer than two profiles contribute no pairs, so
\eqref{eq:sigmaYperp-pairs} degrades at the fine end of the refinement
band, and the number of contributing pairs is reported alongside it.

\subsubsection*{The relevance scale: how finely $w$ needs to be resolved}

By Proposition~\ref{prop:scale-decomp}, the one factor we decline to
estimate, $\sigma_{w^{\perp}}$, enters the bound only through the product
$\sigma_{w^{\perp}}\sigma_{Y^{\perp}}$. Sub-cell sampling structure is
therefore bias-irrelevant to the extent that the field is constant within
cells: choosing cells no larger than the OHC decorrelation length makes
$\sigma_{Y^{\perp}}$ small and suppresses the unestimated term regardless
of how rough the sub-cell sampling turns out to be. The relevance scale
is thus the OHC decorrelation length, and we fix it empirically from a
variogram rather than by inspection.

\paragraph{Procedure.} Working throughout on the deseasonalized field
$y'$ of Definition~\ref{def:offset}, we proceed as follows.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Field construction.} Compute OHC on the native
  $1/12^\circ$ grid over the analysis window and subtract the harmonic
  offset of Section~\ref{sec:deseason}. Its standing-gradient terms
  subsume the per-snapshot planar de-trending that the box would
  otherwise require: spanning the Gulf Stream front, its standing
  gradient renders the variogram non-stationary and unbounded if left
  in.
\item \emph{Projection.} Convert $(\mathrm{lon},\mathrm{lat})$ to a local
  planar frame in kilometres about the box centre, so that lags and
  ranges are directly comparable to the sampling scale of
  \eqref{eq:cell-km}.
\item \emph{Directional empirical variogram.} Within each snapshot, form
  all pairs of grid points (subsampling for tractability), and accumulate
  the semivariance $\tfrac12(y_i-y_j)^2$ into bins of separation
  distance. Classify each pair by orientation and accumulate separately
  for zonal ($|\theta|<30^\circ$), meridional ($|\theta|>60^\circ$) and
  omnidirectional bins. Pool across snapshots. \emph{The variogram must
  be directional}: the frontal structure of this region is strongly
  anisotropic, with long correlation along the current and short
  correlation across it, and an isotropic average would conceal the
  short scale, which is the binding one.
\item \emph{Range extraction.} From each directional variogram read the
  decorrelation length two ways---the empirical $1/e$ crossing, i.e.\ the
  lag at which $\gamma(h)=(1-e^{-1})\,\mathrm{sill}$, and the range
  parameter of a fitted exponential (or Mat\'ern) model---and report
  both. The $1/e$ crossing is the robust primary, as the parametric fit
  can fail on noisy or hole-effect variograms.
\item \emph{Temporal scale.} Separately, compute the lagged
  autocorrelation of the de-seasonalized regional-mean OHC series and
  read its $e$-folding lag, which together with the seasonal amplitude
  determines the number of time bins $K_t$
  (Section~\ref{sec:leakage}).
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{The binding number.} Write $L_{\mathrm{merid}}$ and
$L_{\mathrm{zonal}}$ for the two directional ranges. Because
$\sigma_{Y^{\perp}}$ is controlled by field variation in \emph{every}
direction within a cell, it is the \emph{shorter} of the two---here the
across-front, meridional scale---that governs the residual term. The
relevance scale is therefore $L_{\mathrm{rel}}:=L_{\mathrm{merid}}$, and
the requirement is that cells be no coarser than $L_{\mathrm{rel}}$. As
the ranges depend on the de-trending choices of step~1, we report them
under an alternative de-trending as a sensitivity check and, where these
disagree, adopt the shorter (conservative) value.

\subsubsection*{The sampling scale: how finely $w$ can be resolved}

For a $K_{\mathrm{lon}}\times K_{\mathrm{lat}}$ partition of the box---%
here $\Delta\lambda=6^\circ$ of longitude by $\Delta\phi=4^\circ$ of
latitude about $\phi_0=38^\circ$---the zonal and meridional cell sides
are
\begin{equation}
  \Delta x=\frac{\Delta\lambda}{K_{\mathrm{lon}}}\,(111\,\mathrm{km})\cos\phi_0
    =\frac{525\,\mathrm{km}}{K_{\mathrm{lon}}},

  \Delta y=\frac{\Delta\phi}{K_{\mathrm{lat}}}\,(111\,\mathrm{km})

\paragraph{Why the estimator degrades when $N/K<1$.} The mean occupancy
$N/K$ is the natural measure of how much the counts can support, and
$N/K<1$ marks a qualitative change rather than merely a noisier regime.
Estimator~\eqref{eq:sigmawK-hat} reads the data only through
coincidences: by \eqref{eq:ustat}, $\widehat{\sigma_{w^{\star}}^2}=K U_N-1$
where $U_N$ is the fraction of distinct profile pairs sharing a cell, and
under near-uniform sampling the expected number of coincident ordered
pairs is
\[
  \E\Big[\sum_j n_j(n_j-1)\Big]=N(N-1)\sum_j\pi_j^2\approx\frac{N^2}{K}
  =\frac{N}{K}\cdot N .
\]
Once $N/K<1$ this total falls below $N$ and is often in the single
digits: cells holding a lone profile contribute $n_j(n_j-1)=0$, so the
statistic is determined by the few cells that happen to catch two. Four
consequences follow.

\emph{Quantization.} One additional coincident pair moves
$\widehat{\sigma_{w^{\star}}^2}$ by $2K/[N(N-1)]\approx 2K/N^2$, which
exceeds $2/N$ once $N/K<1$. The estimator's support becomes a coarse
lattice and it cannot resolve small departures from uniformity even in
principle.

\emph{Variance inflation.} Near uniformity $\Var(U_N)$ is of order
$\theta/N\approx 1/(NK)$, so
$\Var\big(\widehat{\sigma_{w^{\star}}^2}\big)\sim K/N$: the standard error
grows like $\sqrt{K/N}$ while the estimand remains $O(1)$. That this is
the same ratio $K/N$ governing the plug-in bias of
Lemma~\ref{lem:unbiased} is no accident---both pathologies have the
single source of too few pairs per cell.

\emph{Grid-phase dependence.} At fine scales, whether two nearby profiles
share a cell depends on where the boundaries happen to fall. Two
partitions with identical $K$ can yield materially different
$\widehat{\sigma_{w^{\star}}}$, so the statistic reads boundary placement
rather than sampling structure---the signature of having passed the
resolvable scale.

\emph{Degeneracy and untrustworthy intervals.} When no two distinct
profiles share a cell, $\sum_j n_j(n_j-1)=0$ and
$\widehat{\sigma_{w^{\star}}^2}=-1$ exactly, its minimum; this occurs with
certainty once $K\gtrsim N^2$. The delete-one-float jackknife
\eqref{eq:jackvar} is consistent for smooth functionals, but in this
regime the statistic is a jumpy, discrete function of the data and the
justification lapses---indeed the jackknife standard error collapses
toward zero where the estimate is degenerate, so intervals can
spuriously \emph{narrow} exactly where they are least reliable.

Accordingly we require mean occupancy comfortably above one. This is the
same guidance as the classical requirement that Pearson expected cell
counts exceed ${\approx}5$, and for the same reason:
$\widehat{\sigma_{w^{\star}}^2}^{\,\mathrm{plug}}=X^2/N$ exactly, so the
sparse-cell caveat transfers verbatim. Because the independent units are
the $F$ floats rather than the $N$ profiles, the effective occupancy is
nearer $F/K$ than $N/K$ and the constraint binds sooner than the raw
profile count suggests; the float-based spacing $s\approx76$\,km is
therefore the honest coarse anchor.

\subsubsection*{The envelope scale: how finely $w$ \emph{may} be resolved}
\label{sec:envelope-scale}

The data-only bound imposes a third constraint, absent when the field
factor came from an external product. Proposition~\ref{prop:sigmaY-envelope}
is vacuous unless its denominator is positive, and by
Lemma~\ref{lem:coarsening} the quantity controlling it increases without
plateau under refinement. The admissible range is therefore bounded
above.

\begin{lemma}[Refinement ceiling]
\label{lem:refinement-ceiling}
The envelope $\overline{\sigma}_{Y'}$ of
Proposition~\ref{prop:sigmaY-envelope} is finite and positive if and only
if
\begin{equation}
  \sigma_{w^{\star}}
  \;<\;\sigma_{\max}(\kappa')
  \;:=\;\tfrac12\Big(\sqrt{\kappa'+3}-\sqrt{\kappa'-1}\Big).
  \label{eq:refinement-ceiling}
\end{equation}
The threshold is decreasing in $\kappa'$, with
$\sigma_{\max}=0.518$ at $\kappa'=3$, $0.382$ at $\kappa'=6$ and $0.318$
at $\kappa'=9$.
\end{lemma}
\deferproof{refinement-ceiling}{%
\begin{proof}[Proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:refinement-ceiling}]
The denominator is $1-\Delta'$ with
$\Delta'=\sigma_{w^{\star}}\sqrt{\kappa'-1}+\sigma_{w^{\star}}^2$, so
positivity is $\sigma_{w^{\star}}^2+\sqrt{\kappa'-1}\,\sigma_{w^{\star}}-1<0$.
The left side is increasing in $\sigma_{w^{\star}}\ge0$ and vanishes at
the positive root
$\big(-\sqrt{\kappa'-1}+\sqrt{\kappa'+3}\big)/2$, giving
\eqref{eq:refinement-ceiling}. Monotonicity in $\kappa'$ follows since
$\sqrt{\kappa'+3}-\sqrt{\kappa'-1}=4/(\sqrt{\kappa'+3}+\sqrt{\kappa'-1})$
is decreasing.
\end{proof}%
}

Unlike the sampling scale, this constraint is not about estimator
degeneracy: $\sigwhat$ may be perfectly well estimated and the envelope
still fail. It is a statement that beyond a certain unevenness the
second-moment contamination can no longer be separated from the
second moment itself. Since $\sigwhat$ is computed at every rung anyway,
\eqref{eq:refinement-ceiling} is checked directly on the refinement
curve, and rungs violating it are reported as such rather than assigned a
number.

\begin{remark}[Conservatism in $\kappa'$ is paid for in resolution]
\label{rem:kappa-resolution}
The two safety margins of the method run against each other. Padding
$\kappa'$ upward, which Remark~\ref{rem:kappa} recommends as the
conservative direction for the field shape, tightens
\eqref{eq:refinement-ceiling} and so forces coarser cells; coarser cells
raise $\sigma_{Y'^{\perp}}$ and with it the unestimated residual product
of Proposition~\ref{prop:scale-decomp}. Conservatism in the field factor
is thus bought with conservatism foregone in the residual term, and the
choice of $\kappa'$ should be reported jointly with the band of
resolutions it admits. This is a further reason to deseasonalize: the raw
field, whose kurtosis is depressed toward the platykurtic value of a
sinusoid (Remark~\ref{rem:kappa}), would advertise a permissive ceiling
that reflects the seasonal cycle rather than the eddy field the residual
term is about.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[The refinement curve is steeper than before]
\label{rem:steeper-curve}
Under the data-only bound the reported quantity at each rung is
\[
  \mathcal{B}(\varepsilon')
  =\sigwhat\,\overline{\sigma}_{Y'}(\varepsilon')+\varepsilon',
  \qquad
  \overline{\sigma}_{Y'}(0)=\sqrt{\frac{\hat R'}{1-\Delta'(\sigwhat)}},
\]
in which $\sigwhat$ appears both as the prefactor and inside the
denominator of the envelope. The curve therefore climbs faster under refinement than it
does when the field factor is supplied externally, where the growth is
only that of $\sigma_{Y'^{\star}}$ under Lemma~\ref{lem:coarsening}, and
it diverges at \eqref{eq:refinement-ceiling}. The most informative
operating point within the band \eqref{eq:verdict} is correspondingly
coarser, and is selected by the curve itself rather than by refining as
far as the count statistic permits.
\end{remark}

\subsubsection*{The operating band, and the verdict}

The scales bracket the choice from opposite directions: cells must be
\emph{fine enough} that $\sigma_{Y'^{\perp}}$ is small
($\Delta x,\Delta y\lesssim L_{\mathrm{rel}}$), and \emph{coarse enough}
both that coincidences are countable ($N/K$, effectively $F/K$, not below
one) and that the envelope survives
(\eqref{eq:refinement-ceiling}). The latter two bind from the same
direction but for unrelated reasons, and either may be the operative one:
whichever of $F/K\approx1$ and
$\sigwhat=\sigma_{\max}(\kappa')$ is reached first at the fine end
terminates the band. The band is non-empty precisely when
\begin{equation}
  L_{\mathrm{rel}} \;\gtrsim\; s_F=\sqrt{|A|/F}\approx 76\,\mathrm{km},
  \label{eq:verdict}
\end{equation}
i.e.\ when the field's shortest decorrelation length is no finer than the
scale the floats can resolve. Condition \eqref{eq:verdict} is not assumed:
it is the verdict that the variogram procedure above returns, and it is
reported as such. Its two branches lead to materially different claims,
and which obtains is an empirical matter to be settled per region and
period.

If \eqref{eq:verdict} holds, cells at the sampling scale sit within a
decorrelation length, $\sigma_{Y^{\perp}}$ is correspondingly small, and
the residual $\sigma_{w^{\perp}}\sigma_{Y^{\perp}}$ is negligible against
the leading term $\sigma_{w^{\star}}\sigma_{Y^{\star}}$. The bound is then
certified at the operating resolution: the sampling structure we cannot
resolve lies at scales where the field does not vary, and so cannot bias
a regional mean.

If instead $L_{\mathrm{rel}}<s_F$, there is a band of field structure,
between $L_{\mathrm{rel}}$ and $s_F$, that is bias-relevant but that the
float pattern cannot resolve. The residual term is then not automatically
negligible and must be confronted rather than assumed away: we report the
bound as conditional on the unresolved $\sigma_{w^{\perp}}$, rather than
claiming a certified envelope.

In either branch we report the diagnostic
\begin{equation}
  \frac{\sigYperphat}{\hat\sigma_Y}
  \;=\;\text{fraction of field variance below the cell scale},
  \label{eq:residual-diagnostic}
\end{equation}
with $\sigYperphat$ from \eqref{eq:sigmaYperp-pairs} and $\hat\sigma_Y$
the envelope $\overline{\sigma}_{Y'}$, as direct evidence of the
magnitude of the dropped term. Being a ratio of two dispersions of the
same field, it is insensitive to a common multiplicative rescaling and
may equally be read off an auxiliary product without violating
Remark~\ref{rem:conservative-Y}. Because $\sigma_{Y'^{\perp}}$ is
estimable even when $\sigma_{w^{\perp}}$ is
not, \eqref{eq:residual-diagnostic} quantifies how much of the residual
product can be bounded regardless of the verdict---a small value confines
the unresolved sampling roughness to scales at which it cannot matter,
whatever its size. This test, applied to any region and period, is what
determines whether a bound can be certified there, and it is the honest
failure mode of the method: where the data cannot support a certified
bound, the procedure says so rather than returning a reassuring number.

\subsubsection*{Reporting}

Because $\sigma_{w^{\star}}$ is resolution-dependent by construction, we
report not a single value but the \emph{refinement curve}
$K\mapsto\sigwhat$ across the $25$--$76$\,km band, each rung carrying its
float-clustered interval (Section~\ref{sec:uncertainty}), with
$L_{\mathrm{rel}}$ and $s_F$ marked. The field factors
$\overline{\sigma}_{Y'}$ and $\sigYperphat$ are evaluated on the
\emph{same} partition at each rung, so that weight and field share the
partition at every resolution, which is the only consistency
Proposition~\ref{prop:scale-decomp} requires.

Within this band we evaluate the all-pairs
estimator~\eqref{eq:sigmawK-hat} on all $N=364$ profiles rather than
retreating to the coarse, float-limited end. Resting on more pairs and
better-populated cells, it has lower sampling variance than its
between-float counterpart~\eqref{eq:sigmawK-bf}; and where consecutive
surfacings of a float re-enter the same cell, the additional
within-float coincidences inflate the estimate, which by
Lemma~\ref{lem:clustering-inflates} is the conservative direction for a
bound (under the checkable condition $c\ge\theta$ that a float coincides
with itself at least as often as two independent draws would). Its
\emph{independent} information nonetheless amounts to only $F=40$
floats, so uncertainty is quantified by float-level resampling rather
than by treating profiles as independent, and we report
\eqref{eq:sigmawK-bf} alongside as the estimate free of any within-float
independence assumption. The gap between the two is itself the diagnostic
of how much apparent dispersion is trajectory autocorrelation rather than
preferential sampling.


\subsection{Testing uniform sampling}
\label{sec:uniformity-test}

The dispersion $\sigwhat$ also supports a formal test of the null of
uniform sampling, $H_0:\pi=\nu$ (equivalently $\pi_j=1/K$ for all $j$).
Under $H_0$ the plug-in dispersion is a rescaled Pearson
goodness-of-fit statistic,
\[
  X^2=\sum_{j=1}^{K}\frac{(n_j-N/K)^2}{N/K}
     =N\,\sigwhatsqplug,
\]
and preferential sampling makes $X^2$ (and $\sigwhatsq$)
large. The classical reference $X^2\sim\chi^2_{K-1}$ is \emph{not} valid
here: the profiles are cluster-dependent (Remark~\ref{rem:trajectory}),
which over-disperses the cell counts and inflates $X^2$ under $H_0$, so a
$\chi^2_{K-1}$ $p$-value over-rejects and reports spurious preferential
sampling; the sparse expected counts $N/K$ compound this.

We calibrate instead by a float-level permutation null that preserves
the within-float trajectory dependence. Each whole float is given an
independent uniform rigid shift in the reference coordinates
$(\mathrm{lon},\ \sin\mathrm{lat},\ t)$, wrapped toroidally onto the
domain; this leaves each float's internal geometry---hence its
within-float clustering and $d_\ell$---intact while placing floats
independently and uniformly, so the permutation distribution reproduces
the within-float over-dispersion of the data under $H_0$. It does not
reproduce \emph{between}-float dependence: the independent per-float
shifts place floats independently, whereas shared advection positively
correlates nearby floats, so the null is mildly under-dispersed and the
test correspondingly anti-conservative in that component. Removing this
would require shifting whole advective clusters jointly, which we leave
to future work; here we simply flag the direction of the effect. For a statistic
$T\in\{X^2,\ \sigwhatsqbf\}$ with observed
value $T_{\mathrm{obs}}$ and $B$ permutation replicates
$T^{(1)},\dots,T^{(B)}$, the one-sided $p$-value is
\[
  \hat p=\frac{1+\#\{b:\,T^{(b)}\ge T_{\mathrm{obs}}\}}{B+1}.
\]
The between-float statistic~\eqref{eq:sigmawK-bf} is the more
informative choice: it is free of the within-float clustering that the
permutation must otherwise absorb, and is centered at $0$ under $H_0$
when distinct floats are independent (Lemma~\ref{lem:bf-unbiased}). It
does not remove between-float dependence, which the per-float shift also
omits from the null, so a small $\hat p$ is evidence against uniformity
\emph{or} against unmodelled between-float coherence, and should be read
with the caveat above.

Two cautions. A large $\hat p$ is evidence of \emph{no detectable}
departure from uniform, not of exact uniformity; with few floats the
test has limited power, so we treat it as corroboration and let the
effect size $\sigwhat$ with its interval---in particular the upper
confidence limit the bound requires---carry the quantitative claim.
Second, the gap between the permutation $\hat p$ and the naive
$\chi^2_{K-1}$ $p$-value is itself worth reporting, as it quantifies how
much the trajectory dependence would have inflated a naive test.

\subsection{Uncertainty quantification}
\label{sec:uncertainty}

The two factors carry different kinds of uncertainty, and we keep them
separate.

For $\sigwhat$, the relevant variability is sampling variability
over floats, and the multinomial/normal approximation to the $\chi^2$
statistic is invalid because of the trajectory dependence of
Remark~\ref{rem:trajectory}. Because
$\sum_j n_j(n_j-1)=\sum_{\ell\neq\ell'}O_{\ell\ell'}$, where
$O_{\ell\ell'}$ is the footprint overlap of floats $\ell$ and $\ell'$,
the estimator is a degree-two U-statistic in the floats; a
with-replacement float bootstrap is consequently biased, since a
duplicated float injects a spurious self-overlap $O_{\ell\ell}$. We
therefore use a \emph{delete-one-float jackknife}: recompute the
estimator with each float's profiles removed in turn and form the
jackknife variance over the $L=40$ floats. The
float---not the profile---is the resampling unit, which reflects the
effective sample size and propagates the within-float correlation into
the interval; the intervals widen at the fine end of the refinement
curve, honestly displaying what $40$ tracks can resolve.

Concretely, both estimators are functions of the $L$ float footprints
$\{m_{\ell j}\}$, and the numerator of \eqref{eq:sigmawK-hat} decomposes
over float pairs as
$\sum_j n_j(n_j-1)=\sum_{\ell\ne\ell'}O_{\ell\ell'}+\sum_\ell\sum_j m_{\ell j}(m_{\ell j}-1)$
with $O_{\ell\ell'}=\sum_j m_{\ell j}m_{\ell'j}$, exhibiting it as a
degree-two $U$-statistic in the floats. The delete-one-float jackknife
variance is
\begin{equation}
  \widehat V_{\mathrm{jack}}
  =\frac{L-1}{L}\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}\big(\hat\theta_{(-\ell)}-\bar\theta_{(\cdot)}\big)^2,
  \qquad \bar\theta_{(\cdot)}=\frac1L\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}\hat\theta_{(-\ell)},
  \label{eq:jackvar}
\end{equation}
where $\hat\theta_{(-\ell)}$ recomputes $\sigwhatsq$ (or its
between-float variant) with float $\ell$'s profiles removed; the reported
interval is $\hat\theta\pm z_{1-\alpha/2}\,\widehat V_{\mathrm{jack}}^{1/2}$
on the $\sigma^2$ scale, with $\sigma$-scale endpoints
$\sqrt{\max(0,\cdot)}$. For $U$-statistics this jackknife is consistent
for the asymptotic variance \citep{arvesen1969}.

The with-replacement float bootstrap, by contrast, is biased for this
functional. When a float is resampled more than once, its identical
copies form coincidence pairs that two distinct floats never would, each
adding a spurious self-overlap $O_{\ell\ell}=\sum_j m_{\ell j}^2$; these ties shift
the bootstrap distribution of $\sigwhatsq$ upward and break
interval coverage---in simulation a with-replacement float bootstrap
attained $\approx 50\%$ coverage against a nominal $95\%$, whereas the
jackknife \eqref{eq:jackvar} attained $\approx 95\%$. The jackknife only
deletes and never duplicates, so it avoids the ties; we use it
throughout and retain the bootstrap only as a diagnostic.

The field factor now carries sampling uncertainty rather than
field-proxy uncertainty, since $\overline{\sigma}_{Y'}$ is a functional
of the observed profiles: the same delete-one-float jackknife applies to
it, refitting the offset within each replicate
(Section~\ref{sec:deseason}). What it does not carry is uncertainty in
$\kappa$, which is a chosen constant rather than an estimate; its
influence is reported as a sensitivity curve over the plausible range of
Remark~\ref{rem:kappa} rather than as an interval.

The reported bound at each resolution is the curve
$\mathcal{B}(\varepsilon')$ of \eqref{eq:bias-eps}, with an interval
obtained by propagating the delete-one-float jackknife
\eqref{eq:jackvar} through both factors jointly, accompanied by the
breakdown budgets $\varepsilon^{\star}$ of
Definition~\ref{def:breakdown} at the trend and posterior-SD benchmarks
and by the residual diagnostic \eqref{eq:residual-diagnostic}. To interpret
severity, we express the bound relative to an external yardstick---the
estimated regional OHC trend over the period, and the posterior
standard deviation of the hierarchical OHC model
(Remark~\ref{rem:coherence})---so that ``not severe'' is stated as a
fraction of a signal the reader already trusts rather than in absolute
units.

\subsection{Summary of the procedure}
\label{sec:procedure}

\begin{enumerate}
\item Construct the offset $m$ of Section~\ref{sec:rg}, freezing its
  seasonal harmonics from the $2004$--$2019$ climatology, and form the
  deseasonalized field $y'=y-m$ at the profile locations.
\item Fix the relevance scale from an empirical variogram of $y'$;
  choose a bracket of equal-measure partitions
  (Definition~\ref{def:partition}) spanning the ${\approx}25$--$76$\,km
  sampling band \eqref{eq:cell-km}, verifying it is at or above the
  relevance scale.
\item Retain all profiles (no thinning): the within-float clustering
  this leaves biases $\sigwhat$ upward only, the safe direction
  for the bound (Remark~\ref{rem:trajectory}).
\item At each partition: form counts $\{n_j\}$ and compute
  $\sigwhat$ by \eqref{eq:sigmawK-hat}; compute the design variance
  $\hat R'$ and residual kurtosis $\hat\kappa'$ of $y'$ over the
  profiles, and $\sigYperphat$ from within-cell float pairs
  \eqref{eq:sigmaYperp-pairs}; verify
  $\Delta'<1$ against Lemma~\ref{lem:refinement-ceiling} using the upper
  jackknife limit of $\sigwhat$ (Remark~\ref{rem:delta-fails}), and form
  the envelope $\overline{\sigma}_{Y'}(\cdot)$ by
  Proposition~\ref{prop:sigmaY-envelope}.
\item Repeat step~4 over a range of conservative $\kappa$
  (Remark~\ref{rem:kappa}) and report the sensitivity.
\item Delete-one-float jackknife $\sigwhat$ for intervals
  \eqref{eq:jackvar} (Section~\ref{sec:uncertainty}).
\item Report the refinement curve of the bound
  $\mathcal{B}(\varepsilon')$ with intervals and the breakdown budgets
  $\varepsilon^{\star}$ of Definition~\ref{def:breakdown}, the residual
  diagnostic \eqref{eq:residual-diagnostic}, the control-variate
  diagnostics $\hat\rho^2$ and $\hat D$ of Section~\ref{sec:rg}, and the
  bound as a fraction of the OHC trend and of the model posterior SD.
\end{enumerate}

The resulting guarantee depends on the data through counts of floats in
cells, the observed OHC values at those floats, and an externally frozen
seasonal offset; the only modeling choices are the cell scale, which is
pinned to a measured
decorrelation length and displayed as a curve rather than fixed
silently. The complex spatio-temporal intensity model of
Remark~\ref{rem:hat-w} and the advection simulator are reserved as
independent cross-checks on this estimate, not as inputs to it.
