\subsection{Notation and standing conventions}
\label{sec:loo-setup}

Throughout this section the velocity fields $\tV_1,\dots,\tV_M$ and the residual fields
$\tZ_1,\dots,\tZ_M$ are \emph{fixed}: they are realised reanalysis output, and no distribution is
placed over them. All randomness enters through the deployment draws
$\omega_{l,d} \overset{iid}{\sim} \D$, and every expectation and variance below is taken with
respect to $\D$ alone, conditional on the realised fields.

For a design $X = \{x_1,\dots,x_n\} \subset \X$ write
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:mu-def}
  \mu_X \;:=\; \hat{\mathbb{P}}_X - \mathbb{P},
  \qquad
  \hat{\mathbb{P}}_X \;=\; \frac1n \sum_{i=1}^{n}\delta_{x_i},
\end{equation}
a signed measure of total mass zero, whose restriction to the partition $\{c_j\}_{j=1}^{C}$ is the
vector $\big(w_1 - p_1, \dots, w_C - p_C\big)$. Because $\tZ_k$ is constant within cells, the
sampling bias against field year $k$ is the bilinear pairing
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:pairing}
  B_k(X) \;=\; \int_{\X} \tZ_k \, d\mu_X
  \;=\; \big\langle \mu_X,\, \tZ_k \big\rangle
  \;=\; \sum_{j=1}^{C}\big(w_j - p_j\big)\,\tZ_{k,j},
\end{equation}
an identity rather than an approximation: $\tZ_k$ is a bounded simple function and $\mu_X$ a finite
signed measure. We abbreviate $\mu^{(l,d)} := \mu_{X^{(l,d)}}$ and
$B_k^{(l,d)} := \langle \mu^{(l,d)}, \tZ_k\rangle$.

\begin{definition}[Mean footprint of a velocity year]
\label{def:footprint}
For each velocity year $l$,
\begin{equation}
  m^{(l)} \;:=\; \E_{\omega \sim \D}\big[\mu_{\G(\omega,\, \tV_l)}\big],
\end{equation}
the deployment-averaged design deviation induced by $\tV_l$. Since $\D$ and $\tV_l$ are fixed,
$m^{(l)}$ is a deterministic zero-mass signed measure.
\end{definition}

\noindent
Existence is immediate: the cellwise weights satisfy $w_j \in [0,1]$ and $C < \infty$, so
$\mu^{(l,d)}$ is uniformly bounded and $m^{(l)}$ is a finite average of bounded quantities.

\subsection{The leave-one-out estimand}
\label{sec:loo-estimand}

\begin{definition}[Leave-one-out reference and estimand]
\label{def:loo}
For an analysis year $k$ define the \emph{leave-one-out reference footprint}
\begin{equation}
  \bar{m}^{(-k)} \;:=\; \frac{1}{M-1}\sum_{l \neq k} m^{(l)} ,
\end{equation}
and the \emph{leave-one-out preferential bias}
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:loo-estimand}
  \boxed{\;\Theta_k \;:=\; \big\langle\, m^{(k)} - \bar{m}^{(-k)},\;\; \tZ_k \,\big\rangle \;}
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

\noindent
In words: the excess sampling bias against field year $k$ produced by that year's own circulation,
relative to the bias the remaining $M-1$ circulations would have produced against the \emph{same}
field. Both terms in \eqref{eq:loo-estimand} are deterministic, so $\Theta_k$ is a fixed quantity
attached to the realised record, in the units of $\tZ$ (J\,m$^{-2}$).

\begin{remark}[Coverage bias cancels; preferential bias does not]
\label{rem:coverage}
Write the mean footprint of each velocity year as a common part plus a year-specific anomaly,
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:mdecomp}
  m^{(l)} \;=\; \bar{m} \;+\; \delta^{(l)},
\end{equation}
where $\bar{m}$ collects whatever is shared by every year --- the array's persistent
non-uniformity, arising from deployment geography, the time-mean circulation, and bathymetry ---
and $\delta^{(l)}$ is the departure induced by year $l$'s eddy field. Substituting
\eqref{eq:mdecomp} into \eqref{eq:loo-estimand},
\begin{equation}
  \Theta_k
  \;=\; \big\langle (\bar{m} + \delta^{(k)}) - (\bar{m} + \bar{\delta}^{(-k)}),\;\; \tZ_k\big\rangle
  \;=\; \big\langle \delta^{(k)} - \bar{\delta}^{(-k)},\;\; \tZ_k \big\rangle ,
\end{equation}
so $\bar{m}$ cancels identically. The cancellation is exact, follows from linearity alone, and
holds for any decomposition of the form \eqref{eq:mdecomp}: a quantity that does not vary with $l$
cannot survive a difference taken across $l$.

The structure responsible is that $\Theta_k$ differences two \emph{designs} against a
\emph{single} field. Had the comparison instead held the design fixed and varied the field, as in
$\langle m^{(k)}, \tZ_k - \tZ_{k'}\rangle$, the common footprint would multiply
$\tZ_k - \tZ_{k'} \neq 0$ and would not cancel. Holding the field fixed while varying the
circulation is therefore the load-bearing feature of the construction, not an incidental choice.

The excluded term $\langle \bar{m}, \tZ_k\rangle$ is the \emph{coverage bias}: the persistent
sampling footprint paired against field year $k$. It is typically the larger of the two
contributions to the total bias $\langle m^{(k)}, \tZ_k\rangle$; it is excluded deliberately, for
the reason given in Remark~\ref{rem:whycare}.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Why the reference excludes $k$]
\label{rem:whyloo}
An alternative is to centre on the grand mean $\bar{m} = M^{-1}\sum_{l} m^{(l)}$ and target
$\langle m^{(k)} - \bar{m}, \tZ_k\rangle$. That construction is less convenient because the
identity $\sum_{l}(m^{(l)} - \bar{m}) = 0$ forces the reference to depend on year $k$ itself: any
excess in $m^{(k)}$ is offset by a compensating deficit distributed across the remaining years, so
a contrast against a strict subset of them acquires a multiplicative distortion and a residual
depending on which years were excluded. Removing $k$ from its own reference eliminates the
constraint, and with it both artefacts. The two estimands differ by a known factor,
$\langle m^{(k)} - \bar m, \tZ_k\rangle = \tfrac{M-1}{M}\,\Theta_k$.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[What $\Theta_k$ does and does not isolate]
\label{rem:interpretation}
$\Theta_k$ is nonzero precisely when the design generated by $\tV_k$ is aligned with $\tZ_k$ to a
greater degree than designs generated by other years' circulations. Because $\tV_k$ and $\tZ_k$
arise from the same ocean state, such alignment is the signature of preferential sampling in the
sense of $[S,X] \neq [S][X]$, restricted to the flow-anomaly component of the design.

The reference $\bar{m}^{(-k)}$ includes the adjacent years $l = k \pm 1$, whose circulations retain
dependence on $\tZ_k$ through ocean memory. Any such residual alignment inflates the reference and
therefore \emph{attenuates} $\Theta_k$. The estimand is thus conservative: it understates the
excess alignment by an amount governed by the persistence of the flow--field coupling, which
Section~\ref{sec:XXX} quantifies through the dependence of $\langle m^{(l)}, \tZ_k\rangle$ on
$|l-k|$. We prefer attenuation of a well-defined target to the alternative of excluding adjacent
years, which reintroduces a reference depending on the excluded set.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Why the preferential component is of interest even when small]
\label{rem:whycare}
Coverage bias is a deterministic function of position: given the profile locations and a
climatology, $\langle \bar{m}, \tZ_k\rangle$ can be computed and removed, or equivalently
suppressed by inverse-intensity weighting of the observations. Preferential bias admits no such
correction. It is a covariance between the sampling design and the component of the field that
position does not predict, so reweighting by position --- which is all an estimated sampling
intensity supplies --- cannot reach it. The two components differ in kind and not merely in
magnitude, and $\Theta_k$ isolates the one that survives the standard corrections.

The mechanism is intrinsic to the platform. Floats are advected by geostrophic currents, and
those currents are related through thermal wind to the density structure that determines heat
content; the instrument is transported by the field it measures. This is a property of Lagrangian
sampling as such --- shared with surface drifters and with any platform whose position is set by
the flow, and absent from moored or ship-based observation --- rather than a defect of the Argo
design.

Two further points bear on interpretation. First, if the underlying mechanism is sign-definite,
for instance if warm-core anticyclones retain floats more effectively than cold-core cyclones,
the bias does not average away as the array grows: it is a floor rather than an error term.
Second, and more consequentially, \textbf{the threat to climate applications is drift rather than
level}. A preferential offset constant in time affects the estimated mean state but cancels in
anomalies and trends, which are the quantities used in ocean heat uptake and Earth energy
imbalance assessments; a \emph{time-varying} offset does not. The Argo array roughly tripled in
size between 2004 and 2007, parking depths were standardised, float models changed, and
high-latitude coverage expanded. Any accompanying change in trapping propensity appears as a
spurious heat flux
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:flux}
  \frac{\Delta\Theta}{\Delta t \times 3.15\times10^{7}\,\mathrm{s}} \quad \mathrm{W\,m^{-2}},
\end{equation}
with $\Delta t$ the span in years, so that a drift of $5\times10^{7}$ J\,m$^{-2}$ over a decade
corresponds to $0.16$ W\,m$^{-2}$ --- of order a fifth of the present energy imbalance.
Estimating $\Theta_k$ separately by year, rather than pooling alone, is therefore the more
informative use of the construction.

Finally, ``not correctable by reweighting'' should not be read as ``not correctable''. Because
altimetry observes the mesoscale field independently of Argo, post-stratifying profiles by eddy
class supplies the missing information and removes the class-driven component of the bias. The
accurate statement is that preferential bias is invisible to position-based methods and can be
addressed only using external information about the field itself.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Unbiased estimation}
\label{sec:loo-estimator}

Let $\bar{G}_{kl} := D^{-1}\sum_{d=1}^{D} B_k^{(l,d)}$ denote the cell mean of the simulation
array.

\begin{definition}[Leave-one-out estimator]
\label{def:loo-est}
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:loo-est}
  \boxed{\;\widehat{\Theta}_k \;:=\; \bar{G}_{kk} \;-\; \frac{1}{M-1}\sum_{l \neq k} \bar{G}_{kl}\;}
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Exact unbiasedness]
\label{prop:unbiased}
For every $k$,
\begin{equation}
  \E\big[\widehat{\Theta}_k\big] \;=\; \Theta_k ,
\end{equation}
with no assumption on the velocity fields, the residual fields, or their relationship beyond the
existence of $m^{(l)}$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Each $\mu^{(l,d)}$ is bounded and $C < \infty$, so $B_k^{(l,d)}$ is integrable and expectation
commutes with the finite sums below. For any $l$, by linearity of the pairing in its first
argument and Definition~\ref{def:footprint},
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:cellmean}
  \E\big[\bar{G}_{kl}\big]
  \;=\; \frac{1}{D}\sum_{d=1}^{D} \E\big[\langle \mu^{(l,d)},\, \tZ_k\rangle\big]
  \;=\; \frac{1}{D}\sum_{d=1}^{D} \big\langle \E\big[\mu^{(l,d)}\big],\, \tZ_k\big\rangle
  \;=\; \big\langle m^{(l)},\, \tZ_k \big\rangle ,
\end{equation}
the last step because the $\omega_{l,d}$ are identically distributed across $d$. Applying
\eqref{eq:cellmean} to \eqref{eq:loo-est} and using linearity once more,
\begin{equation}
  \E\big[\widehat{\Theta}_k\big]
  \;=\; \big\langle m^{(k)}, \tZ_k\big\rangle - \frac{1}{M-1}\sum_{l\neq k}\big\langle m^{(l)}, \tZ_k\big\rangle
  \;=\; \Big\langle m^{(k)} - \frac{1}{M-1}\sum_{l\neq k} m^{(l)},\;\tZ_k\Big\rangle
  \;=\; \Theta_k . \qedhere
\end{equation}
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
\label{rem:noassumption}
The proof uses only linearity of \eqref{eq:pairing}, boundedness, and the fact that deployment
draws are identically distributed within a cell. In particular it does not require the designs to
be identically distributed across velocity years, nor the residual fields to be exchangeable across
years, nor any distributional form for $B$. Those conditions are needed to attach a $p$-value to
$\widehat{\Theta}_k$ (Section~\ref{sec:XXX}), not to estimate $\Theta_k$. Estimation and inference
are therefore separable: $\widehat{\Theta}_k$ may be reported with its standard error even where
the randomisation hypothesis underlying the permutation test is in doubt.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Variance}
\label{sec:loo-var}

Write, for each cell,
\begin{equation}
  \sigma^2_{kl} \;:=\; \Var_{\omega\sim\D}\Big(\big\langle \mu_{\G(\omega,\tV_l)},\, \tZ_k\big\rangle\Big) ,
\end{equation}
the deployment variance of the single-design bias. Finiteness again follows from boundedness of
$\mu$ and $C < \infty$.

\begin{proposition}[Variance of the estimator]
\label{prop:var}
If the deployment draws $\{\omega_{l,d}\}$ are mutually independent across all pairs $(l,d)$, then
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:var-general}
  \Var\big(\widehat{\Theta}_k\big)
  \;=\; \frac{1}{D}\left[\, \sigma^2_{kk} \;+\; \frac{1}{(M-1)^2}\sum_{l \neq k} \sigma^2_{kl} \,\right] .
\end{equation}
If in addition $\sigma^2_{kl} \equiv \sigma^2_k$ for all $l$, this reduces to
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:var-homo}
  \Var\big(\widehat{\Theta}_k\big) \;=\; \frac{\sigma_k^{2}}{D}\left(1 + \frac{1}{M-1}\right),
  \qquad
  \mathrm{sd}\big(\widehat{\Theta}_k\big) \;=\; \sigma_k\sqrt{\frac{M}{(M-1)D}} .
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Within cell $(k,l)$ the summands $B_k^{(l,d)}$, $d = 1,\dots,D$, are i.i.d.\ with variance
$\sigma^2_{kl}$, so $\Var(\bar{G}_{kl}) = \sigma^2_{kl}/D$. Independence across pairs $(l,d)$
makes the cell means $\{\bar{G}_{kl}\}_{l}$ mutually independent, so the variance of the linear
combination \eqref{eq:loo-est} is the weighted sum of variances with squared coefficients $1$ and
$(M-1)^{-2}$:
\begin{equation}
  \Var\big(\widehat{\Theta}_k\big)
  \;=\; \Var\big(\bar{G}_{kk}\big) + \frac{1}{(M-1)^2}\sum_{l\neq k}\Var\big(\bar{G}_{kl}\big) ,
\end{equation}
which is \eqref{eq:var-general}. Substituting $\sigma^2_{kl} \equiv \sigma^2_k$ and using
$\sum_{l\neq k} 1 = M-1$ gives \eqref{eq:var-homo}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Estimating $\sigma^2_{kl}$, and the effective sample size]
\label{rem:sigma}
$\sigma^2_{kl}$ is estimated by the within-cell sample variance of $\{B_k^{(l,d)}\}_{d=1}^{D}$,
which requires no model. It is worth noting what it is not: for a design consisting of $n$
independent draws from $\mathbb{P}$ one would have
$\sigma^2_{kl} = \Var_{\mathbb{P}}(\tZ_k)\,\E[1/n]$, but advected floats are strongly clustered
along trajectories, so consecutive profiles are near-replicates and the effective sample size is
far below $n$. The empirical within-cell variance captures this automatically; the i.i.d.\ formula
would understate it substantially and should not be substituted.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Common random numbers]
\label{rem:crn}
If a single deployment realisation $\omega_d$ is reused across velocity years, the cell means
$\{\bar{G}_{kl}\}_l$ are no longer independent and \eqref{eq:var-general} requires a covariance
term. Writing $\rho$ for the common correlation between $\bar{G}_{kl}$ and $\bar{G}_{kl'}$,
$l \neq l'$, under the homoscedastic model,
\begin{equation}
  \Var\big(\widehat{\Theta}_k\big)
  \;=\; \frac{\sigma_k^2}{D}\left(1 + \frac{1}{M-1}\right)(1-\rho) ,
\end{equation}
a variance reduction when $\rho > 0$. Proposition~\ref{prop:unbiased} is unaffected, since it uses
only marginal first moments. We nonetheless draw $\omega_{l,d}$ independently for each pair: the
dependence induced by shared seeds decays with $|l - l'|$, which would render the velocity-year
labels non-exchangeable and invalidate the permutation reference distribution of
Section~\ref{sec:XXX}, and the achievable reduction is small because trajectories from common seeds
separate over a Lyapunov time short relative to the simulation length.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Comparison with a gapped contrast]
\label{rem:gap}
A contrast that omits adjacent years, using $\mathcal{C}_k = \{l : |l-k| \ge 2\}$ with
$c = |\mathcal{C}_k|$, has variance $\sigma_k^2 D^{-1}(1 + c^{-1})$ under the same homoscedastic
model. Since $c < M-1$, the gapped contrast has strictly larger variance than
\eqref{eq:var-homo} \emph{and} is biased for $\Theta_k$ unless the omitted years satisfy an
additional condition. The leave-one-out form is therefore preferable on both counts, and any
gapped variant is best reported as a sensitivity analysis for Remark~\ref{rem:interpretation}
rather than as the primary estimate.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Pooled estimator}
\label{sec:loo-pooled}

\begin{definition}
\begin{equation}
  \bar{\Theta} \;:=\; \frac{1}{m}\sum_{k=1}^{m} \Theta_k ,
  \qquad
  \widehat{\bar{\Theta}} \;:=\; \frac{1}{m}\sum_{k=1}^{m} \widehat{\Theta}_k ,
\end{equation}
where $k$ ranges over the $m \le M$ analysis years.
\end{definition}

\noindent
Unbiasedness is immediate from Proposition~\ref{prop:unbiased} and linearity:
$\E[\widehat{\bar{\Theta}}] = \bar{\Theta}$, again without assumption. The variance does not
factorise so simply, because $\widehat{\Theta}_k$ and $\widehat{\Theta}_{k'}$ share cell means
computed from the same simulations; it is therefore estimated directly from the empirical
covariance of $\{\widehat{\Theta}_k\}$ across replicate subsamples, or bounded above by
$m^{-1}\max_k \Var(\widehat{\Theta}_k) \cdot m = \max_k \Var(\widehat{\Theta}_k)$ in the worst case
of perfect correlation.
