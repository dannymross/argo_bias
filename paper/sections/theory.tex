% Theory and methods: doubly robust estimation of annual North Atlantic OHC under Lagrangian sampling.

\section{Overview}\label{sec:overview}

The target is the area-mean $0$--$2000$\,m ocean heat content (OHC) of the deep North Atlantic in
each year. Argo floats sample it where the flow takes them, so the profiles are not a uniform draw
from the domain. We estimate it with a cross-fit doubly robust (DR) estimator: a map (local mean
field plus locally stationary kriging) plus an inverse-intensity-weighted correction of the map's
error. The argument runs in eight steps.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item The estimand is a linear functional of a fixed field (\cref{sec:setting}).
  \item Conditional on the ocean, the profiles form a point process whose only randomness is the
        deployments; its intensity is a fixed function of the deployment law and the flow
        (\cref{sec:design}).
  \item Any linear estimator has an exact bias identity; the map's error is its integrated
        residual, which nothing forces to vanish (\cref{sec:linear,sec:outcome}).
  \item DR adds to the map a weighted estimate of the map's own error from floats the map did not
        see. Its error has an exact representation, and its bias is a product of map error and
        intensity error plus named remainders (\cref{sec:dr}).
  \item Its variance comes from the design alone and has a float-cluster estimator
        (\cref{sec:variance}).
  \item DR trades the map's error for the noise and residual bias of
        estimating it; an exact MSE decomposition says when this pays (\cref{sec:gain}).
  \item The intensity is estimated by simulating the deployments in the GLORYS flow
        (\cref{sec:qhat}); on real data, the error of that simulation is what the bias multiplies
        (\cref{sec:real}).
  \item Validation separates what holds by construction from what must be tested
        (\cref{sec:validation}).
\end{enumerate}
Approximations and heuristics are labelled where they occur. Assumptions are collected in
\cref{sec:assumptions} and open choices in \cref{sec:open}.

\section{Setting and estimand}\label{sec:setting}

Spacetime is $\X=\mathbb S^2\times\R$ with Borel $\sigma$-algebra $\B(\X)$. Let $\alpha$ be surface
measure on the sphere of radius $R_\oplus$ (m$^2$) and $\mu=\alpha\otimes\mathrm{Leb}$ (m$^2$\,day).
Let $A\subset\mathbb S^2$ be the analysis region (\cref{sec:open}), $M$ the mask of sea floor deeper
than $2000$\,m, and $A^*\subseteq A\cap M$ its Argo-reachable part (\cref{def:domain}). For year $y$,
with time interval $\T_y$, the basin-year domain and its target probability measure are
\[
  \X_y=A^*\times\T_y,\qquad \Prob_y(E)=\frac{\mu(E\cap\X_y)}{\mu(\X_y)},\quad E\in\B(\X).
\]
$Z:\X\to\R$ is the $0$--$2000$\,m OHC (J\,m$^{-2}$), bounded and measurable, and \emph{fixed}: all
randomness is in the sampling. The estimand is
\[
  \theta_y=\int_\X Z\dd\Prob_y ,
\]
the area-and-time mean OHC of the reachable basin in year $y$; total heat is $\alpha(A^*)\,\theta_y$.
Changes $\theta_{y+1}-\theta_y$ and other contrasts $\sum_y\gamma_y\theta_y$ are linear functionals
of the same kind (\cref{rem:changes}).

\begin{definition}[Reachable domain]\label{def:domain}
With $\hat q=\sum_cN_c\hat q^{(1)}_c$ the simulated intensity of the whole array (\cref{sec:qhat}), a
threshold $q_A>0$ and analysis years $\mathcal Y$,
\[
  A^*=\Big\{s\in A\cap M:\ \frac1{|\T_y|}\int_{\T_y}\hat q(s,t)\,dt\ge q_A\ \text{for every }y\in\mathcal Y\Big\}.
\]
\end{definition}
$A^*$ is fixed in advance from the simulation alone, never from $Z$ or residuals, so the estimand
does not depend on the outcome; and it is the same in every year, so the series refers to the same
ocean throughout. The excluded deep water (expected to be the Gulf of Mexico and Caribbean,
ice-affected parts of the Labrador Sea, and areas near the edge of the velocity domain) is reported
by area; if a full-basin number is wanted, it is filled by the map alone and labelled as carrying no DR
guarantee. In the chain $\hat q$ comes from independent replicates and $A^*$ is fixed. On real data
$\mathcal D_c$ is built from the realized launches, so $A^*$ depends on them; we condition on the launch
pool and treat $A^*$ as fixed, a convention stated with the results. Excluding unreachable water is what makes the coverage
assumption below (\cref{ass:cover}) reasonable.

\begin{remark}[Truth in the chain]\label{rem:glorys-estimand}
GLORYS12 values are cell averages, so in the simulations $Z$ is constant on native $1/12^\circ$
cell-days and $\theta_y$ is an exact finite sum. That sum is the chain's estimand by definition; no
quadrature error enters the truth.
\end{remark}

\section{The sampling design as a point process}\label{sec:design}

\begin{assumption}[Deployment-law frame]\label{ass:frame}
The velocity $u$ and the field $Z$ are fixed. The profile locations are random only through the
deployments and any randomization added by the analyst (fold labels, \cref{sec:dr}).
\end{assumption}

A deployment $s$ is a launch position, launch time and lifespan. Given $u$, advection is a
deterministic map $s\mapsto\Phi_u(s)$, the finite set of the float's profile points (park-depth
trajectory, one profile per cycle, until its lifespan ends or it leaves the model domain).

\begin{assumption}[Independent deployments]\label{ass:deploy}
Deployments are grouped into cohorts $c$ (launch years). Cohort $c$ has a fixed number $N_c$ of
deployments, drawn independently from a law $\mathcal D_c$; different cohorts are independent.
\end{assumption}

In the chain this holds by construction (\cref{sec:qhat}); for the real array it is a model
(\cref{sec:real}).

\paragraph{Intensity.} The profile counting measure is $N=\sum_j\sum_{x\in\Phi_u(s_j)}\delta_x$, a
random locally finite counting measure on $\X$. Its intensity measure is $\Lambda(E)=\E N(E)$. For one
deployment, $\Lambda^{(1)}_c(E)=\E\,\#\{\Phi_u(s)\cap E\}$ with $s\sim\mathcal D_c$, and
$\Lambda=\sum_cN_c\Lambda^{(1)}_c$.

\begin{assumption}[Absolute continuity]\label{ass:ac}
$\Lambda$ is $\sigma$-finite and $\Lambda^{(1)}_c\ll\mu$ for every $c$.
\end{assumption}

The single-deployment intensity and the sampling intensity are the Radon--Nikodym derivatives
\[
  q^{(1)}_c=\frac{d\Lambda^{(1)}_c}{d\mu},\qquad q=\frac{d\Lambda}{d\mu}=\sum_cN_c\,q^{(1)}_c
  \quad(\text{profiles m}^{-2}\,\text{day}^{-1}),
\]
functionals of $(\mathcal D,u)$ alone. \Cref{ass:ac} needs a launch law with a density (the bootstrap
jitter of \cref{sec:qhat} supplies one); exactly repeated launches would put atoms on trajectories.

\begin{lemma}[Campbell; \citealp{daley2003}]\label{lem:campbell}
For measurable $f\ge0$, or $f\in L^1(\Lambda)$,
$\E\big[\sum_{x\in N}f(x)\big]=\int f\dd\Lambda=\int f\,q\dd\mu$.
\end{lemma}

The test function $f$ must not depend on the design. Every cross-fitting and independence device
below exists to meet this requirement.

\begin{remark}[Relation to preferential sampling]\label{rem:diggle}
\citet{diggle2010} call sampling preferential when field and point process are dependent,
$[Z,N]\neq[Z][N]$, with both random. Here the ocean is conditioned on, so $q$ is a fixed function and
preferentiality appears as alignment of $q$ with $Z$; the simple mean's bias is approximately
$\Cov_{\Prob_y}(q/\bar q_y,Z)$, with $\bar q_y=\Lambda(\X_y)/\mu(\X_y)$. The alignment arises because
$q$ depends on $u$, which is dynamically coupled to $Z$. Conditional statements imply unconditional
ones by the tower property; the conditional ones are those relevant to the one ocean we observe.
\end{remark}

\section{Linear estimators and their bias}\label{sec:linear}

A linear estimator is $\hat\theta=\sum_ia_iZ(x_i)$, with weights that may depend on the design but
not on the values. Its estimator measure is $\nu=\sum_ia_i\delta_{x_i}$, so $\hat\theta=\int Z\dd\nu$.

\begin{proposition}[Bias identity]\label{prop:linear}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item For every realization, $\hat\theta-\theta_y=\int Z\dd(\nu-\Prob_y)$.
  \item If $a_i=a(x_i)$ for a fixed measurable $a\ge0$ supported in $\X_y$, then $\E\nu$ has density
        $w=a\,q\,\mu(\X_y)$ with respect to $\Prob_y$ and
        $\E\hat\theta-\theta_y=\int Z(w-1)\dd\Prob_y=\Cov_{\Prob_y}(w,Z)+(\bar w-1)\theta_y$, with
        $\bar w=\int w\dd\Prob_y$.
  \item (Horvitz--Thompson.) With $a=\mathbf 1_{\{q>0\}}/(q\,\mu(\X_y))$ on $\X_y$,
        $\E\hat\theta_{\mathrm{HT}}-\theta_y=-\int_{\{q=0\}}Z\dd\Prob_y$, zero under positivity
        $\Prob_y\ll\Lambda$ \citep{horvitz1952,cordy1993}.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
(1) is the definition of $\nu$. (2) By \cref{lem:campbell}, $\E\int f\dd\nu=\int f\,a\,q\,\mu(\X_y)\dd\Prob_y$
for bounded measurable $f$, which identifies the density; take $f=Z$. (3) is (2) with
$w=\mathbf 1_{\{q>0\}}$.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{The map.} Let $\hat m:\X\to\R$ be a map built from the profiles (\cref{sec:outcome}) and
$Q_y(\hat m)=\sum_g\omega_g\hat m(x^*_g)$ its quadrature, $\sum_g\omega_g=1$, with remainder
$R^{\mathrm{quad}}=Q_y(\hat m)-\int\hat m\dd\Prob_y$. The map estimator $\hat\theta_{\mathrm K}=Q_y(\hat m)$
has, exactly,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:krige-error}
  \hat\theta_{\mathrm K}-\theta_y=-\int(Z-\hat m)\dd\Prob_y+R^{\mathrm{quad}} .
\end{equation}
Its bias is $-\int b\dd\Prob_y$ (plus $\E R^{\mathrm{quad}}$), with $b=Z-\E\hat m$ the pointwise bias of
the map. Nothing in the construction of $\hat m$ makes this vanish, and preferential sampling makes it
nonzero in three ways:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item the mean field is fitted to profiles whose locations covary with $Z$;
  \item simple kriging shrinks the anomaly to zero where data are sparse, so there the map reverts to
        that mean field;
  \item a misspecified covariance distorts the kriging weights (on GLORYS the exponential kernel's
        fitted ranges are inflated $1.4$--$2.3\times$).
\end{enumerate}

\section{The outcome model}\label{sec:outcome}

The map is $\hat m=\hat\mu+\hat\eta$.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{Mean field} \citep[eq.~5]{sukianto2026}. At each centre of a $1^\circ$ grid, ordinary least
        squares over the profiles in a $\pm5^\circ$ window fits a quadratic in position, six annual
        harmonics and a quadratic time trend. $\hat\mu$ at the centre is the fitted intercept,
        harmonics and trend, and is bilinear between centres.
  \item \emph{Anomaly.} $\hat\eta$ is simple kriging of $Z-\hat\mu$ with the locally stationary
        anisotropic exponential covariance of \citet{kuusela2018},
        $k(x,x')=\phi\,e^{-d(x,x')}+\tau^2\mathbf 1\{x=x'\}$,
        $d^2=(\Delta\mathrm{lon}/\theta_{\mathrm{lon}})^2+(\Delta\mathrm{lat}/\theta_{\mathrm{lat}})^2+(\Delta t/\theta_t)^2$,
        parameters fixed per $2^\circ$ cell, conditioning on profiles within $\pm10^\circ$ and
        $\pm180$\,d.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{The map as a linear smoother.} With the covariance parameters fixed, both steps are linear
in the profile values $Z_l=Z(x_l)$, $l\in J$, with weights that depend on the positions only. The
mean field is $\hat\mu(x)=\sum_l\ell_l(x)Z_l$, where $\ell(x)$ collects the local least-squares hat
weights at the surrounding centres and their bilinear interpolation. Simple kriging gives
$\hat\eta(x)=\sum_lw_l(x)\{Z_l-\hat\mu(x_l)\}$ with
\[
  w(x)^\top=c(x)^\top(K+\tau^2I)^{-1},\qquad c_l(x)=\phi\,e^{-d(x,x_l)},\quad K_{lm}=\phi\,e^{-d(x_l,x_m)},
\]
using the parameters $(\phi,\theta,\tau^2)$ of the $2^\circ$ cell containing $x$ and the profiles in that
cell's conditioning window ($w_l(x)=0$ outside it). The nugget enters only the data covariance: $c(x)$
omits it, so $\hat\eta$ predicts the smooth component, does not interpolate the profiles, and $\tau^2$
sets how strongly it shrinks toward $\hat\mu$. Here $Z$ is exact, so $\tau^2$ is a working parameter for
variability unresolved at the covariance ranges, not measurement error. Hence
\[
  \hat m(x)=\sum_{l\in J}h_l(x)\,Z_l,\qquad h(x)^\top=\ell(x)^\top+w(x)^\top(I-L),\quad L_{lm}=\ell_m(x_l),
\]
and the map estimator is a weighted sum of the profiles,
\[
  \hat\theta_{\mathrm K}=Q_y(\hat m)=\sum_{l\in J}a_lZ_l,\qquad a_l=\sum_g\omega_g\,h_l(x^*_g).
\]
If every local fit has full rank, the mean field reproduces constants ($\sum_l\ell_l(x)=1$, since the
model has an intercept) and the kriged anomaly of a constant is zero, so $\sum_lh_l(x)=1$ and
$\sum_la_l=1$: the map is a weighted mean of the profiles, with weights that can be negative.
\Cref{prop:linear}(1) then gives its error exactly, $\hat\theta_{\mathrm K}-\theta_y=\int Z\dd(\nu_{\mathrm K}-\Prob_y)$
with $\nu_{\mathrm K}=\sum_la_l\delta_{x_l}$. Part (2) does not apply, because $a_l$ depends on the
whole configuration, not on $x_l$ alone. The weights are set by geometry, not by the sampling
intensity: within a $\pm5^\circ$ window every profile counts equally in the mean-field fit, so where
floats crowd, the crowded water dominates; $(K+\tau^2I)^{-1}$ declusters only within the covariance
ranges. This is mechanism~1 above.

\paragraph{What the theory needs from the map.} One property only: $\hat m_{-k}$ is a measurable
function of the profiles (positions and values) of floats outside fold $k$ and of design-fixed
quantities. Its form is otherwise free, and the DR guarantees do not require it to be correct.

\begin{remark}[Covariance parameters must be fold-external]\label{rem:cov-params}
If the covariance parameters are fitted to all profiles, $\hat m_{-k}$ depends on fold $k$ and
\cref{thm:bias} is no longer exact (the effect is small, since one fold moves the fit little). Exact
options: parameters fixed externally (the oracle fit to the full GLORYS field, unavailable in
practice) or refitted per fold. The mean field is already refitted per fold.
\end{remark}

\section{The doubly robust estimator}\label{sec:dr}

\subsection{Folds and the fold intensity}

Each float gets a label $L_j\in\{1,\dots,K\}$ by a uniform random permutation within its cohort, so
fold $k$ holds $\lfloor N_c/K\rfloor$ or $\lceil N_c/K\rceil$ cohort-$c$ floats; the labels depend only on the
fixed $N_c$ and are independent of everything else.
Let $N_k$ count fold-$k$ profiles, and let $\mathcal G_{-k}$ be the $\sigma$-field of all labels, the
deployments and profiles of floats outside fold $k$, and the design-fixed quantities (intensity
estimates, quadrature, external covariance parameters). Then $\hat m_{-k}$ and the fold-cohort counts
$n_{k,c}=\#\{j:L_j=k,\,c_j=c\}$ are $\mathcal G_{-k}$-measurable.

\begin{lemma}[Campbell for a fold]\label{lem:fold-campbell}
Under \cref{ass:frame,ass:deploy,ass:ac}, $\Lambda_{k|-k}(E)=\E[N_k(E)\mid\mathcal G_{-k}]$ has density
\[
  q_{k|-k}=\sum_cn_{k,c}\,q^{(1)}_c ,
\]
and $\E\big[\sum_{x\in N_k}f(x)\mid\mathcal G_{-k}\big]=\int f\,q_{k|-k}\dd\mu$ for every
$\mathcal G_{-k}\otimes\B(\X)$-measurable $f\ge0$ (or with $\E\int|f|\dd\Lambda_{k|-k}<\infty$).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Given the labels, fold-$k$ deployments are independent of the others (\cref{ass:deploy}) and the flow
is fixed (\cref{ass:frame}); so given $\mathcal G_{-k}$ the fold-$k$ floats are independent, $n_{k,c}$
of them drawn from $\mathcal D_c$. Apply \cref{lem:campbell} to each under the regular conditional law
($\X$ is Polish) and sum; a $\mathcal G_{-k}$-measurable $f$ is fixed under that law.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Fold intensity estimate.} With design-fixed estimates $\hat q^{(1)}_c$ (\cref{sec:qhat}),
the \emph{cohort-matched} fold intensity is $\hat q_k=\sum_cn_{k,c}\hat q^{(1)}_c$. It is
$\mathcal G_{-k}$-measurable and targets $q_{k|-k}$ exactly. Its support is the same as the full
array's: $\hat q^{(1)}_c$ is the intensity of one random draw from the whole cohort pool, and every fold
holds about $N_c/K$ floats of every cohort.

\begin{remark}[Why match cohorts]\label{rem:cohort}
The simpler $\hat q/K$ targets $\E q_{k|-k}=q/K$. With unstratified labels $n_{k,c}$ fluctuates around
$N_c/K$, so $\hat q/K$ carries a ratio error $K\sum_cn_{k,c}q^{(1)}_c/q$ that varies in space wherever
the cohort mix does, of relative size about $(N_c/K)^{-1/2}$. Stratifying the labels by cohort reduces
the fluctuation to rounding, and matching cohorts removes what is left, both at no cost.
\end{remark}

\begin{assumption}[Simulated coverage]\label{ass:cover}
There is $q_0>0$ with $\hat q_k\ge q_0$ on $\X_y$ for every $k$.
\end{assumption}

Fold-$k$ profiles get weight $v_i=1/\hat q_k(x_i)\le1/q_0$, which with bounded $Z$ and $\hat m$ gives the
integrability \cref{lem:fold-campbell} needs, and the intensity ratio is
\[
  \lambda_k=\frac{q_{k|-k}}{\hat q_k}\quad\text{on }\X_y ,
\]
with $\lambda_k\equiv1$ if every $\hat q^{(1)}_c$ is exact. \Cref{ass:cover} concerns $\hat q_k$ only, so
it is checked directly on the simulation (report $\min_{\X_y}\hat q_k$ relative to its mean). With a
Gaussian kernel $\hat q_k>0$ wherever a replicate profile passes within a few bandwidths, so the content of
the assumption is the size of $q_0$, which sets the largest weight. It is stronger than
\cref{def:domain}, which bounds only annual means; where it fails, $A^*$ is shrunk (larger $q_A$) or the
kernel widened, not the weights altered. It does not assume
real floats reach every point: on real data $q_{k|-k}$ may be small or zero where $\hat q_k$ is not, and
that is a weight error, $\lambda_k<1$, entering the product term of \cref{thm:bias}.

\subsection{Estimator}

Let $I_k$ index fold-$k$ profiles in $\X_y$, and define
\[
  \hat A_k=\frac1{\mu(\X_y)}\sum_{i\in I_k}v_ie_k(x_i),\qquad
  \hat\Delta_k=\frac1{\mu(\X_y)}\sum_{i\in I_k}v_i .
\]
$\hat A_k$ is the Horvitz--Thompson estimate of the map's error $\int e_k\dd\Prob_y$, and $\hat\Delta_k$
estimates the total mass $1$ (exactly, in expectation, when $\lambda_k\equiv1$). The fold estimate and
the DR estimator are
\[
  \hat\theta_k=Q_y(\hat m_{-k})+\hat C_k,\qquad \hat C_k=\frac{\hat A_k}{\hat\Delta_k}
  \ (0\text{ if }I_k=\varnothing),\qquad
  \hat\theta_{\mathrm{DR},y}=\frac1K\sum_{k=1}^K\hat\theta_k ,
\]
a fold average (DML1, \citealp{chernozhukov2018}). The correction $\hat C_k$ is the Hájek
(self-normalized) weighted mean of the held-out residuals. The Horvitz--Thompson variant
$\tilde\theta_k=Q_y(\hat m_{-k})+\hat A_k$ is used only in the proofs.

\begin{proposition}[Hájek is DR with a constant map]\label{prop:hajek-is-dr}
If $\hat m_{-k}\equiv m_0$, then $\hat\theta_k=\sum_{I_k}v_iZ(x_i)/\sum_{I_k}v_i$ for every $m_0$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
$Q_y(m_0)=m_0$ and $\sum v_i(Z_i-m_0)/\sum v_i=\sum v_iZ_i/\sum v_i-m_0$.
\end{proof}

So DR differs from the Hájek estimator in one respect: at the same weights, the constant outcome model
is replaced by the map.

\begin{remark}[DR as a linear estimator]\label{rem:dr-linear}
With $\hat m_{-k}=\sum_{l\in J_{-k}}h^{(-k)}_lZ_l$ the smoother fitted to the out-of-fold profiles
$J_{-k}$, $a^{(-k)}_l$ its quadrature weights and $\bar v_i=v_i/\sum_{I_k}v_{i'}$,
\[
  \hat\theta_k=\sum_{i\in I_k}\bar v_iZ_i+\sum_{l\in J_{-k}}\Big(a^{(-k)}_l-\sum_{i\in I_k}\bar v_i\,h^{(-k)}_l(x_i)\Big)Z_l
  =\int Z\dd\nu_{\mathrm H,k}+\Big(Q_y(\hat m_{-k})-\int\hat m_{-k}\dd\nu_{\mathrm H,k}\Big).
\]
So DR is also linear in the values, with weights set by positions and $\hat q_k$. Read one way, it is
the map plus a weighted mean of its held-out residuals. Read the other way, it is Hájek on the held-out
profiles plus an adjustment: the map's average over the domain minus its average over the held-out
sites, which corrects for where the held-out floats happened to be. If the map reproduces constants, the
out-of-fold weights sum to zero, so that adjustment is a contrast and the total weight is one.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Exact error}

\begin{theorem}[Realized error]\label{thm:realized}
For every realization, intensity estimate and map with $I_k\neq\varnothing$, with
$\nu_{\mathrm H,k}=\sum_{I_k}v_i\delta_{x_i}/\sum_{I_k}v_i$,
\[
  \hat\theta_k-\theta_y
  =\underbrace{\int e_k\dd\nu_{\mathrm H,k}-\int e_k\dd\Prob_y}_{\text{error of the correction}}\;+\;R^{\mathrm{quad}}_k
  =\int e_k\dd(\nu_{\mathrm H,k}-\Prob_y)+R^{\mathrm{quad}}_k .
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
$\theta_y=\int\hat m_{-k}\dd\Prob_y+\int e_k\dd\Prob_y$ and
$\hat\theta_k=\int\hat m_{-k}\dd\Prob_y+R^{\mathrm{quad}}_k+\int e_k\dd\nu_{\mathrm H,k}$.
\end{proof}

By \eqref{eq:krige-error} the map's error is $-\int e\dd\Prob_y$. DR replaces it by the error of an
estimate of it, i.e.\ by the discrepancy between the weighted held-out profiles and the target measure,
integrated against the map's residual. This is algebra and holds for any array, simulated or real.

\subsection{Conditional bias}

Assume $\bar\lambda_k:=\int\lambda_k\dd\Prob_y>0$, and let $\bar e_k=\int e_k\dd\Prob_y$ and
$r_k=\int e_k\lambda_k\dd\Prob_y/\bar\lambda_k$.

\begin{theorem}[Conditional bias]\label{thm:bias}
Under \cref{ass:frame,ass:deploy,ass:ac,ass:cover},
\[
  \E[\hat\theta_k-\theta_y\mid\mathcal G_{-k}]
  =\underbrace{\frac1{\bar\lambda_k}\int(e_k-\bar e_k)(\lambda_k-\bar\lambda_k)\dd\Prob_y}_{\beta^{\mathrm{prod}}_k}
  \;+\;R^{\mathrm{quad}}_k+R^{\mathrm{ratio}}_k ,
\]
where $R^{\mathrm{ratio}}_k=\E[\hat C_k\mid\mathcal G_{-k}]-r_k$. On $\{\hat\Delta_k>0\}$ the exact
identity
\[
  \frac{\hat A_k}{\hat\Delta_k}-r_k=\frac{\hat A_k-r_k\hat\Delta_k}{\bar\lambda_k}
  -\frac{\hat\Delta_k-\bar\lambda_k}{\bar\lambda_k}\Big(\frac{\hat A_k}{\hat\Delta_k}-r_k\Big)
\]
holds, and its first term has conditional mean zero, so $R^{\mathrm{ratio}}_k$ comes from the second
term only, a covariance of two sample means (by the delta method, of order one over the number of
fold-$k$ floats that reach $\X_y$). For the Horvitz--Thompson variant, exactly,
$\E[\tilde\theta_k-\theta_y\mid\mathcal G_{-k}]=\int e_k(\lambda_k-1)\dd\Prob_y+R^{\mathrm{quad}}_k$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
\Cref{lem:fold-campbell} with the $\mathcal G_{-k}$-measurable test functions
$e_k\mathbf 1_{\X_y}/(\hat q_k\mu(\X_y))$ and $\mathbf 1_{\X_y}/(\hat q_k\mu(\X_y))$ gives
$\E[\hat A_k\mid\mathcal G_{-k}]=\int e_k\lambda_k\dd\Prob_y$ and
$\E[\hat\Delta_k\mid\mathcal G_{-k}]=\bar\lambda_k$; the HT statement follows as in \cref{thm:realized}.
For the Hájek form, $r_k-\int e_k\dd\Prob_y=\bar\lambda_k^{-1}\int e_k(\lambda_k-\bar\lambda_k)\dd\Prob_y$,
and $e_k$ may be centred because $\int(\lambda_k-\bar\lambda_k)\dd\Prob_y=0$. The ratio identity
follows by expanding its right side, and $\E[\hat A_k-r_k\hat\Delta_k\mid\mathcal G_{-k}]=0$ by the
definition of $r_k$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Double robustness and product bound]\label{cor:dr}
$\beta^{\mathrm{prod}}_k=0$ if either $\lambda_k$ is constant on $\X_y$ (intensity right up to scale) or
$e_k$ is constant on $\X_y$ (map right up to a constant). In general, with moments under $\Prob_y$,
\[
  \beta^{\mathrm{prod}}_k=\sd(e_k)\,\mathrm{cv}(\lambda_k)\,\mathrm{Corr}(e_k,\lambda_k),
  \qquad
  |\beta^{\mathrm{prod}}_k|\le\sd(e_k)\,\mathrm{cv}(\lambda_k),
\]
with $\mathrm{cv}=\sd/\text{mean}$. The bound is a deterministic worst case over alignment.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
$\beta^{\mathrm{prod}}_k=\Cov_{\Prob_y}(e_k,\lambda_k)/\bar\lambda_k$; then Cauchy--Schwarz.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Reading the bias.} The bias left by DR is (map scatter) $\times$ (relative intensity error)
$\times$ (their correlation). Every weight error enters here, including water the simulation reaches but
real floats do not ($\lambda_k=0$). The
correlation is not guaranteed to be small: flow error and map error both live mostly at the mesoscale,
and their alignment on real data is an empirical question (\cref{sec:real}).

\begin{remark}[The chain]\label{rem:chain-bias}
In the chain $\mathcal D_c$ is known, so $\lambda_k-1$ is only the smoothing, Monte Carlo and Jensen
error of $\hat q^{(1)}_c$ (\cref{sec:qhat}). As replicates grow and the bandwidth shrinks,
$\lambda_k\to1$ and the bias reduces to the remainders.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Changes and contrasts]\label{rem:changes}
For $\sum_y\gamma_y\theta_y$, replace $\Prob_y$ by $\sum_y\gamma_y\Prob_y$. With per-year Hájek
normalization the change estimate is the difference of yearly DR estimates, and its bias is the
difference of yearly biases, so a bias common to both years cancels.
\end{remark}

\section{Variance}\label{sec:variance}

Given $\mathcal G_{-k}$ the map is fixed, so $\Var(\hat\theta_k\mid\mathcal G_{-k})=\mathcal V_k:=\Var(\hat C_k\mid\mathcal G_{-k})$.
For fold-$k$ float $j$, define per-float totals
\[
  a_j=\frac1{\mu(\X_y)}\sum_{x\in\Phi_u(s_j)\cap\X_y}\frac{e_k(x)}{\hat q_k(x)},\qquad
  d_j=\frac1{\mu(\X_y)}\sum_{x\in\Phi_u(s_j)\cap\X_y}\frac1{\hat q_k(x)},
\]
so $\hat A_k=\sum_{L_j=k}a_j$ and $\hat\Delta_k=\sum_{L_j=k}d_j$ (floats that never reach $\X_y$
contribute zeros and stay in the sums). Let $M^{(2)}_c$ be the second factorial moment measure of one
cohort-$c$ float's profile set,
$M^{(2)}_c(E\times E')=\E\,\#\{(x,x')\in\Phi_u(s)^2:x\neq x',x\in E,x'\in E'\}$, which counts pairs of
distinct profiles of the \emph{same} float, and let $\rho_k$ be the expected number
of fold-$k$ profiles per unit $\Prob_y$-mass under $\hat q_k$, $\rho_k=\hat q_k\,\mu(\X_y)$.

\begin{proposition}[Variance of the correction]\label{prop:var}
Under the assumptions of \cref{thm:bias}, let $\tilde e_k=e_k-r_k$ and $f=\tilde e_k\mathbf 1_{\X_y}/(\hat q_k\,\mu(\X_y))$.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item (Exact.)
  \[
    \Var\big(\hat A_k-r_k\hat\Delta_k\mid\mathcal G_{-k}\big)
    =\sum_cn_{k,c}\Big[\int f^2\dd\Lambda^{(1)}_c+\iint f(x)f(x')\,M^{(2)}_c(dx,dx')-\Big(\int f\dd\Lambda^{(1)}_c\Big)^2\Big],
  \]
  and the first terms sum to $\int\tilde e_k^{\,2}\lambda_k/\rho_k\dd\Prob_y$. Write
  $\mathrm{deff}_k$ for the whole sum divided by this first part.
  \item (Delta method.) The first term of the ratio identity of \cref{thm:bias} gives
  \[
    \mathcal V_k\approx\frac{\mathrm{deff}_k}{\bar\lambda_k^{\,2}}\int\frac{\tilde e_k^{\,2}\lambda_k}{\rho_k}\dd\Prob_y .
  \]
  \item (Estimator; $n_{k,c}\ge2$.) With $u_j=a_j-\hat r_kd_j$, $\hat r_k=\hat A_k/\hat\Delta_k$ and $\bar u_{k,c}$ the
  mean of $u_j$ over fold-$k$ floats of cohort $c$,
  \[
    \hat{\mathcal V}_k=\frac1{\hat\Delta_k^2}\sum_c\frac{n_{k,c}}{n_{k,c}-1}\sum_{L_j=k,\,c_j=c}(u_j-\bar u_{k,c})^2 .
  \]
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
$\hat A_k-r_k\hat\Delta_k=\sum_{L_j=k}S_j$ with $S_j=\sum_{x\in\Phi_u(s_j)}f(x)$, and $r_k$ is
$\mathcal G_{-k}$-measurable. Given $\mathcal G_{-k}$ the $S_j$ are independent, i.i.d.\ within cohort
(\cref{lem:fold-campbell}), so the variance is $\sum_cn_{k,c}\Var_c(S)$. Expand
$\E_cS^2=\E_c\sum_xf^2+\E_c\sum_{x\neq x'}f(x)f(x')$ and use \cref{lem:campbell} and the definition of
$M^{(2)}_c$; $\E_cS=\int f\dd\Lambda^{(1)}_c$. For the first part,
$\sum_cn_{k,c}q^{(1)}_c=\lambda_k\hat q_k$ and $d\mu/\mu(\X_y)=d\Prob_y$ on $\X_y$. (3) replaces each
cohort variance by its sample variance.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Reading the variance.}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \emph{Only a float's own profiles add correlation.} The field is fixed, so different floats are
        independent: two nearby floats have correlated residuals as numbers, but not as random
        variables over re-runs of the deployments. The pair term is along-track: consecutive profiles
        inside the same anomaly repeat one piece of information. Heuristically, $\mathrm{deff}_k\approx1+{}$
        the number of a float's other profiles within the residual decorrelation scale; a float that
        spends two months in one eddy is about one observation of it, not six.
  \item \emph{Sparse water dominates.} The integrand is $\tilde e^{\,2}/\rho$, so residuals where few
        profiles were expected carry large weights. The map tends to be worst where floats are scarce,
        and that is also where its check is noisiest; $q_0$ bounds this.
  \item \emph{Ideal allocation.} With $\lambda_k=1$ and fixed $\int\rho\dd\Prob_y$, Cauchy--Schwarz gives
        \[
          \int\frac{\tilde e^{\,2}}{\rho}\dd\Prob_y\ge\frac{(\int|\tilde e|\dd\Prob_y)^2}{\int\rho\dd\Prob_y},
        \]
        with equality iff $\rho\propto|\tilde e|$ (Neyman allocation). Floats crowding into energetic
        water, where residuals are large, help the variance even though they bias the naive mean.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Combining folds (approximation).} Fold estimates are dependent ($\hat m_{-k}$ uses fold $k'$,
whose correction uses fold $k'$'s design). The working estimate $\hat{\mathcal V}=K^{-2}\sum_k\hat{\mathcal V}_k$
ignores these covariances and the variance of the conditional bias, which is second order when the
bias is small. This is a heuristic, checked by the coverage of $\hat\theta_{\mathrm{DR},y}\pm1.96\,\hat{\mathcal V}^{1/2}$
over chain replicates (\cref{sec:validation}). The interval is design-based, conditional on the ocean,
nominally $95\%$, asymptotic, and valid only up to the bias terms of \cref{thm:bias}.

\paragraph{Several years.} A float alive in two years enters both corrections; for contrasts, form
per-float totals with the contrast weights before taking variances.

\section{DR versus the map: bias, variance and MSE}\label{sec:gain}

This section compares $\hat\theta_{\mathrm{DR},y}$ with the map $\hat\theta_{\mathrm K}=\int\hat m\dd\Prob_y$
and says when DR is better. \Cref{prop:mse} is exact; the headline \eqref{eq:headline} is an
approximation. Quadrature is taken as exact here; otherwise it adds the same term to both.

\paragraph{The idea.} The map's error has two parts: a \emph{systematic offset} (e.g.\ a mean field
that runs cold wherever floats are sparse, because it was fitted to floats crowding into cold water)
and \emph{local scatter} (misplaced eddies, positive here and negative there). DR checks the map against
held-out floats and subtracts the weighted average discrepancy. This removes the offset, but the check
is a sample average of residuals, so it inherits their scatter as noise; with wrong weights it removes
the offset only partly. DR pays when the offset removed exceeds the noise added. This is the
survey-sampling contrast between model-based estimation, best when the model is right, and
model-assisted estimation, which protects against a wrong model at a cost in variance
\citep{hansen1983,sarndal1992}.

\subsection{Exact decomposition}

With $e_k=Z-\hat m_{-k}$ and $e=Z-\hat m$ (full-data map), let $T_k=\int e_k\dd\Prob_y$ and
$T=\int e\dd\Prob_y$ be the fold map's and the full map's integrated error. Decompose the correction as
\[
  \hat C_k=T_k+\beta_k+\xi_k,\qquad \beta_k=\E[\hat C_k\mid\mathcal G_{-k}]-T_k=\beta^{\mathrm{prod}}_k+R^{\mathrm{ratio}}_k,
  \qquad\E[\xi_k\mid\mathcal G_{-k}]=0 ,
\]
so $\beta_k$ is what the correction gets wrong on average and $\xi_k$ its noise, with
$\E[\xi_k^2\mid\mathcal G_{-k}]=\mathcal V_k$.

\begin{proposition}[Error and MSE]\label{prop:mse}
If $\E\hat C_k^2<\infty$, then exactly
\begin{align*}
  \hat\theta_{\mathrm K}-\theta_y&=-T, & \mathrm{MSE}_{\mathrm K}&=\E T^2=(\E T)^2+\Var T,\\
  \hat\theta_k-\theta_y&=\beta_k+\xi_k, & \mathrm{MSE}_{\mathrm{DR},k}&=\E\beta_k^2+\E\mathcal V_k ,
\end{align*}
and $\mathrm{MSE}_{\mathrm{DR}}\le K^{-1}\sum_k\mathrm{MSE}_{\mathrm{DR},k}$. Hence DR has the smaller MSE
whenever
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mse-sufficient}
  \E T^2>\frac1K\sum_k\big\{\E\beta_k^2+\E\mathcal V_k\big\}.
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The error identities are \eqref{eq:krige-error} and \cref{thm:realized} in the new symbols. The cross
term vanishes: $\E[\beta_k\xi_k]=\E[\beta_k\E(\xi_k\mid\mathcal G_{-k})]=0$, since $\beta_k$ is
$\mathcal G_{-k}$-measurable. The fold bound is $(K^{-1}\sum a_k)^2\le K^{-1}\sum a_k^2$.
\end{proof}

The map pays $T$, an error that exists but is never measured; DR pays instead $\beta_k$ and $\xi_k$.
Comparing with the full map ($T_k\approx T$; an approximation, good for moderate $K$),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mse-diff}
  \mathrm{MSE}_{\mathrm K}-\mathrm{MSE}_{\mathrm{DR},k}\approx
  \underbrace{\E T^2}_{\text{removed}}-\underbrace{\E\beta_k^2}_{\text{left over}}
  -\underbrace{\E\mathcal V_k}_{\text{added noise}} .
\end{equation}
Restricting to the reachable domain is what makes this clean: with unreachable water inside $\X_y$,
a gap term would be shared by both estimators and its interaction with $T$ could make DR lose even with
exact weights.

\subsection{Size of each term}

Let $\bar e=\int e\dd\Prob_y$ (the map's mean error), $\sigma_e=\sd_{\Prob_y}(e)$ (its scatter), and define
the effective number of profiles by $\sigma_e^2/n_{\mathrm{eff}}=\int\tilde e_k^{\,2}\lambda_k/\rho_k\dd\Prob_y$.
For uniform $\rho$, $\lambda=1$ and $\tilde e$ of variance $\sigma_e^2$, $n_{\mathrm{eff}}$ is the expected
number of fold-$k$ profiles in $\X_y$; uneven $\rho$ lowers it. Then:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{removed:} $T=\bar e$, exactly. It need not shrink as floats are added: a mean field
        fitted to preferentially placed profiles converges to the wrong limit;
  \item \emph{left over:} $\beta^{\mathrm{prod}}_k=\sigma_e\,\mathrm{cv}(\lambda)\,\mathrm{Corr}(e_k,\lambda_k)$,
        exactly (\cref{cor:dr}); set by the design model, it does not shrink with more floats either;
  \item \emph{added:} $\mathcal V_k\approx\sigma_e^2\,\mathrm{deff}_k/(\bar\lambda_k^2n_{\mathrm{eff}})$
        (\cref{prop:var}), which does;
  \item \emph{ratio remainder:} of order $1/n_{\mathrm{eff}}$, so its square and its cross term with
        $\beta^{\mathrm{prod}}_k$ are negligible beside $\mathcal V_k$ when $\mathrm{cv}(\lambda)|\mathrm{Corr}|\ll1$.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{When DR improves on the map}

Approximate further: $\bar\lambda_k\approx1$, and $\sigma_e$, $\mathrm{deff}$, $n_{\mathrm{eff}}$ non-random.
Dividing \eqref{eq:mse-diff} by $\sigma_e^2$, DR has the smaller MSE if and only if, to this order,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:headline}
  \boxed{\;
  \underbrace{\frac{\E\bar e^{\,2}}{\sigma_e^2}}_{\substack{\text{map offset relative}\\\text{to its scatter}}}
  >\underbrace{\mathrm{cv}(\lambda)^2\,\mathrm{Corr}(e,\lambda)^2}_{\substack{\text{design-model error}\\\text{aligned with map error}}}
  +\underbrace{\frac{\mathrm{deff}}{n_{\mathrm{eff}}}}_{\text{sampling noise}}\;}
\end{equation}
This is a heuristic summary of \cref{prop:mse}, in units of the map's scatter: the left side is what
can be gained, the right side what it costs.

\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}p{5.6cm}p{4.0cm}p{4.4cm}@{}}
\toprule
Situation & Terms in \eqref{eq:headline} & Outcome\\
\midrule
Map with a systematic offset, many floats & left large, noise small & DR wins\\
Map unbiased on average, locally noisy & left $\approx0$ & DR loses: pays noise, gains nothing\\
Small target (box-month) & $n_{\mathrm{eff}}$ small & map wins unless the offset is large\\
Large target (basin-year), same map & $n_{\mathrm{eff}}$ large & DR favoured\\
Design-model error aligned with map error & middle term $\gtrsim$ left & no gain in bias\\
Extreme weights (small $q_0$) & $n_{\mathrm{eff}}\downarrow$, $\mathrm{cv}(\lambda)\uparrow$ & DR worse than either part \citep{kang2007}\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\paragraph{How the dials act.} A \emph{better map} lowers $\sigma_e$, shrinking both the leftover bias and
the noise, but usually lowers $\bar e$ too; the ratio $\bar e/\sigma_e$ decides, and DR helps most when
the map's error is a smooth offset rather than local noise. \emph{More floats}, or a \emph{larger
target}, shrink only $\mathrm{deff}/n_{\mathrm{eff}}$, so eventually offset versus design-model
alignment decides alone; this is why the basin-year target is favourable. A \emph{higher} $q_A$ drops thinly reached water,
raising $q_0$ and $n_{\mathrm{eff}}$ at the cost of a smaller estimand.

\subsection{A two-region example}\label{sec:two-region}

A stylized illustration (heuristic). The domain is a sparse region $S$ of
$\Prob_y$-mass $p$ and a dense region of mass $1-p$. The map is offset by $\delta$ in $S$, with scatter
$\sigma_S$ there, and is exact in the dense region; $n_S$ fold profiles are expected in $S$ and many in
the dense region. The map's error is $T=p\delta$.

\paragraph{Correct weights: how many floats?} With $\lambda=1$, DR is unbiased up to $O(1/n)$, and its
variance comes from $S$, where the centred residual is $\delta(1-p)$ plus scatter:
$\mathcal V\approx\mathrm{deff}\,p^2\{\sigma_S^2+\delta^2(1-p)^2\}/n_S$. DR wins iff $p^2\delta^2>\mathcal V$, i.e.
\[
  \frac{n_S}{\mathrm{deff}}>(1-p)^2+\frac{\sigma_S^2}{\delta^2}
  \qquad\Big(\text{sufficient: }\frac{n_S}{\mathrm{deff}}>1+\frac{\sigma_S^2}{\delta^2}\Big).
\]
$n_S/\mathrm{deff}$ is roughly the number of independent float visits to $S$. If the offset equals the
scatter, two suffice; if it is a tenth of the scatter, about a hundred are needed. A large coherent map
error is cheap to remove; a subtle one is not.

\paragraph{Wrong weights: how wrong can they be?} Let $\lambda=\lambda_S$ on $S$ and $1$ elsewhere
($\lambda_S<1$: $\hat q$ too high in $S$, weights too small). The correction then targets
$p\lambda_S\delta/(p\lambda_S+1-p)$, so DR removes the fraction $\lambda_S/(p\lambda_S+1-p)$ of the
offset, and
\[
  |b_{\mathrm{DR}}|<|b_{\mathrm K}|\iff(1-p)|\lambda_S-1|<p\lambda_S+1-p\iff\lambda_S<\frac{2(1-p)}{1-2p}
  \quad(p<\tfrac12),
\]
a threshold near $2$ for a small region. Under-weighting $S$ never makes DR's bias worse than the map's;
it only removes less (half the offset at $\lambda_S=\tfrac12$). Over-weighting by up to about two still
helps; beyond that DR over-corrects past the map's bias. So the dangerous direction is a too-narrow simulated spread, which makes $\hat q$ too small where real
floats stray ($\lambda>1$), as in the earlier toy experiments. Rule of thumb: err toward wider, flatter intensity estimates.

\paragraph{By-product: a test of the map.} $\hat C_k$ estimates the map's error $T_k$, biased only
by $\beta_k$, so $\hat C_k/\hat{\mathcal V}_k^{1/2}$ is a data-based test of map bias, worth reporting
even when the map is the reported estimator.

\section{Estimating the intensity by simulation}\label{sec:qhat}

\paragraph{Deployment law.} $\mathcal D_c$ is a year-stratified smoothed bootstrap of the real launches:
cohort $c$ draws $N_c$ launches uniformly with replacement from the real launches of year $c$ (position,
date and lifespan together), and jitters each by independent uniforms of $\pm1/12^\circ$ in latitude and
longitude (the latter divided by $\cos\varphi$) and $\pm10$\,d in time, redrawing positions off deep
water. One draw has the year's empirical launch measure convolved with a uniform kernel truncated to
deep water. The jitter is a smoothing bandwidth giving $\mathcal D_c$ a density, not a model of
trajectory error.

\paragraph{Replicates.} Replicates $r=1,\dots,R$ draw deployments, advect them at park depth in GLORYS
(Parcels, RK4) and record profiles every $10$\,d. For \cref{lem:campbell} to apply, the replicates used
for $\hat q$ must be independent of the design being weighted: in the chain, disjoint from the evaluated
replicates. (Weighting each replicate with a $\hat q$ that includes it shifted Hájek by $-0.001$ to
$-0.02$ GJ\,m$^{-2}$.)

\paragraph{Kernel estimator.} On native cells $E$ (GLORYS cell $\times$ day), let
\[
  \bar N_c(E)=\frac1{RN_c}\sum_{r=1}^R\sum_{j:\,c_j=c}\#\{\Phi_u(s_j^{(r)})\cap E\},
\]
so $\E\bar N_c(E)=\Lambda^{(1)}_c(E)$ by \cref{lem:campbell}. With $W_h$ a separable Gaussian kernel (standard deviations $\sigma_s$ in space,
$\sigma_t$ in time) and $\mu_{\mathcal S}(E)=\mu(E\cap\mathcal S)$ the measure of the float support
$\mathcal S$ (deep enough to park, inside the velocity domain and run window),
\[
  \hat q^{(1)}_c=\frac{W_h*\bar N_c}{W_h*\mu_{\mathcal S}}
\]
\citep{diggle1985}. Dividing by the kernel mass inside the support removes the downward bias at coasts
and domain edges. So far $\sigma_s=1/6^\circ$ and $\sigma_t=4$\,d, fixed a priori.

\paragraph{Error.} The denominator is deterministic, so $\E\hat q^{(1)}_c=\bar q_{c,h}:=(W_h*\Lambda^{(1)}_c)/(W_h*\mu_{\mathcal S})$
exactly, and $\hat q^{(1)}_c-q^{(1)}_c$ is smoothing bias $\bar q_{c,h}-q^{(1)}_c$ ($O(h^2)$ in the
interior and $O(h)$ near $\partial\mathcal S$, for $q^{(1)}_c\in C^2$) plus Monte Carlo error (variance
$O(\kappa_h\,q/(R\,h_s^2h_t))$, with $\kappa_h\ge1$ the over-dispersion from one float's consecutive
profiles sharing a kernel). Weights use the reciprocal. With $\epsilon=(\hat q-q)/q$, the identity
$q/\hat q=1-\epsilon+\epsilon^2q/\hat q$ is exact, so
\[
  \E\frac{q}{\hat q}-1=-\frac{\bar q_h-q}{q}+\E\Big[\epsilon^2\frac{q}{\hat q}\Big],
\]
a signed smoothing term plus a nonnegative Jensen term of the order of the relative Monte Carlo
variance, $O(\kappa_h/(R\,q\,h_s^2h_t))$. Only the variation
of $\lambda_k$ over $\X_y$ matters (\cref{cor:dr}); a global scale error cancels.

\section{The deployed array}\label{sec:real}

For real Argo, \cref{thm:realized} holds as stated, and \cref{thm:bias} holds with
$\lambda_k=q^{\mathrm{true}}_{k|-k}/\hat q_k$, $q^{\mathrm{true}}=q(\mathcal D^{\mathrm{true}},u^{\mathrm{true}})$,
plus a dependence remainder. The residual bias is thus a product of map error and simulated-design
error, which has five sources (the first expected to dominate):
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \emph{Flow.} GLORYS-advected copies of real floats separate from them by a median of $53$\,km at
        $10$\,d, $135$\,km at $30$\,d and $589$\,km at one year.
  \item \emph{Deployment law.} $\mathcal D_c$ is built from the realized launches, which are themselves
        the design (\cref{rem:selfshare}).
  \item \emph{Retention.} Quality and depth filters, modelled by per-float, per-age retention rates.
  \item \emph{Dependence.} Floats from one cruise, or sharing flow errors, are not independent across
        folds, so $q_{k|-k}\neq\sum_cn_{k,c}q^{(1)}_c$ and the bias gains
        $R^{\mathrm{dep}}_k=\int e_k(q_{k|-k}-\sum_cn_{k,c}q^{(1)}_c)/\hat q_k\dd\Prob_y$ (HT form).
        Folds by cruise reduce it \citep{chiang2022}.
  \item \emph{Measurement.} Mean-zero error independent of the design adds variance only.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{remark}[Self-share]\label{rem:selfshare}
If $\mathcal D_c$ uses all real launches, jittered copies of a float's own launch follow its track for a
while, inflating $\hat q_k$ at its early profiles and down-weighting them. Remedy: build fold $k$'s
$\hat q^{(1)}_c$ from other folds' launches only. A uniform draw from the full pool, conditioned on
falling in the other folds' pool, is uniform on that pool, so the existing replicates serve, filtered by
pool index. \Cref{ass:cover} near launch sites then needs each fold-$k$ launch to lie within the jitter
support of other folds' launches.
\end{remark}

\paragraph{Sensitivity band.} $\lambda_k$ is not identified. Following \citet{tan2006} and
\citet{zhao2019}, suppose $\lambda_k\in[\Gamma^{-1},\Gamma]$ up to a common scale on $\X_y$; this presumes real floats
reach all of $A^*$, since reweighting observed profiles cannot represent water with $\lambda_k=0$. The Hájek
correction is scale-invariant, so its range over the band is the min and max over $h_i\in[\Gamma^{-1},\Gamma]$
of $\sum_{I_k}h_iv_ie_k(x_i)/\sum_{I_k}h_iv_i$, a linear-fractional programme solved by a threshold in
the sorted residuals ($O(n\log n)$). The band is deterministic. $\Gamma$ is
calibrated by the flow-error experiment (\cref{sec:validation}).

\paragraph{Diagnostics (heuristic).} If deployments are exogenous, newly launched floats sample without
preferential selection, so the residual contrast between older and new floats indicates the alignment of
$\lambda-1$ with $e$, i.e.\ the sign and rough size of $\beta^{\mathrm{prod}}$. Real profile density
against $\hat q$ flags parts of $A^*$ that real floats do not reach. The North Atlantic detection analysis found it near zero except in
the Gulf Stream once sea-level anomaly enters the mean field.

\section{Open choices}\label{sec:open}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \emph{Domain $A$.} The existing replicates cover $11$--$66^\circ$N, $87$--$12^\circ$W, 2010--2019,
        so an interior such as $15$--$60^\circ$N, $80$--$20^\circ$W needs no new advection; the full basin
        needs new velocity and a new ensemble.
  \item \emph{Covariance parameters.} External or per-fold refits (\cref{rem:cov-params}).
  \item \emph{Folds.} $K$; by float (chain) or by cruise (real data).
  \item \emph{Reachability threshold $q_A$.} Higher shrinks $A^*$ (a smaller estimand) but bounds the
        weights more tightly; reported with the area excluded.
  \item \emph{Correction window.} The correction can pool over a larger $W\supseteq\X_y$ if the map's bias
        is smooth over $W$, trading variance for bias; unnecessary for basin-year targets.
  \item \emph{Stratification.} Hájek normalization per subregion absorbs regional scale errors in $\hat q$,
        at the cost of a ratio remainder per stratum.
  \item \emph{Bandwidth $(\sigma_s,\sigma_t)$ and $R$.} Fixed a priori so far; the chain can report bias
        against both.
\end{enumerate}
